<h2>TL;DR</h2>
<p>
If you want to move mind uploading forward seriously, the fastest route is to define in advance both the condition under which you can say “this worked” and the condition under which you must say “this failed.” Mind-Upload is building the verification infrastructure needed to make those checks comparable.

<section class="section" id="what-you-can-say">
<h2 class="section-title">First: what can and cannot be said now?</h2>
<div class="key-points">
<h4>Rule</h4>
<ul>
<li><strong>You can say:</strong> “Under these conditions, this task improved on this metric.”</li>
<li><strong>You cannot say:</strong> strong claims such as “consciousness was transferred” or “the person survived,” unless the definition and verification conditions are already fixed.</li>
<li><strong>Mind-Upload's role:</strong> decompose strong claims into smaller claims that can be falsified, and accumulate those instead.</li>
</ul>
</div>
</section>

<section class="section" id="definition">
<h2 class="section-title">What is WBE? An operational definition</h2>
<p>
WBE, or Whole Brain Emulation, is a broad label for research on running brain-relevant function on another substrate. But the required technology changes depending on what exactly counts as “reproduced.” To prevent the discussion from collapsing into slogans, Mind-Upload separates claim strength with a <strong>claim ladder</strong> (see P0/P1 in the Roadmap).
</p>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Key point</strong>
<p>
“The inputs and outputs look similar” is not enough for the kind of generation and internal causation WBE would require. That is why benchmark design is central.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>When you get stuck on “Isn't the connectome enough?”</strong>
<p>
Wiring diagrams matter, but they do not by themselves satisfy even the lower bound of WBE requirements. If you want a literature-based explanation of what is still missing when synaptic efficacy, activity-dependent transcription / chromatin state, perisynaptic ECM / perineuronal nets, thermal-state, ionic milieu / chloride homeostasis, bioenergetic / mitochondrial state, delay and myelination, neuromodulation, glia, cell-type labels, and <strong>intrinsic excitability / homeostatic set points</strong> are not captured, see <a href="wiki/connectome-is-not-enough.html">Wiki: Why wiring diagrams alone are not enough</a> and <a href="wiki/homeostatic-plasticity-and-maintenance-state.html">Wiki: Homeostatic plasticity and maintenance state</a>.
</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="section" id="state-completeness">
<h2 class="section-title">Hidden state that cannot be ignored at the entry point</h2>
<p>
It is dangerous to read WBE as if the wiring diagram and cell type were enough and everything else were just fine calibration. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-2907-3" target="_blank">Gouwens et al. (2021)</a> showed that morpho-electric variation still remains inside a single transcriptomic type. Furthermore, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adg9982" target="_blank">Santoni et al. (2024)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-025-02195-z" target="_blank">Coda et al. (2025)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09334-w" target="_blank">Terceros et al. (2026)</a> show that activity-dependent chromatin and transcriptional state can change which neurons are eligible for memory allocation and which late programs stabilize memory. In addition, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/385533a0" target="_blank">Frey &amp; Morris (1997)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms2250" target="_blank">Shires et al. (2012)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2010.12.008" target="_blank">Govindarajan et al. (2011)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2006.08.015" target="_blank">Fonseca et al. (2006)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1080/15548627.2020.1775393" target="_blank">Pandey et al. (2021)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-08459-0" target="_blank">Thomas et al. (2025)</a> show that late stabilization also depends on where synaptic tags are set, which branches capture plasticity-related proteins, how local translation/degradation/autophagy remain balanced, and which spine-state persists long enough to serve as the tag. In addition, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2016.01.046" target="_blank">Hengen et al. (2016)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2021.04.004" target="_blank">Torrado Pacheco et al. (2021)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-47838-5" target="_blank">Xu et al. (2024)</a> show that sleep/wake cycles affect firing-rate homeostasis and recovery of the network's operating regime. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1252304" target="_blank">Gibson et al. (2014)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1254960" target="_blank">McKenzie et al. (2014)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-023-01558-3" target="_blank">Looser et al. (2024)</a> show that myelin and oligodendrocyte support matter for timing and axonal health. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7793.1998.249bu.x" target="_blank">Hardingham &amp; Larkman (1998)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7793.2000.00059.x" target="_blank">Volgushev et al. (2000)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.8446900" target="_blank">Moser et al. (1993)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/nature07448" target="_blank">Long &amp; Fee (2008)</a> further show that local thermal-state can change synaptic reliability, spike generation, field-potential amplitude, and sequence timing without rewiring. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1072699" target="_blank">Pizzorusso et al. (2002)</a> showed that digesting chondroitin-sulfate proteoglycans can reopen adult ocular-dominance plasticity, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2338" target="_blank">Frischknecht et al. (2009)</a> showed that brain extracellular matrix constrains AMPA-receptor lateral mobility and short-term plasticity, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1174146" target="_blank">Gogolla et al. (2009)</a> showed that perineuronal nets protect fear memories from erasure, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.matbio.2024.11.001" target="_blank">Jabłońska et al. (2024)</a> showed that ECM integrity regulates hippocampal GABAergic plasticity. In addition, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1245423" target="_blank">Glykys et al. (2014)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-017-01749-0" target="_blank">Heubl et al. (2017)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aad4821" target="_blank">Ding et al. (2016)</a> show that local chloride set point and interstitial ion composition can change inhibitory polarity and brain-state regime even without rewiring. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2013.12.042" target="_blank">Rangaraju et al. (2014)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2018.12.013" target="_blank">Rangaraju et al. (2019)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2018.09.025" target="_blank">Divakaruni et al. (2018)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-44233-8" target="_blank">Bapat et al. (2024)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-08963-3" target="_blank">Hu et al. (2025)</a> further show that local ATP supply, mitochondrial positioning, fission/fusion, and synaptic ATP-synthase organization can still change repeated-burst reliability and dendritic plasticity even on the same graph. Finally, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2011.02.018" target="_blank">Suzuki et al. (2011)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07311-5" target="_blank">Cahill et al. (2024)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2211572119" target="_blank">Lee et al. (2022)</a> show that glial/metabolic support and active maintenance matter for memory consolidation and slow state.
</p>

<div class="note-box">
<strong>Do not confuse hidden state with the measurement stack</strong>
<p>
The hidden states listed here are a list of important variables, not a list of variables currently observed. For what EEG, fMRI, spatial transcriptomics, Patch-seq, volume EM, and same-brain connectomics directly observe, and where each one reaches its claim ceiling, see <a href="wiki/measurement-stack-and-claim-ceiling.html">Wiki: Measurement-stack observability and claim ceilings</a>. The purpose is to stop the word “multimodal” from being read as if it already meant state-complete. In the same way, healthy-human <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awab466" target="_blank">MRS thermometry (Rzechorzek et al., 2022)</a> is an important macro thermal route, but it still does not directly observe cell-specific temperature microgradients. Human <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/nbm.3384" target="_blank">31P-MRS (Ren et al., 2015)</a> and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf072" target="_blank">dynamic DMRSI (Li et al., 2025)</a> are important macro energetic routes, but they still do not directly observe branch-local ATP reserve or mitochondrial positioning.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>How this hidden-state list becomes an operational rule</strong>
<p>
The March 2026 update added the <a href="verification.html#observability-budget">Observability Budget</a> to Verification in order to convert hidden-state criticism into an operational rule. From L1 upward, results are expected to report the measurement stack, directly observed quantities, remaining latent state, claim ceiling, and abstention conditions together.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>What this means in the introduction</strong>
<p>
The direct takeaway is that <strong>connectome-complete means progress on the structural scaffold</strong>, not that one may already declare <strong>emulation-complete</strong>. That is an inference from the literature above, which leaves maintenance-state variables as a separate problem. The detailed justification is concentrated in <a href="wiki/connectome-is-not-enough.html">Wiki: Why wiring diagrams alone are not enough</a> and <a href="wiki/homeostatic-plasticity-and-maintenance-state.html">Wiki: Homeostatic plasticity and maintenance state</a>.
</p>
</div>

<section class="section" id="decode-vs-emulate">
<h2 class="section-title">Decode (translation) and emulate (generation) are different</h2>
<p>
Decoding stays at the level of translating observed neural activity into a sentence, label, or other output. WBE would require a system whose internal state evolves autonomously over time, responds to intervention, and can generate future output from that internal dynamics. That gap is why <strong>counterfactuals</strong> and <strong>intervention prediction</strong> matter.
</p>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>The common substitution error</strong>
<p>
If a result is summarized only as “brain to text worked,” what may actually exist is a translator optimized to produce plausible text. To support a claim closer to WBE, the system must be checked under branching conditions: if the condition changes, does the internal behavior still match in the relevant way?
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>When you get stuck at the entrance to causal verification</strong>
<p>
Held-out accuracy, intervention, counterfactual, and perturbation-based verification have different strengths. For a plain-language route into that distinction, see <a href="wiki/counterfactual-and-perturbation-verification.html">Wiki: Counterfactual, intervention, and perturbation verification</a>.
</p>
</div>

<section class="section" id="philosophical-position">
<h2 class="section-title">Philosophical presuppositions: what is assumed, and what is not</h2>
<p>
The plausibility of WBE depends strongly on what philosophical stance one takes toward consciousness. Mind-Upload does not assume any single stance is correct. Instead, it advances within the range that can be tested.
</p>

<thead>

<div class="note-box">
<strong>Important constraint</strong>
<p>
Whatever the deeper metaphysical truth may be, Mind-Upload uses measurable functional indicators as the common language. The philosophical consequence is postponed until enough empirical structure exists to constrain it.
</p>

<section class="section" id="alternative-approaches">
<h2 class="section-title">Technology-route comparison: what evidence each route currently provides</h2>
<p>
There are multiple routes toward something WBE-like. What should be fixed first at the entry page is not “which philosophy should we choose?” but rather <strong>what each route has actually demonstrated, and how far that evidence reaches</strong>. The comparison below stays at the level of measurement density, intervention capacity, closed-loop demonstration, and missing state variables.
</p>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>How to read this section</strong>
<p>
Non-invasive decoding, invasive closed loop, connectomics, and large-scale simulation are not simply competing camps. They are <strong>complementary routes that close different gaps</strong>. The question here is not which route “wins,” but what level of claim each route can currently support.
</p>
</div>
<table class="data-table">
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Route</th>
<th>Evidence already published</th>
<th>What can be said safely</th>
<th>What is still missing</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td><strong>Non-invasive decoding</strong></td>
<td>Tang et al. (2023) showed semantic reconstruction from fMRI and reported the importance of subject cooperation in both training and use.</td>
<td>A strong <strong>L1</strong> achievement: limited-condition semantic readout and task-dependent state estimation.</td>
<td>Internal causal structure, intervention response, sufficient temporal resolution, and generative matching under untrained conditions.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Invasive / hybrid neuroprosthesis</strong></td>
<td>Willett et al. (2023) showed a 62 words-per-minute speech BCI with a 125,000-word vocabulary; Flesher et al. (2021) improved robotic grasp time with bidirectional BCI; Berger et al. (2011) reported closed-loop memory-task recovery.</td>
<td>There is evidence approaching <strong>L2-L3</strong> for local circuits: not just decoding, but also causal intervention and task-specific closed-loop improvement.</td>
<td>Multi-region long-term stability, whole-brain coverage, drift management, and neuron-by-neuron replacement benchmarks.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Structural connectomics / local calibration / connectome-constrained modeling</strong></td>
<td>Dorkenwald et al. (2024) provided an adult drosophila whole-brain wiring diagram, MICrONS Consortium et al. (2025) linked function and connectomics within mouse visual cortex, and Lappalainen et al. (2024) showed rich activity prediction in a connectome-constrained fly visual model.</td>
<td>Extremely important as a structural atlas, same-brain local scaffold, and conditional hypothesis engine for local circuit work.</td>
<td>Unique internal-state recovery, omitted mechanisms such as electrical synapses / nonlinear chemical synapses / neuromodulation, whole-brain generalization, and maintenance-state completeness are still missing.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Large-scale biophysical simulation</strong></td>
<td>Billeh et al. (2020) built a data-driven multiscale model of awake mouse V1, while Prinz et al. (2004) showed that similar activity can arise from very different parameter sets.</td>
<td>Useful for mechanistic hypothesis testing, local digital twins, and sensitivity analysis of missing measurements.</td>
<td>Parameter identifiability, whole-brain fidelity, OOD generalization, and perturbation matching remain open.</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>What this comparison really shows</strong>
<p>
The strongest support in the current primary literature is for <strong>better decoding</strong>, <strong>closed-loop improvement in local subsystems</strong>, <strong>structure-function mapping</strong>, and <strong>local digital twins</strong>. The key question is therefore not route preference, but <strong>which evidence profile supports which rung of the claim ladder</strong>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Do not collapse connectome progress into one rung</strong>
<p>
This site now separates at least four kinds of connectome progress: <strong>wiring atlas</strong>, <strong>same-brain local scaffold</strong>, <strong>connectome-constrained conditional predictor</strong>, and <strong>identifiability audit</strong>. Dorkenwald et al. (2024), MICrONS Consortium et al. (2025), and Lappalainen et al. (2024) strengthen the first three, while Beiran &amp; Litwin-Kumar (2025) show why connectome alone still does not uniquely fix dynamics. Therefore, “connectome progress” is not one claim level on this site.
</p>
</div>
</section>

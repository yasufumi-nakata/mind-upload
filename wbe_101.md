<p>
If you want to move mind uploading forward seriously, the fastest route is to define in advance both the condition under which you can say “this worked” and the condition under which you must say “this failed.” Mind-Upload is building the verification infrastructure needed to make those checks comparable.

<section class="section" id="what-you-can-say">
<h2 class="section-title">First: what can and cannot be said now?</h2>
<div class="key-points">
<h4>Rule</h4>
<ul>
<li><strong>You can say:</strong> “Under these conditions, this task improved on this metric.”</li>
<li><strong>You cannot say:</strong> strong claims such as “consciousness was transferred” or “the person survived,” unless the definition and verification conditions are already fixed.</li>
<li><strong>Mind-Upload's role:</strong> decompose strong claims into smaller claims that can be falsified, and accumulate those instead.</li>
</ul>
</div>
</section>

<section class="section" id="definition">
<h2 class="section-title">What is WBE? An operational definition</h2>
<p>
WBE, or Whole Brain Emulation, is a broad label for research on running brain-relevant function on another substrate. But the required technology changes depending on what exactly counts as “reproduced.” To prevent the discussion from collapsing into slogans, Mind-Upload separates claim strength with a <strong>claim ladder</strong> (see P0/P1 in the Roadmap).
</p>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Key point</strong>
<p>
“The inputs and outputs look similar” is not enough for the kind of generation and internal causation WBE would require. That is why benchmark design is central.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>When you get stuck on “Isn't the connectome enough?”</strong>
<p>
Wiring diagrams matter, but they do not by themselves satisfy even the lower bound of WBE requirements. If you want a literature-based explanation of what is still missing when synaptic efficacy, activity-dependent transcription / chromatin state, post-transcriptional RNA-state, cargo-transport / cytoskeletal trafficking state, perisynaptic ECM / perineuronal nets, thermal-state, ionic milieu / chloride homeostasis, bioenergetic / mitochondrial state, delay and myelination, neuromodulation, glia, cell-type labels, and <strong>intrinsic excitability / homeostatic set points</strong> are not captured, see <a href="wiki/connectome-is-not-enough.html">Wiki: Why wiring diagrams alone are not enough</a> and <a href="wiki/homeostatic-plasticity-and-maintenance-state.html">Wiki: Homeostatic plasticity and maintenance state</a>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>When a thermodynamic paper sounds like the whole answer</strong>
<p>
At the WBE entry point, it is too early to treat one thermodynamic label as one solved layer. Mind-Upload separates <strong>Landauer lower bounds</strong>, <strong>biological energy budgets</strong>, <strong>irreversibility of coarse-grained neural time series</strong>, and <strong>model-based entropy-flow estimates</strong>. These can strengthen hypothesis formation, but on this site they remain <strong>auxiliary evidence</strong> unless the paper also discloses its <strong>signal route</strong>, <strong>estimator family</strong>, <strong>null / surrogate control</strong>, <strong>quantity type</strong>, and <strong>cost isolation</strong>. The shortest follow-up is <a href="faq.html#q1f">FAQ: how to read thermodynamic claims</a> plus <a href="wiki/thermodynamic-grounding-basics.html">Wiki: thermodynamic grounding basics</a>.
</p>
</div>

<section class="section" id="state-completeness">
<h2 class="section-title">Hidden state that cannot be ignored at the entry point</h2>
<p>
It is dangerous to read WBE as if the wiring diagram and cell type were enough and everything else were just fine calibration. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-2907-3" target="_blank">Gouwens et al. (2021)</a> showed that morpho-electric variation still remains inside a single transcriptomic type. Intrinsic excitability is also already split into different hidden-state families rather than one generic leftover. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2014.07.017" target="_blank">Yiu et al. (2014)</a> and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-66975-3" target="_blank">Hadzibegovic et al. (2025)</a> constrain <strong>relative-excitability / allocation bias</strong>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/nature09160" target="_blank">Grubb &amp; Burrone (2010)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/nature09087" target="_blank">Kuba et al. (2010)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-20232-x" target="_blank">Jamann et al. (2021)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adf3885" target="_blank">Fréal et al. (2023)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-02152-5" target="_blank">Benoit et al. (2025)</a> constrain <strong>AIS / Na<sup>+</sup>-channel-state plasticity</strong>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2014.04.002" target="_blank">O'Leary et al. (2014)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2016.01.046" target="_blank">Hengen et al. (2016)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2021.04.004" target="_blank">Torrado Pacheco et al. (2021)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-47838-5" target="_blank">Xu et al. (2024)</a> constrain <strong>firing-rate set point / recovery control</strong>. These routes do not share the same physiological locus or time axis, so the word <strong>excitability</strong> is already too coarse unless the paper says which route it actually constrains. Furthermore, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adg9982" target="_blank">Santoni et al. (2024)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/nature07925" target="_blank">Guan et al. (2009)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-14498-4" target="_blank">Gulmez Karaca et al. (2020)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2014.10.032" target="_blank">Bharadwaj et al. (2014)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-025-02368-y" target="_blank">Coda et al. (2025)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09774-6" target="_blank">Terceros et al. (2026)</a> show that activity-dependent chromatin and transcriptional state can change which neurons are eligible for memory allocation and which late programs stabilize memory, but they do not constrain one single molecular object. On this site, <strong>chromatin accessibility</strong>, <strong>histone-acetylation / histone-methylation control</strong>, <strong>DNA-methylation-dependent stabilization</strong>, <strong>higher-order chromatin looping</strong>, and <strong>locus-specific epigenetic editing</strong> are treated as different object families with different persistence claims and different human ceilings. But gene-level abundance is still not the whole RNA-state: <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4069" target="_blank">Wang et al. (2015)</a> showed that a neuron-specific LSD1 splice isoform regulates memory formation, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2019.03.032" target="_blank">Dai et al. (2019)</a> showed that presynaptic neurexin alternative splicing changes postsynaptic receptor balance and contextual memory, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0666-1" target="_blank">Shi et al. (2018)</a> and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.202514926" target="_blank">Li et al. (2025)</a> showed that m6A-reader routes can alter hippocampus-dependent learning and memory, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/scisignal.adr1442" target="_blank">Peterson et al. (2025)</a> showed that ADAR2-mediated GluA2 RNA editing contributes to homeostatic synaptic plasticity. In addition, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/385533a0" target="_blank">Frey &amp; Morris (1997)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms2250" target="_blank">Shires et al. (2012)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2010.12.008" target="_blank">Govindarajan et al. (2011)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2006.08.015" target="_blank">Fonseca et al. (2006)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1080/15548627.2020.1775393" target="_blank">Pandey et al. (2021)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-08459-0" target="_blank">Thomas et al. (2025)</a> show that late stabilization also depends on where synaptic tags are set, which branches capture plasticity-related proteins, how local translation/degradation/autophagy remain balanced, and which spine-state persists long enough to serve as the tag. Separately, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2006.09.040" target="_blank">Park et al. (2006)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0902304106" target="_blank">Maas et al. <div class="note-box">
<strong>Presynaptic release machinery is not the same as synaptic-density or synapse count</strong>
<p>
At the entry point, it is now also too coarse to treat <strong>synapse count</strong>, <strong>regional SV2A density</strong>, and <strong>current presynaptic release machinery</strong> as one bundle. The site now keeps <strong>release-site number</strong>, <strong>docked-vesicle architecture</strong>, <strong>active-zone nanostructure / priming-site assembly</strong>, and <strong>current release competence</strong> separate from regional synaptic-density proxy rows. The longer literature-backed critique is in <a href="wiki/connectome-is-not-enough.html">Wiki: Why wiring diagrams alone are not enough</a>, and the audit-side stop rule is in <a href="verification.html#latent-state-error-budget">Verification: latent-state error budget</a>.
</p>
</div>
<p>
If by “connectome” one means a human diffusion-MRI tractography connectome, the ceiling is even lower. Validation papers show useful macro pathway information, but not an edge-complete or synapse-resolved graph: <a href="https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1405672111" target="_blank">Thomas et al. (2014)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1418198112" target="_blank">Reveley et al. (2015)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0493-16.2016" target="_blank">Donahue et al. (2016)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s00429-020-02129-z" target="_blank">Schilling et al. (2020)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2021.118300" target="_blank">Grisot et al. (2021)</a>. More recent route-audit work also shows that the derived graph is not a stable object independent of the pipeline or acquisition protocol: <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2023.120376" target="_blank">Sarwar et al. (2023)</a> found that filtering gains become modest in complex human-like architectures, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2024.120904" target="_blank">He et al. (2024)</a> showed that filtering can shift laterality indices for more than 10% of connections, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mri.2025.110424" target="_blank">McMaster et al. (2025)</a> showed that voxel resolution changes the resulting connectome, <a href="https://doi.org/10.3389/fnimg.2026.1670604" target="_blank">Bramati et al. (2026)</a> showed on a single <strong>3 T</strong> scanner with uniform processing that common diffusion-sampling schemes still shift tractography outputs, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2025.103580" target="_blank">Manzano-Patrón et al. (2025)</a> made tractography uncertainty explicit rather than silent, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2025.103498" target="_blank">Zhu et al. (2025)</a> improved reconstruction by adding microscopy to MRI. Together, these papers support reading tractography-derived human connectomes as <strong>algorithm- and acquisition-conditioned macro pathway priors</strong>, <strong>targeted bundle hypotheses</strong>, or at most <strong>calibrated bundle comparisons</strong>, not as human connectome completion.

<p>
The practical reading rule is to ask four questions in order: <strong>which cell or compartment is directly observed</strong>, <strong>whether the route is causal or proxy</strong>, <strong>whether it is local or macro</strong>, and <strong>what model burden remains</strong>. Until those answers line up across rows, the bundle stays below <strong>same-subject maintenance-controller</strong> language.
</p>
<h3 class="section-title" id="human-observability-ladder">What human evidence currently looks like</h3>
<p>
One weakness of the earlier front door was that it listed hidden states without separating the layers of human evidence that now exist. The primary literature no longer supports either extreme reading: neither <strong>"humans show almost nothing"</strong> nor <strong>"humans are close to state-complete measurement."</strong> Instead, different measurement classes push different layers upward, and they reduce different error terms. Another weakness was that <strong>proxy class</strong> and <strong>operational maturity</strong> could still be silently collapsed into one impression. This table now keeps those two readings separate.
The first row is included because it is a human-side advance, but it is <strong>not</strong> a living-human in vivo route and must be read through the destructive-structure audit rather than through proxy-ladder language alone.

(2022)</a> measured <strong>choroid-plexus water cycling</strong>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1177/0271678X251369218" target="_blank">Wu et al. (2026)</a> measured apparent <strong>BCSFB exchange</strong> with scan-rescan repeatability, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1177/0271678X261429042" target="_blank">Petitclerc et al. (2026)</a> simultaneously estimated <strong>K</strong><sub>bl&rarr;GM</sub> and <strong>K</strong><sub>bl&rarr;CSF</sub> in one ASL acquisition. On this site, neurovascular claims now require a route card that fixes <strong>claim family</strong>, <strong>biological locus</strong>, <strong>direct observable</strong>, <strong>human quantity type / transport regime</strong>, <strong>crossed boundary / carrier class</strong>, <strong>interface sensitivity / dominant transport interpretation</strong>, <strong>validation / repeatability ceiling</strong>, and <strong>abstention boundary</strong>. The longer rule is in <a href="wiki/homeostatic-plasticity-and-maintenance-state.html#neurovascular-bbb-route-card">Wiki: neurovascular / BBB route card</a>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Do not fold local human ultrastructure into the in vivo proxy ladder</strong>
<p>
The safe reading here is stricter than simply saying that human evidence is getting richer. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crmeth.2023.100520" target="_blank">Lu et al. (2023)</a> showed that preservation route changes extracellular-space retention and native geometry, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adk4858" target="_blank">Shapson-Coe et al. (2024)</a> remained a rapidly preserved local surgical fragment, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08790-w" target="_blank">MICrONS Consortium et al. (2025)</a> remained a sequential same-brain pipeline rather than same-time capture, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07558-y" target="_blank">Dorkenwald et al. (2024)</a> still required large proofreading effort at petascale. Therefore, this site treats local human ultrastructure as a <strong>destructive-route class</strong> and the living-human rows below as a separate <strong>in vivo proxy ladder</strong>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Clearance / immune support is not passive cleanup</strong>
<p>
At the entry point, it is also too coarse to read <strong>clearance evidence</strong> as one class. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14432" target="_blank">Louveau et al. (2015)</a> and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1419-5" target="_blank">Ahn et al. (2019)</a> established meningeal-lymphatic drainage routes, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2025.02.022" target="_blank">Kim et al. (2025)</a> showed that the meningeal-lymphatics-microglia axis can regulate synaptic physiology, and the human lane is no longer transport-only. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-40937-z" target="_blank">Biechele et al. (2023)</a> showed why TSPO is not a universal human activation-state meter, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awaf078" target="_blank">Wijesinghe et al. (2025)</a> validated TSPO PET as a microglial biomarker in PSP, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1186/s13550-022-00929-4" target="_blank">Horti et al. (2022)</a> plus <a href="https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.124.268699" target="_blank">Ogata et al. (2025)</a> established first-in-human CSF1R PET routes, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.124.268525" target="_blank">Yan et al. (2025)</a> quantified COX-2 in healthy human brain. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aax5440" target="_blank">Fultz et al. (2019)</a> then measured macroscopic CSF oscillations during human NREM sleep, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2025.121142" target="_blank">Kim, Huang, &amp; Liu (2025)</a> measured parenchyma-CSF water exchange with MT spin labeling, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awaa443" target="_blank">Eide &amp; Ringstad (2021)</a> showed that sleep deprivation impairs molecular clearance in humans, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-37685-5" target="_blank">Eide et al. (2023)</a> linked intrathecal gadobutrol retention and population-pharmacokinetic CSF-to-blood clearance variables to different plasma biomarker patterns, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-02073-3" target="_blank">Hirschler et al. (2025)</a> measured region-specific CSF-mobility drivers, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-66548-4" target="_blank">Lim et al. (2025)</a> reported respiration-conditioned CSF net flow in awake humans while explicitly cautioning that plane-specific 2D PC-MRI net flow does not by itself represent true whole-brain bulk circulation, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-58726-1" target="_blank">Yoo et al. (2025)</a> reported exercise-conditioned contrast influx and parasagittal meningeal-lymphatic flow changes, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-68374-8" target="_blank">Dagum et al. (2026)</a> linked sleep-active physiology to overnight Aβ / tau clearance to plasma in healthy older adults. On this site, <strong>drainage anatomy</strong>, <strong>microglia-related synaptic control</strong>, <strong>TSPO disease-context / validation-bounded PET</strong>, <strong>CSF1R route-setting PET</strong>, <strong>COX-2 enzyme-defined PET</strong>, <strong>macroscopic CSF oscillation</strong>, <strong>parenchyma-CSF water exchange</strong>, <strong>respiration-conditioned net-flow MRI</strong>, <strong>exercise-conditioned contrast influx</strong>, <strong>intrathecal tracer retention / CSF-to-blood clearance</strong>, <strong>human CSF-mobility MRI</strong>, and <strong>model-based human biomarker efflux</strong> are treated as different inferential objects. Therefore, a human CSF-mobility map is not read as direct flux ground truth, a respiration-conditioned net-flow estimate is not read as route-free whole-brain circulation truth, an intervention-conditioned contrast route is not read as a route-free baseline, an intrathecal-tracer route is not read as natural-sleep whole-brain clearance truth, a plasma Aβ / tau clearance model is not read as local immune-controller identification, and none of the PET rows is read as one generic human microglia-state scalar. The longer operating rule is in <a href="wiki/homeostatic-plasticity-and-maintenance-state.html#clearance-route-card">Wiki: clearance / immune route card</a>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Human immune support is not only a clearance-transport lane</strong>
<p>
This site now keeps two human lanes apart. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aax5440" target="_blank">Fultz et al. (2019)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2025.121142" target="_blank">Kim, Huang, &amp; Liu (2025)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-02073-3" target="_blank">Hirschler et al. (2025)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-37685-5" target="_blank">Eide et al. (2023)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-68374-8" target="_blank">Dagum et al. (2026)</a> are <strong>transport-side</strong> human routes. By contrast, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awaf078" target="_blank">Wijesinghe et al. (2025)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1186/s13550-022-00929-4" target="_blank">Horti et al. (2022)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.124.268699" target="_blank">Ogata et al. (2025)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.124.268525" target="_blank">Yan et al. (2025)</a> are <strong>target-defined neuroimmune PET</strong> routes. Those rows do not share one direct observable, one target, one tracer family, one time window, or one safe claim ceiling. On this site, <strong>immune evidence</strong> is therefore not read as one generic microglia meter and is not folded back into clearance transport.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Spectroscopy-derived human rows now need a quantity-type split</strong>
<p>
The remaining weakness in this ladder was that <strong>spectroscopy-derived human routes</strong> could still sound like one continuous advance. The primary literature does not support that shortcut. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-66124-w" target="_blank">Lucchetti et al. (2025)</a> defined the human metabolic connectome as a within-subject similarity matrix built from <strong>pairwise correlations among five metabolites across gray-matter parcels</strong> in <strong>51 healthy participants</strong>, with replication in an independent sample of 13. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/nbm.3384" target="_blank">Ren et al. (2015)</a> used <sup>31</sup>P-MRS in <strong>12 subjects</strong> to estimate <strong>resting ATP synthesis, phosphorus-metabolite concentrations, and intra-/extracellular pH</strong>. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.26560" target="_blank">Ren et al. (2017)</a> then used <strong>band-inversion / magnetization-transfer modeling</strong> to estimate <strong>PCr&rarr;&gamma;-ATP and Pi&rarr;&gamma;-ATP exchange fluxes</strong> in the resting human brain. <a href="https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2024.1389111" target="_blank">Guo et al. (2024)</a> mapped <strong>whole-brain intracellular NAD content</strong> at <strong>7 T</strong>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1177/0271678X261415784" target="_blank">Kaiser et al. (2026)</a> detected <strong>task-evoked NAD<sup>+</sup> dynamics</strong> in a <strong>functionally localized occipital voxel</strong> from <strong>25 healthy volunteers</strong>. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf072" target="_blank">Li et al. (2025)</a> used <strong>dynamic DMRSI plus a kinetic model</strong> to map <strong>CMR<sub>Glc</sub>, CMR<sub>Lac</sub>, V<sub>TCA</sub>, and T<sub>max</sub></strong> in <strong>five healthy participants</strong> and reported repeat measurements only at the <strong>same operating point</strong>. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.70308" target="_blank">Karkouri et al. (2026)</a> then produced <strong>absolute HDO / Glc / Glx / Lac maps and rate maps</strong> at <strong>7 T</strong> in <strong>12 healthy volunteers</strong> plus <strong>five glioblastoma patients</strong>, with only <strong>two healthy post-glucose scans</strong> in the abstracted protocol. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/jmri.29532" target="_blank">Ahmadian et al. (2025)</a> further showed that <strong>deuterium dose</strong> materially changes downstream metabolite visibility, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1186/s41747-024-00426-4" target="_blank">Bøgh et al. (2024)</a> showed that <strong>3 T DMI repeatability depends on the named acquisition and time-point regime</strong>, with the best whole-brain repeatability at <strong>120 min</strong> in that protocol. Those are not one inferential object. On this site, the 1H-MRSI row is read as a <strong>parcel-level biochemical similarity scaffold</strong>, the <sup>31</sup>P rows as separate <strong>metabolite / pH</strong>, <strong>exchange-flux</strong>, <strong>NAD-content mapping</strong>, and <strong>localized functional NAD-dynamics</strong> proxy classes, and the deuterium rows as <strong>quantity-defined metabolite or kinetic-rate imaging under an explicit operating point</strong>, not as one merged spectroscopy ladder.
</p>
<p>
The measurement model also has to stay visible. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/brb3.1852" target="_blank">Bhogal et al. (2020)</a> showed that in vivo MRSI remains sensitive to <strong>low SNR</strong>, <strong>partial-volume effects</strong>, and <strong>extracranial lipid artifacts</strong>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2022.119574" target="_blank">Wright et al. (2022)</a> showed that using averaged instead of voxel-specific metabolite T<sub>1</sub> corrections can bias maps, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1148/radiol.232401" target="_blank">Baboli et al. (2024)</a> showed that absolute quantification changes when tissue-water / relaxation correction is individualized, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1109/TBME.2025.3572448" target="_blank">Guo et al. (2025)</a> showed that high-resolution 1H-MRSI metabolite-distribution mapping itself depends on acquisition / processing choices that manage <strong>spectral ghosting</strong>, <strong>aliasing</strong>, and <strong>low SNR</strong>. Therefore, on this site, a spectroscopy-derived human claim is not read without naming whether the object is <strong>static similarity</strong>, <strong>high-resolution metabolite distribution</strong>, <strong>energetic balance</strong>, <strong>deuterium absolute metabolite mapping / quantification</strong>, or <strong>kinetic rate imaging</strong>, together with the <strong>metabolite set</strong>, <strong>parceling or voxel unit</strong>, <strong>resolution plus PSF / partial-volume correction</strong>, <strong>water / lipid handling</strong>, <strong>spectral QC thresholds</strong>, and any <strong>blood-input or kinetic-model burden</strong>. For deuterium rows, the card now also asks for the <strong>dose</strong>, the <strong>time-point window</strong>, the <strong>field strength / coil route</strong>, and whether repeatability was shown only <strong>within the same setup</strong> or across protocols. The longer operational rules are in <a href="wiki/homeostatic-plasticity-and-maintenance-state.html#metabolic-connectome-route-card">Wiki: metabolic-connectome route card</a> and <a href="wiki/homeostatic-plasticity-and-maintenance-state.html#bioenergetic-route-card">Wiki: bioenergetic / mitochondrial route card</a>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>The tractography row now needs a route card</strong>
<p>
The weak point on this page was not that it called tractography a <strong>macro pathway prior</strong>, but that the phrase could still sound too stable, as if every tractography-derived connectome were the same inferential object once a modern pipeline had been applied. The newer route-audit literature argues against that shortcut at three different stages. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1418198112" target="_blank">Reveley et al. (2015)</a> showed that superficial white matter can hide long-range cortical connections from roughly half of the cortical surface, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.23936" target="_blank">Schilling et al. (2018)</a> showed that tractography endpoints are biased toward gyral crowns across deterministic and probabilistic algorithms, multiple diffusion models, and even very high-resolution data. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2023.120376" target="_blank">Sarwar et al. (2023)</a> then showed that filtering helps much less in complex human-like architectures than in simple bundles, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2024.120904" target="_blank">He et al. (2024)</a> showed that filtering can materially change structural laterality estimates, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1162/netn_a_00324" target="_blank">Gajwani et al. (2023)</a> showed across <strong>40 pipelines</strong> and <strong>44</strong> group-representative reconstructions that hub location is highly variable and that hub connectivity correlates with regional surface area in <strong>69%</strong> of assessed pipelines. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mri.2025.110424" target="_blank">McMaster et al. (2025)</a> then showed that voxel size changes the resulting connectome and recommended resampling to <strong>1 mm isotropic</strong> for robust comparisons, <a href="https://doi.org/10.3389/fnimg.2026.1670604" target="_blank">Bramati et al. (2026)</a> showed on the <strong>same 3 T scanner</strong> with uniform processing that the q-space sampling scheme itself can change tractography outputs, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2025.103580" target="_blank">Manzano-Patrón et al. (2025)</a> showed that uncertainty should be propagated rather than hidden, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2025.103498" target="_blank">Zhu et al. (2025)</a> improved reconstruction by fusing MRI with microscopy. Therefore, this site now asks readers to treat the tractography row as a <strong>route card</strong>: read acquisition / harmonization, cortical endpoint assignment, parcellation / weighting, uncertainty, calibration, and abstention before reading the graph. The longer operational rule is in <a href="wiki/connectome-is-not-enough.html#tractography-route-card">Wiki: tractography route card</a>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Human proxy class is not the same thing as operational maturity</strong>
<p>
The strongest correction needed here was not to delete these rows, but to stop readers from treating them as equally mature. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1750-23.2024" target="_blank">Johansen et al. (2024)</a> built an SV2A atlas from <strong>33 healthy participants</strong> calibrated against postmortem autoradiography, which is a real atlas resource but still a cohort-level synaptic-density proxy. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-66124-w" target="_blank">Lucchetti et al. (2025)</a> used <strong>51 healthy adolescents</strong> plus an <strong>independent replication sample of 13</strong> to derive a metabolic similarity matrix from five <sup>1</sup>H-MRSI metabolites, which is a biochemical organization scaffold rather than direct flux imaging. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/nbm.3384" target="_blank">Ren et al. (2015)</a> estimated resting ATP-synthesis-related, phosphorus-metabolite, and pH quantities in <strong>12 participants</strong>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.26560" target="_blank">Ren et al. (2017)</a> added a <strong>7 T MT exchange-flux route</strong> with an explicit <strong>5-pool model</strong>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2024.1389111" target="_blank">Guo et al. (2024)</a> mapped <strong>whole-brain intracellular NAD</strong> at <strong>2.3 cc nominal resolution</strong> over roughly <strong>51 min</strong>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1177/0271678X261415784" target="_blank">Kaiser et al. (2026)</a> added a <strong>task-evoked 31P fMRS NAD<sup>+</sup> route</strong> in <strong>25 healthy volunteers</strong>. Meanwhile, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf072" target="_blank">Li et al. (2025)</a> quantified glucose-transport and metabolic-rate maps in only <strong>five healthy participants</strong> using <strong>7 T</strong>, custom dual-frequency coils, and blood input functions, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.70308" target="_blank">Karkouri et al. (2026)</a> added absolute deuterium metabolite and rate maps with a separate calibration burden. Those spectroscopy rows are already enough to show that quantity type and acquisition burden diverge inside the same broad modality family. Meanwhile, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.29998" target="_blank">Baadsvik et al. (2024)</a> demonstrated myelin-bilayer mapping in <strong>two healthy volunteers</strong> on high-performance hardware. Likewise, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-02073-3" target="_blank">Hirschler et al. (2025)</a> introduced a specialized 7 T CSF-mobility route whose whole-brain rest maps were shown in <strong>20 healthy younger individuals</strong>, with driver analyses reported in <strong>11 of 24 total healthy participants</strong>. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-68374-8" target="_blank">Dagum et al. (2026)</a> combined a <strong>randomized crossover trial with 39 participants</strong>, an <strong>investigational wearable</strong>, and a <strong>compartmental model</strong> to infer sleep-linked clearance. Therefore, on this site, the ladder now has to be read along two axes at once: <strong>what variable class the route constrains</strong> and <strong>how specialized or deployment-limited that route still is</strong>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Human proxy class, route maturity, and calibrator role are three different questions</strong>
<p>
Even when a living-human route is real, that still does not say <strong>how deployable it is</strong> or <strong>which hidden-state family it safely calibrates</strong>. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1750-23.2024" target="_blank">Johansen et al. (2024)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-66124-w" target="_blank">Lucchetti et al. (2025)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/nbm.3384" target="_blank">Ren et al. (2015)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf072" target="_blank">Li et al. (2025)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.70308" target="_blank">Karkouri et al. (2026)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.29998" target="_blank">Baadsvik et al. (2024)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.121.263254" target="_blank">Villemagne et al. (2022)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.121.263255" target="_blank">Villemagne et al. (2022)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s12149-025-02083-y" target="_blank">Hiraoka et al. (2025)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s12149-025-02144-2" target="_blank">Mesfin et al. (2026)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s00259-025-07542-2" target="_blank">Matsuoka et al. (2026)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.118.208009" target="_blank">Tyacke et al. (2018)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-021-01429-y" target="_blank">Livingston et al. (2022)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-025-03355-9" target="_blank">Best et al. (2026)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-02073-3" target="_blank">Hirschler et al. (2025)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-68374-8" target="_blank">Dagum et al. (2026)</a> do not calibrate the same latent variable. On this site, entry-level readers should therefore ask three separate questions before promoting a human route: <strong>what class is it</strong>, <strong>how operationally mature is it</strong>, and <strong>what does it actually calibrate</strong>. The longer technical rule is in <a href="wiki/homeostatic-plasticity-and-maintenance-state.html#human-proxy-calibrator-matrix">Wiki: human proxy calibrator-role matrix</a>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>PET rows need a measurement model</strong>
<p>
Within this ladder, PET-based rows are not readable from the modality label alone. <a href="https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.120.249144" target="_blank">Naganawa et al. (2021)</a> showed that human SV2A PET quantification depends on the tracer, arterial-versus-reference quantification route, compartment model, and named scan window. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1177/0271678X20946198" target="_blank">Smart et al. (2021)</a> showed that [<sup>11</sup>C]UCB-J binding measures remain unchanged during brief visual activation even when tracer influx rises with blood flow, so momentary neural activity is not the same object as synaptic-density PET. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-022-01186-3" target="_blank">Hansen et al. (2022)</a> collated group-average receptor maps from more than 1,200 healthy participants, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-08492-z" target="_blank">Nakuci &amp; Bansal (2025)</a> then used those kinds of receptor / transporter densities as a modeling scaffold for spontaneous activity, so atlas priors remain normative rather than same-subject current-state readout. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-013-3103-z" target="_blank">Wong et al. (2013)</a> showed selected D<sub>2</sub>-receptor target engagement by an administered drug, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2025.1651016" target="_blank">Schlosser et al. (2025)</a> showed that even a higher ketamine dose can return an informative <strong>null occupancy</strong> result for SERT rather than a release estimate. By contrast, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/30498" target="_blank">Koepp et al. (1998)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41386-019-0567-5" target="_blank">Erritzoe et al. (2020)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.124.268317" target="_blank">Miederer et al. (2025)</a> used task- or challenge-linked binding changes as bounded release-sensitive proxies for selected dopamine or serotonin systems. Therefore, on this site, PET evidence is not read without its tracer, occupancy-versus-displacement design, quantification model, spatial scope, and time window.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Occupancy and displacement PET answer different questions</strong>
<p>
<a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-013-3103-z" target="_blank">Wong et al. (2013)</a> estimated whether an administered drug occupied a selected receptor target, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2025.1651016" target="_blank">Schlosser et al. (2025)</a> showed that the same occupancy framework can also return a route-specific <strong>null result</strong> rather than endogenous release. By contrast, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/30498" target="_blank">Koepp et al. (1998)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41386-019-0567-5" target="_blank">Erritzoe et al. (2020)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.124.268317" target="_blank">Miederer et al. (2025)</a> used task- or drug-challenge-linked binding changes as endogenous release proxies within bounded windows. Those are not the same inferential object. On this site, occupancy PET is read as <strong>target engagement</strong>, while displacement PET is read as a <strong>challenge-limited release proxy</strong>. Neither is promoted to direct whole-brain neuromodulatory state.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Do not compress neuromodulation into one line</strong>
<p>
Mixed arousal proxies, local transmitter sensors, receptor / transporter atlas priors, occupancy PET, and displacement / release-sensitive PET are not one measurement class. On this site, they reduce different error terms and support different claim ceilings. The short rule is simple: do not promote a receptor map, an occupancy result, or a challenge-limited PET effect to the claim that the current whole-brain neuromodulatory state was identified. For the detailed ladder, see <a href="wiki/measurement-stack-and-claim-ceiling.html#neuromodulatory-proxy-ladder">Wiki: neuromodulatory proxy ladder</a>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Neuromodulatory evidence now gets its own route card</strong>
<p>
At the entry point, it is too coarse to read <strong>neuromodulatory evidence</strong> as one class. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-57289-5" target="_blank">Carro-Dom&iacute;nguez et al. (2025)</a> is a <strong>mixed arousal proxy in human sleep</strong>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2024.114808" target="_blank">Neyhart et al. (2024)</a> is a <strong>local transmitter-sensor route with explicit axon-activity and clearance constraints</strong>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-022-01186-3" target="_blank">Hansen et al. (2022)</a> plus <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-08492-z" target="_blank">Nakuci &amp; Bansal (2025)</a> define a <strong>regional receptor / transporter atlas prior and modeling scaffold</strong>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-013-3103-z" target="_blank">Wong et al. (2013)</a> plus <a href="https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2025.1651016" target="_blank">Schlosser et al. (2025)</a> are <strong>occupancy PET target-engagement routes</strong>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/30498" target="_blank">Koepp et al. (1998)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41386-019-0567-5" target="_blank">Erritzoe et al. (2020)</a>, plus <a href="https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.124.268317" target="_blank">Miederer et al. (2025)</a> are <strong>challenge-limited displacement / release proxies</strong>. On this site, neuromodulatory claims now require a route card that fixes <strong>claim family</strong>, <strong>transmitter axis</strong>, <strong>direct observable</strong>, <strong>challenge or administered-drug route</strong>, <strong>time window / model burden</strong>, and <strong>abstention boundary</strong>. The longer operating rule is in <a href="wiki/homeostatic-plasticity-and-maintenance-state.html#neuromodulatory-route-card">Wiki: neuromodulatory route card</a>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Do not read multiple human proxy rows as automatic state completeness</strong>
<p>
A second correction follows from the same literature: even when several human proxy classes appear in the same argument, they still do not compose automatically into a state-complete readout. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adk4858" target="_blank">Shapson-Coe et al. (2024)</a> is a local structural scaffold, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1750-23.2024" target="_blank">Johansen et al. (2024)</a> is a cohort-level synaptic-density proxy, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-66124-w" target="_blank">Lucchetti et al. (2025)</a> is a parcel-level biochemical scaffold, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/nbm.3384" target="_blank">Ren et al. (2015)</a> is a 31P metabolite / pH balance route, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.26560" target="_blank">Ren et al. (2017)</a> is a 31P MT exchange-flux route, <a href="https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2024.1389111" target="_blank">Guo et al. (2024)</a> is a 31P NAD-content mapping route, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1177/0271678X261415784" target="_blank">Kaiser et al. (2026)</a> is a localized functional 31P NAD-dynamics route, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.70308" target="_blank">Karkouri et al. (2026)</a> is a specialized deuterium metabolite-mapping / absolute-quantification route, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf072" target="_blank">Li et al. (2025)</a> is a 7 T dynamic deuterium kinetic-rate route, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.29998" target="_blank">Baadsvik et al. (2024)</a> is a specialized myelin route, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-02073-3" target="_blank">Hirschler et al. (2025)</a> plus <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-68374-8" target="_blank">Dagum et al. (2026)</a> are support-state routes with explicit model burden. These rows differ in quantity type, spatial unit, time window, quantification model, and operating burden, and the cited papers do not show that they can already be acquired in the same person, same session, same perturbation regime, with externally validated fusion. Therefore, on this site, <strong>proxy-rich human evidence</strong> is read as stronger than a single proxy row but still weaker than <strong>same-subject, cross-stack, externally calibrated state identification</strong>. The longer technical rule is summarized in <a href="verification.html#human-proxy-composition-card">Verification: Human Proxy Composition Card</a>, <a href="wiki/measurement-stack-and-claim-ceiling.html#human-proxy-composition">Wiki: human-proxy composition rule</a>, and <a href="wiki/human-proxy-composition.html">Wiki: Human Proxy Composition and Route Maturity</a>. That ranking is an inference from the measurement properties summarized above.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box" id="human-proxy-composition-rule">
<strong>2026-03-21 rule: proxy bundles need an explicit composition audit</strong>
<p>
The correction that still remained after the March 2026 ladder update was operational, not conceptual. Readers could now see that <a href="https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1750-23.2024" target="_blank">Johansen et al. (2024)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-66124-w" target="_blank">Lucchetti et al. (2025)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/nbm.3384" target="_blank">Ren et al. (2015)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.26560" target="_blank">Ren et al. (2017)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2024.1389111" target="_blank">Guo et al. (2024)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1177/0271678X261415784" target="_blank">Kaiser et al. (2026)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf072" target="_blank">Li et al. (2025)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.70308" target="_blank">Karkouri et al. (2026)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.29998" target="_blank">Baadsvik et al. (2024)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-02073-3" target="_blank">Hirschler et al. (2025)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-68374-8" target="_blank">Dagum et al. (2026)</a> constrain different state families, but the page still did not say <strong>when several rows may legitimately be promoted together</strong>. The primary literature supports a stricter rule: one row is a cohort-level synaptic-density atlas, one is a five-metabolite similarity graph in 51 adolescents plus 13-site replication, one is a 31P metabolite / pH balance route in 12 resting participants, one is a 7 T MT exchange-flux route, one is a whole-brain 7 T NAD-content map, one is a task-evoked 31P fMRS NAD<sup>+</sup> route in 25 participants, one is a 7 T dynamic kinetic map in five participants, one is a 7 T deuterium metabolite-mapping / absolute-quantification route with its own calibration burden, one is a two-volunteer myelin proof-of-principle, one is a 20-person CSF-mobility rest-mapping route whose driver analysis was reported in 11 of 24 total healthy participants, and one is a model-heavy glymphatic clearance study in a randomized crossover trial of 39 participants. Those are not interchangeable pieces of one already-validated whole-brain state meter. The same multimodal literature also shows why agreement across rows is not enough by itself: <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-44363-z" target="_blank">Vafaii et al. (2024)</a> found both common and divergent structure across simultaneous Ca<sup>2+</sup> and BOLD, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-64414-x" target="_blank">Chen et al. (2025)</a> found tightly coupled global progression plus <strong>two distinct network patterns</strong> in simultaneous EEG-PET-MRI, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-01945-y" target="_blank">Bolt et al. (2025)</a> showed that a major global fMRI mode is substantially coupled to autonomic physiology, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-02132-9" target="_blank">Epp et al. (2025)</a> showed that significant task BOLD changes can coexist with opposite oxygen-metabolism changes across many cortical voxels. The robustness literature makes the stop line narrower still: <a href="https://doi.org/10.1177/0271678X17724947" target="_blank">Finnema et al. (2018)</a> showed route-specific SV2A PET test-retest constraints, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1186/s41747-024-00426-4" target="_blank">Bøgh et al. (2024)</a> showed route-local repeatability for <strong>3 T</strong> deuterium metabolic imaging at its own operating point, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/nbm.5256" target="_blank">Morgan et al. (2024)</a> showed that even BBB water-exchange estimates can change materially across <strong>DP-ASL</strong> and <strong>ME-ASL</strong> within one cohort, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0206583" target="_blank">Holiga et al. (2018)</a> showed that common fMRI measures vary from <strong>poor to excellent</strong> in repeatability, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2021.117864" target="_blank">Wirsich et al. (2021)</a> showed that some simultaneous EEG-fMRI relations do reproduce across centres, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awac335" target="_blank">Amiri et al. (2023)</a> showed that full EEG+fMRI availability itself can be restricted to a subset, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awaf412" target="_blank">Manasova et al. (2026)</a> showed higher inter-modality disagreement in some clinically important groups even while multimodal performance rose overall. The key operational correction is now explicit: <strong>the same named quantity is not yet the same validated row</strong>. A repeatable <strong>3 T</strong> DMI route does not automatically become interchangeable with specialized <strong>7 T</strong> deuterium kinetic or absolute-quantification routes, and a BBB water-exchange estimate is still route-dependent when different ASL families give materially different numbers. On this site, a bundle of human proxy rows is promoted only after a <a href="verification.html#human-proxy-composition-card">Human Proxy Composition Card</a> names the <strong>claimed latent variable</strong>, the <strong>stack that directly constrains it</strong>, the <strong>evidence relation across rows</strong>, the <strong>evidence role each row is actually allowed to play</strong>, the <strong>quantity type / model burden / acquisition burden</strong>, the <strong>cross-row nuisance audit</strong>, the <strong>external calibration route</strong>, the <strong>increment over the strongest single row</strong>, the <strong>residual latent families</strong>, the <strong>repeatability regime</strong>, the <strong>method-family non-equivalence</strong>, the <strong>cross-centre transfer window</strong>, and the <strong>row-overlap geometry / missingness policy</strong>. Operationally, those fields now compress to <strong>three promotion gates</strong>: <strong>robustness</strong>, <strong>common-driver / quantity-bridge separation</strong>, and <strong>increment over the strongest single row</strong>. Without that card, the bundle stays at <strong>proxy-rich but ceiling-limited human evidence</strong>. For the longer cross-stack critique, see <a href="wiki/human-proxy-composition.html">Wiki: Human Proxy Composition and Route Maturity</a>.
</p>
<p>
One more split is now required <strong>inside</strong> named proxy families. <a href="https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.120.249144" target="_blank">Naganawa et al. (2021)</a> constrain an <strong>SV2A quantification route</strong>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1750-23.2024" target="_blank">Johansen et al. (2024)</a> constrain a <strong>healthy atlas / baseline route</strong>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.14327" target="_blank">Snellman et al. (2024)</a> constrain a <strong>disease / risk-contrast route</strong>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1162/imag_a_00190" target="_blank">Shatalina et al. (2024)</a> constrain a <strong>task / cognition association route</strong>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1177/0271678X20946198" target="_blank">Smart et al. (2021)</a> constrain an <strong>activation-null timescale boundary</strong>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-022-01465-2" target="_blank">Holmes et al. (2022)</a> constrain an <strong>intervention-response null at 24 h</strong>. Therefore, even at the entry point, <code>SV2A PET</code> is no longer treated as one reusable bundle row. The composition audit now has to name the <strong>family-internal comparison family</strong> before any shared synaptic-density role is inferred.
</p>
<p>
The same papers also do not license one shared <strong>evidence role</strong>. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1750-23.2024" target="_blank">Johansen et al. (2024)</a> is a <strong>healthy atlas / cohort-prior route</strong>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.14327" target="_blank">Snellman et al. (2024)</a> is a <strong>cross-sectional risk-contrast route</strong>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1177/0271678X17724947" target="_blank">Finnema et al. (2018)</a> is a <strong>same-subject baseline / repeatability route</strong>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1177/0271678X20946198" target="_blank">Smart et al. (2021)</a> is a <strong>within-subject activation-change boundary</strong>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-022-01465-2" target="_blank">Holmes et al. (2022)</a> is a <strong>24 h intervention-response boundary</strong>. On this site, the composition audit therefore also has to name whether each row is being used as a <strong>normative atlas / cohort prior</strong>, a <strong>cross-sectional contrast</strong>, a <strong>same-subject baseline readout</strong>, a <strong>within-subject change witness</strong>, or a <strong>perturbation-response witness</strong>. One evidence role is not allowed to stand in for another.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Same session is not yet the same state axis</strong>
<p>
Even a same-subject or same-session bundle can still mix <strong>different temporal objects</strong>. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-66124-w" target="_blank">Lucchetti et al. (2025)</a> is a <strong>static parcel-similarity scaffold</strong>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2024.1389111" target="_blank">Guo et al. (2026)</a> is a <strong>task-evoked localized NAD-dynamics route</strong>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf072" target="_blank">Li et al. (2025)</a> is a <strong>minutes-long kinetic mapping route</strong>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-68374-8" target="_blank">Dagum et al. (2026)</a> is an <strong>overnight perturbation-and-efflux route</strong>. Those rows do not all answer the same question at the same time axis. On this site, a proxy bundle therefore has to disclose <strong>effective time window / state-axis compatibility</strong> and <strong>physiological / perturbation regime compatibility</strong> before same-subject wording is allowed to raise the claim ceiling. The longer operational rule is in <a href="wiki/human-proxy-composition.html">Wiki: Human Proxy Composition and Route Maturity</a> and <a href="verification.html#human-proxy-composition-card">Verification: Human Proxy Composition Card</a>.
</p>

Therefore, at the entry point, <code>human timing-support evidence</code> is not treated as one reusable row. A route has to be typed first as <strong>tract-scale transmission-speed estimation</strong>, <strong>MWF / calibrated T1w:T2w comparison</strong>, <strong>relaxometry / MT<sub>sat</sub> comparison</strong>, <strong>bilayer-sensitive mapping</strong>, <strong>qT1 remyelination-sensitive pathology</strong>, or a <strong>T1w/FLAIR tissue-health-sensitive ratio</strong> before it is allowed to support a bundle claim.

<strong>What this table fixes at the entry point</strong>
<p>
The point of the comparison is not to downplay progress. The point is to stop three specific overreads before they spread upward through the site: <strong>quantity-type collapse</strong> (<code>density = rate = similarity = mobility</code>), <strong>deployment-maturity collapse</strong> (<code>specialized proof-of-principle = routine same-subject observability</code>), and <strong>fusion collapse</strong> (<code>same-session agreement = one validated latent coordinate</code>). The current primary literature supports progress on each row separately, but it does not support skipping those three audits.

<strong>Same-subject is still not same-state when the bridge is sequential</strong>
<p>
One more shortcut still has to be blocked at the entry point. A result can be <strong>same-subject</strong> or <strong>same-brain</strong> and still fail to be one state sample if the bridge runs from live measurement to later fixation / ex vivo follow-up or from one day / regime to another. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-30199-6" target="_blank">Bosch et al. (2022)</a> and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08790-w" target="_blank">MICrONS Consortium et al. (2025)</a> show that strong correlative same-brain workflows still carry <strong>landmarks</strong>, <strong>targeted subvolumes</strong>, or local structure-function correspondences rather than one global state object. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-019-0555-4" target="_blank">Gallego et al. (2020)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-01982-7" target="_blank">Noda et al. (2025)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abj0751" target="_blank">Van De Ville et al. (2021)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2021.672946" target="_blank">Di et al. (2021)</a> then show that <strong>latent manifolds</strong>, <strong>representational maps</strong>, and <strong>fingerprint features</strong> can remain stable on different timescales while raw units or amplitudes drift. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-59652-y" target="_blank">Karpowicz et al. (2025)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41551-025-01536-z" target="_blank">Wilson et al. (2025)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09127-3" target="_blank">Wairagkar et al. (2025)</a> further show that stable use across time can depend on <strong>alignment</strong>, <strong>recalibration</strong>, or only a short <strong>fixed-decoder horizon</strong>. Therefore, on this site, specimen identity is not allowed to stand in for <strong>state continuity</strong>. If a claim depends on treating a multi-stage bridge as one latent-state sample, it now also needs a <a href="verification.html#state-continuity-bridge-card">Verification: State-Continuity Bridge Card</a> that names the <strong>carried object / witness</strong>, the <strong>tolerance / failure rule</strong>, and the <strong>rescue route</strong>, in addition to the <a href="verification.html#human-proxy-composition-card">Human Proxy Composition Card</a> or <a href="verification.html#destructive-structure-card">Destructive-Structure Route Card</a>. The longer bridge-specific background is in <a href="wiki/state-continuity-bridge.html">Wiki: State-Continuity Bridge</a>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Proxy-rich evidence is still not the same as unique internal-state recovery</strong>
<p>
The remaining weak point in this ladder was that several strong rows placed side by side could still sound like a near-complete solution. The primary literature does not support that shortcut. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1155/2019/8497093" target="_blank">Villaverde (2019)</a> distinguished observability from structural identifiability, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1352" target="_blank">Prinz et al. (2004)</a> showed that similar circuit activity can arise from disparate parameters, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1162/netn_a_00354" target="_blank">Rasero et al. (2024)</a> showed that similar human activation patterns can still hide different macroscopic network states, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-02080-4" target="_blank">Beiran &amp; Litwin-Kumar (2025)</a> showed that even connectome-constrained networks remain degenerate until additional activity observations are added. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.csbj.2025.10.058" target="_blank">Liu et al. (2025)</a> then showed that practical identifiability depends on how data are collected, not only on how models are fit. Therefore, on this site, the human observability ladder answers <strong>which variable class becomes more constrained</strong>; it does <strong>not</strong> answer by itself whether the compatible internal-state family has collapsed to one explanation. That second question is handled separately in <a href="verification.html#identifiability-card">Verification: Identifiability Card</a>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box" id="causal-observability-asymmetry">
<strong>The strongest causal papers and the best human routes are not the same ladder</strong>
<p>
A further correction is needed at the entry point. The papers that most strongly show that a maintenance-state family <strong>matters causally</strong> and the papers that most strongly improve <strong>living-human observability</strong> are often not the same papers, not the same species, and not the same spatial unit. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2014.07.017" target="_blank">Yiu et al. (2014)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-66975-3" target="_blank">Hadzibegovic et al. (2025)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-02152-5" target="_blank">Benoit et al. (2025)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2016.01.046" target="_blank">Hengen et al. (2016)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09774-6" target="_blank">Terceros et al. (2026)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09619-2" target="_blank">Dewa et al. (2025)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-10068-0" target="_blank">Bukalo et al. (2026)</a> strengthen causal relevance in local rodent circuits. The strongest current human routes remain heterogeneous and route-limited instead: perturbation-conditioned excitability proxies such as <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brs.2017.11.016" target="_blank">Zrenner et al. (2018)</a>, target-defined astrocyte-related PET routes such as <a href="https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.121.263254" target="_blank">Villemagne et al. (2022)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.118.208009" target="_blank">Tyacke et al. (2018)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s12149-025-02083-y" target="_blank">Hiraoka et al. (2025)</a>, plus bounded clearance-side routes such as <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-02073-3" target="_blank">Hirschler et al. (2025)</a> and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-68374-8" target="_blank">Dagum et al. (2026)</a>. Those human rows already differ in <strong>target class</strong>, <strong>direct observable</strong>, <strong>time window</strong>, and <strong>model burden</strong>. Therefore, the primary literature does not license the shortcut from <strong>"causal importance was shown somewhere"</strong> plus <strong>"a human proxy improved somewhere"</strong> to <strong>"the responsible controller is now measured in humans"</strong>. The composition rule that now enforces that stop line is spelled out in <a href="wiki/human-proxy-composition.html#causal-bridge">Wiki: Human Proxy Composition</a>.

(2025)</a> then showed in <strong>six healthy participants</strong> with arterial blood sampling that <strong>scan window</strong>, <strong>reference region</strong>, and <strong>kinetic-model choice</strong> still materially shape how <strong>[<sup>18</sup>F]SMBT-1</strong> is quantified, while <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s12149-025-02144-2" target="_blank">Mesfin et al. (2026)</a> added a separate <strong>whole-body biodistribution route</strong> with multi-session healthy-human scans over <strong>330 min</strong>. A parallel MAO-B tracer family is not identical either: <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s00259-025-07542-2" target="_blank">Matsuoka et al. (2026)</a> showed that <strong><sup>11</sup>C-SL25.1188</strong> MAO-B density in <strong>MCI / AD</strong> can be estimated with a simplified <strong>arterial-free DVR</strong> route and that the signal peaks around the <strong>MCI stage</strong>, while <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-025-03355-9" target="_blank">Best et al. (2026)</a> showed that <strong>SL25.1188</strong> binding can still shift with <strong>cohort severity</strong> and <strong>recent cigarette exposure</strong>. <a href="https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.118.208009" target="_blank">Tyacke et al. (2018)</a> established a first-in-human <strong>I<sub>2</sub>BS PET route</strong> with idazoxan-sensitive but not isocarboxazid-sensitive competition, which means that human astrocyte-related PET already splits by <strong>target class</strong>. On the clearance side, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-02073-3" target="_blank">Hirschler et al. (2025)</a> used a CSF-specific <strong>7 T MRI mobility route</strong> in <strong>20 healthy younger individuals</strong> and showed region-specific driving forces plus a separate CAA comparison cohort, whereas <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-68374-8" target="_blank">Dagum et al. (2026)</a> used an <strong>investigational wearable plus a multicompartment model</strong> in a randomized crossover study with <strong>39 analyzed participants</strong> to infer overnight brain-to-plasma biomarker efflux. These human rows therefore do not share one direct observable, one time axis, one quantification regime, or one controller identity. <div class="note-box">
<strong>Even simultaneous multimodal human acquisition still needs a Fusion Card</strong>
<p>
Same-session acquisition narrows one kind of mismatch, but it still does not prove that the modalities share one externally validated biological state variable. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1162/IMAG.a.136" target="_blank">Kothe et al. (2025)</a> showed that synchronization middleware solves stream alignment rather than device-side delay truth, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2020.116595" target="_blank">Wei et al. (2020)</a> showed that EEG-fMRI fusion remains model-conditioned, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-44363-z" target="_blank">Vafaii et al. (2024)</a> plus <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-64414-x" target="_blank">Chen et al. (2025)</a> showed that simultaneous multimodal recordings can reveal both common and divergent structure across modalities. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-01945-y" target="_blank">Bolt et al. (2025)</a> and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-019-0659-0" target="_blank">Özbay et al. (2019)</a> further showed that low-frequency/global fMRI-linked fluctuations can also carry autonomic physiology. Therefore, on this site, even strong same-session multimodal human evidence is still read through a <a href="verification.html#fusion-card">Fusion Card</a> rather than as automatic state completeness.
</p>
</div>

<div class="note-box">
<strong>A shared multimodal factor is not yet one solved state variable</strong>
<p>
This site now makes one more separation explicit at the entry page. A <strong>shared cross-modal component</strong> is a statistical object, not yet a biologically unique one. If a paper reports a coupled low-frequency mode or one common latent factor, the public claim still has to say whether that factor is being read as a <strong>shared neural candidate</strong>, a <strong>modality-specific residual left in the background</strong>, or a <strong>physiology-linked global factor</strong>. That disclosure now lives inside the <a href="verification.html#fusion-card">Fusion Card</a> rather than being left implicit under the word <strong>multimodal</strong>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Nanoscale structure still needs a destructive-route audit</strong>
<p>
At the entry point, it is also too strong to read <strong>nanoscale</strong>, <strong>petascale</strong>, or <strong>same-brain EM</strong> as if the route had already preserved the native live state by default. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crmeth.2023.100520" target="_blank">Lu et al. (2023)</a> showed that fixation route materially changes extracellular-space preservation and that even high-pressure freezing remains thickness-limited, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adk4858" target="_blank">Shapson-Coe et al. (2024)</a> showed that a human nanoscale reconstruction is still a rapidly preserved <strong>local surgical fragment</strong>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08790-w" target="_blank">MICrONS Consortium et al. (2025)</a> showed that same-brain function plus EM is a <strong>sequential local pipeline</strong> rather than simultaneous whole-state capture. Therefore, on this site, destructive ultrastructure is read through a <strong>preservation / registration / proofreading audit</strong>, not from resolution language alone. The longer rule is in <a href="wiki/measurement-stack-and-claim-ceiling.html#destructive-ultrastructure-wall">Wiki: preservation / registration / throughput wall for destructive ultrastructure</a> and <a href="verification.html#destructive-structure-card">Verification: Destructive-Structure Route Card</a>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Entry rule from this ladder</strong>
<p>
Do not collapse <strong>"human evidence exists"</strong> into one sentence. Structural scaffold, synaptic-density PET, receptor / transporter atlas priors, occupancy PET, displacement / release-sensitive PET, perturbation-conditioned sleep-homeostasis / plasticity proxies, state-gated perturbation routes, biochemical similarity scaffolds, <sup>31</sup>P metabolite / pH balance, <sup>31</sup>P MT exchange-flux, <sup>31</sup>P NAD-content mapping, localized functional <sup>31</sup>P NAD-dynamics, deuterium metabolite-mapping / absolute-quantification, deuterium kinetic-rate imaging, ionic proxies, thermometry, myelin maps, and clearance proxies are not interchangeable. On this site, each row reduces a different latent-state error term, and several of the higher-rung human routes still remain <strong>specialized, model-heavy, or small-cohort</strong>. None of them by itself upgrades WBE to state-complete or field-ready measurement. That last sentence is an inference from the measurement classes summarized above.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Do not promote perturbation-conditioned human routes to direct controller readout</strong>
<p>
Within this ladder, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhs014" target="_blank">Huber et al. (2013)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms12455" target="_blank">Kuhn et al. (2016)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2026.121723" target="_blank">Fehér et al. (2026)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brs.2017.11.016" target="_blank">Zrenner et al. (2018)</a> show that wake / sleep / nap history and ongoing EEG-defined state can change plasticity efficacy in humans. That is a real advance, but it still does not directly reveal AIS geometry, channel distribution, or the cell-specific recovery controller that produced the change. On this site, these rows are therefore read as <strong>perturbation-conditioned proxies</strong>, not as direct excitability ground truth.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Do not confuse hidden state with the measurement stack</strong>
<p>
The hidden states listed here are a list of important variables, not a list of variables currently observed. For what EEG, fMRI, spatial transcriptomics, Patch-seq, volume EM, and same-brain connectomics directly observe, and where each one reaches its claim ceiling, see <a href="wiki/measurement-stack-and-claim-ceiling.html">Wiki: Measurement-stack observability and claim ceilings</a>. The purpose is to stop the word “multimodal” from being read as if it already meant state-complete. In the same way, healthy-human <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awab466" target="_blank">MRS thermometry (Rzechorzek et al., 2022)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2024.1398034" target="_blank">task-linked thermal mapping (Rogala et al., 2024)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s00062-024-01467-3" target="_blank">frontal-lobe thermometry (Tan et al., 2025)</a> are important passive or task-linked macro thermal routes, while <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jshs.2023.09.001" target="_blank">Tan et al. (2024)</a> and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinph.2025.02.262" target="_blank">Inoue et al. (2025)</a> add bounded human perturbation-conditioned thermal routes through severe heat exposure and intraoperative focal cooling. None of those human rows, however, directly observes cell-specific temperature microgradients, synapse-level heating burden, or local thermal-controller state. Human <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-66124-w" target="_blank">five-metabolite 1H-MRSI similarity scaffolds (Lucchetti et al., 2025)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1109/TBME.2025.3572448" target="_blank">high-resolution 1H-MRSI metabolite-distribution mapping (Guo et al., 2025)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/nbm.3384" target="_blank"><sup>31</sup>P metabolite / pH balance (Ren et al., 2015)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.26560" target="_blank"><sup>31</sup>P MT exchange-flux (Ren et al., 2017)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2024.1389111" target="_blank"><sup>31</sup>P NAD-content mapping (Guo et al., 2024)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1177/0271678X261415784" target="_blank">localized functional <sup>31</sup>P NAD-dynamics (Kaiser et al., 2026)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.70308" target="_blank">deuterium metabolite-mapping / absolute quantification (Karkouri et al., 2026)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf072" target="_blank">dynamic deuterium kinetic-rate imaging under blood-input and explicit kinetic modeling (Li et al., 2025)</a> are important but distinct macro energetic routes. They still do not directly observe branch-local ATP reserve or mitochondrial positioning, and they do not collapse similarity, high-resolution metabolite distribution, metabolite balance, exchange flux, NAD content, local task dynamics, deuterium absolute metabolite mapping / quantification, and kinetic-rate imaging into one solved energetic object. Current human in vivo routes likewise do not directly observe isoform choice, m6A-reader engagement, or RNA-editing ratios across the whole living brain; specialized long-read atlas work such as <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-024-01616-4" target="_blank">Joglekar et al. (2024)</a> is informative but still not a whole-brain in vivo human route. Current human in vivo routes likewise do not directly observe phosphosite occupancy, kinase/phosphatase balance, or compartment-specific second-messenger nanodomains across the whole living brain; ex vivo phosphoproteome atlas work such as <a href="https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jproteome.2c00244" target="_blank">Biswas et al. (2023)</a> is informative but still not a comparable in vivo whole-brain human route. Human sodium MRI is also not one fixed ionic object: <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.23225" target="_blank">Qian et al. (2012)</a> mapped tissue sodium, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/nbm.2813" target="_blank">Fleysher et al. (2013)</a> estimated ISMF / ISC / ISVF with combined SQ+TQF imaging, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-18388-1" target="_blank">Rodriguez et al. (2022)</a> reported repeatable normalized sodium density-weighted quantification, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-07800-1" target="_blank">Qian et al. (2025)</a> separated mono- and bi-T<sub>2</sub> sodium signals. Those quantity types still do not directly observe cell-specific chloride concentration, KCC2 / NKCC1 balance, or local inhibitory reversal potential, and they do not collapse routine whole-brain intra- versus extracellular sodium partition into a solved problem. Current human in vivo routes likewise do not directly observe branch- or bouton-specific cargo pausing, motor engagement, or microtubule traffic state. Likewise, receptor / transporter atlas priors, occupancy PET, and displacement / release-sensitive PET constrain selected neuromodulatory systems, but they still do not directly observe the instantaneous whole-brain transmitter field.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>How this hidden-state list becomes an operational rule</strong>
<p>
The March 2026 update added the <a href="verification.html#observability-budget">Observability Budget</a> to Verification in order to convert hidden-state criticism into an operational rule. For several living-human proxy rows used together, the site now also asks for a <a href="verification.html#human-proxy-composition-card">Human Proxy Composition Card</a> so direct observables, evidence roles, same-subject relation, model burden, and residual latent states are not hidden behind the word <strong>multimodal</strong> or the phrase <strong>proxy-rich</strong>. For multimodal or atlas-prior results, the site additionally stacks a <a href="verification.html#fusion-card">Fusion Card</a> so that acquisition relation, synchronization, co-registration, fusion model, shared-vs-specific component disclosure, and abstention boundary are made explicit rather than implied by the word <strong>multimodal</strong>. For destructive ultrastructure claims, the site additionally asks for a <a href="verification.html#destructive-structure-card">Destructive-Structure Route Card</a> so preservation route, registration scope, throughput burden, and omitted live-state families are not hidden behind the words <strong>petascale</strong> or <strong>nanoscale</strong>. From L1 upward, results are expected to report these boundaries together.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>What this means in the introduction</strong>
<p>
The direct takeaway is that <strong>connectome-complete means progress on the structural scaffold</strong>, not that one may already declare <strong>emulation-complete</strong>. That is an inference from the literature above, which leaves maintenance-state variables as a separate problem. The detailed justification is concentrated in <a href="wiki/connectome-is-not-enough.html">Wiki: Why wiring diagrams alone are not enough</a> and <a href="wiki/homeostatic-plasticity-and-maintenance-state.html">Wiki: Homeostatic plasticity and maintenance state</a>.
</p>

(2013)</a> and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1232655" target="_blank">Ravassard et al. (2016)</a> showed that nasal respiration modulates human limbic activity and memory, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abd0380" target="_blank">Flesher et al. Therefore, Mind-Upload reads a closed-loop result safely only after the paper says which body / environment channels were preserved, substituted, or omitted, and which slow internal-milieu routes remained matched or latent.
</p>

<section class="section" id="decode-vs-emulate">
<h2 class="section-title">Decode (translation) and emulate (generation) are different</h2>
<p>
Decoding stays at the level of translating observed neural activity into a sentence, label, or other output. WBE would require a system whose internal state evolves autonomously over time, responds to intervention, and can generate future output from that internal dynamics. That gap is why <strong>counterfactuals</strong> and <strong>intervention prediction</strong> matter.
</p>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>The common substitution error</strong>
<p>
If a result is summarized only as “brain to text worked,” what may actually exist is a translator optimized to produce plausible text. To support a claim closer to WBE, the system must be checked under branching conditions: if the condition changes, does the internal behavior still match in the relevant way?
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>When you get stuck at the entrance to causal verification</strong>
<p>
Held-out accuracy, intervention, counterfactual, and perturbation-based verification have different strengths. For a plain-language route into that distinction, see <a href="wiki/counterfactual-and-perturbation-verification.html">Wiki: Counterfactual, intervention, and perturbation verification</a>.
</p>
</div>

<section class="section" id="alternative-approaches">
<h2 class="section-title">Technology-route comparison: what evidence each route currently provides</h2>
<p>
There are multiple routes toward something WBE-like. What should be fixed first at the entry page is not “which philosophy should we choose?” but rather <strong>what each route has actually demonstrated, and how far that evidence reaches</strong>. The comparison below stays at the level of measurement density, intervention capacity, closed-loop demonstration, and missing state variables.
</p>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>How to read this section</strong>
<p>
Non-invasive decoding, invasive closed loop, connectomics, and large-scale simulation are not simply competing camps. They are <strong>complementary routes that close different gaps</strong>. The question here is not which route “wins,” but what level of claim each route can currently support.
</p>
</div>
<table class="data-table">
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Route</th>
<th>Evidence already published</th>
<th>What can be said safely</th>
<th>What is still missing</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td><strong>Non-invasive decoding</strong></td>
<td>Tang et al. (2023) showed subject-cooperative semantic reconstruction from within-subject fMRI; Défossez et al. (2023) showed 3 s speech-segment identification from non-invasive M/EEG; d'Ascoli et al. (2025) scaled known-word-onset decoding to 723 participants and five million words; and Ye et al. (2025) showed prompt-conditioned fMRI-to-LLM generation.</td>
<td>A stronger <strong>L1</strong> picture than older summaries allowed: task-conditioned semantic reconstruction, candidate-bank segment retrieval, known-onset word decoding, and prompt-conditioned generation all make measurable progress within bounded regimes.</td>
<td>Free-running onset detection, unrestricted brain-only generation, intervention response, cross-person unique internal-state recovery, and WBE-relevant state completeness are still missing.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Invasive / hybrid neuroprosthesis</strong></td>
<td>Willett et al. (2023) showed a 62 words-per-minute speech BCI with a 125,000-word vocabulary and a bounded no-new-day-training slice; Singh et al. (2025) showed cross-subject transfer learning for individual speech-decoder initialization; Merk et al. (2025) showed connectomics-informed across-patient movement decoding without patient individual training across 56 implanted patients / 1,480 ECoG channels and extended the framework to emotion and seizure-related use cases; Flesher et al. (2021) improved robotic grasp time with bidirectional BCI; Berger et al. (2011) reported closed-loop memory-task recovery.</td>
<td>There is evidence approaching <strong>L2-L3</strong> for local circuits, but it now breaks into distinct routes: same-session communication throughput, transfer-assisted initialization, connectomics-informed across-patient symptom decoding, and task-specific causal closed-loop improvement.</td>
<td>Long-horizon fixed-decoder durability, rescue burden across days, physiological-state guards across everyday context, multi-region coverage, neuron-by-neuron replacement benchmarks, and a disclosed body / environment boundary are still missing.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Structural connectomics / local calibration / connectome-constrained modeling</strong></td>
<td>Dorkenwald et al. (2024) provided an adult drosophila whole-brain wiring atlas, MICrONS Consortium et al. (2025) linked responses of 75,909 excitatory neurons to ex vivo EM within one mouse visual-cortex volume, and Lappalainen et al. (2024) showed rich fly-visual-system activity prediction only when single-neuron-resolution connectivity and task optimization were used.</td>
<td>Extremely important as a structural atlas, same-brain local scaffold, and task-bounded conditional hypothesis engine for local circuit work.</td>
<td>Edge robustness for weaker connections, cross-modal cell-label robustness, unique internal-state recovery, shared extracellular / electrical state such as electrical synapses or ephaptic field effects, nonlinear chemical synapses, neuromodulation, whole-brain generalization, and maintenance-state completeness are still missing.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Large-scale biophysical simulation</strong></td>
<td>Billeh et al. (2020) built a data-driven multiscale model of awake mouse V1, while Prinz et al. (2004) showed that similar activity can arise from very different parameter sets.</td>
<td>Useful for mechanistic hypothesis testing, stimulus-conditioned digital-twin / connectome-constrained predictor families, and sensitivity analysis of missing measurements.</td>
<td>Parameter identifiability, whole-brain fidelity, OOD generalization, and perturbation matching remain open.</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>2026-03-25 addendum: non-invasive language decoding is not one evidence class</strong>
<p>
The front-door comparison was still too coarse when it summarized all non-invasive language work through Tang (2023) alone. The current primary literature is narrower and more structured than that. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-023-01304-9" target="_blank">Tang et al. (2023)</a> is a <strong>within-subject fMRI semantic-reconstruction route</strong> with an explicit subject-cooperation requirement. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-023-00714-5" target="_blank">Défossez et al. (2023)</a> is a <strong>non-invasive M/EEG speech-segment retrieval route</strong> over 3 s windows from large candidate sets whose predictions primarily reflect lexical and contextual representations. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-65499-0" target="_blank">d'Ascoli et al. (2025)</a> is a <strong>known-word-onset word-decoding route</strong> that still depends strongly on protocol, with <strong>MEG &gt; EEG</strong> and <strong>reading &gt; listening</strong>. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-07731-7" target="_blank">Ye et al. (2025)</a> is a <strong>prompt-conditioned generative route</strong> where brain input improves over a permuted-brain control but does not erase prompt / LLM dependence. On this site, those are therefore treated as different evidence classes rather than one monotonic path to unrestricted thought reading.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>2026-03-28 addendum: invasive language BCIs are not one operational route either</strong>
<p>
The invasive side was still too coarse if it only contrasted `speech BCI` against `whole-brain emulation`. The current primary literature supports a sharper split. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06377-x" target="_blank">Willett et al. (2023)</a> strengthened <strong>same-session communication throughput</strong> and also exposed a bounded <strong>fixed-decoder slice</strong> by showing reasonable offline performance even without new-day retraining. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-01905-6" target="_blank">Littlejohn et al. (2025)</a> strengthened <strong>streaming throughput / expressivity</strong>. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09127-3" target="_blank">Wairagkar et al. (2025)</a> strengthened <strong>instantaneous voice synthesis with silence fallback</strong>, but did not erase the distinction between fast voice output and long-horizon fixed decoders. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-63825-0" target="_blank">Singh et al. (2025)</a> instead strengthened a <strong>cross-subject transfer / initialization</strong> route, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-59652-y" target="_blank">Karpowicz et al. (2025)</a> plus <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41551-025-01536-z" target="_blank">Wilson et al. (2025)</a> strengthened <strong>adaptive stabilization / unsupervised rescue</strong>. On this site, those are no longer read as one monotonic invasive-BCI ladder.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>2026-04-01 addendum: therapeutic invasive decoders are not one universal route</strong>
<p>
One more invasive shortcut had to be blocked at the entry page. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41551-025-01467-9" target="_blank">Merk et al. (2025)</a> combined invasive electrophysiology with whole-brain connectomic fingerprints to decode movement without patient-individual training across <strong>56 implanted patients</strong>, and extended the same platform to emotion and seizure-related routes. That is a major advance in <strong>symptom-linked therapeutic decoding</strong>, but it is still not a subject-free universal decoder or a step that closes WBE-level state completeness. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-024-03196-z" target="_blank">Oehrn et al. (2024)</a> then showed that adaptive DBS can improve outcomes through <strong>patient-specific biomarker selection</strong> rather than one generic controller, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41531-026-01273-3" target="_blank">Zhu et al. (2026)</a> showed that even eyes-open versus eyes-closed physiology can shift basal-ganglia oscillatory feedback signals enough that fixed thresholds risk confusing benign state change with pathology. On this site, a generalizable invasive decoder is therefore read as a <strong>connectomics-informed, symptom-/state-conditioned therapeutic-control route</strong> that still needs a named <strong>implant target</strong>, <strong>controller family</strong>, <strong>physiological-state guard</strong>, and <strong>programming burden</strong>, rather than as generic internal-state reading or emulation.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>What this comparison really shows</strong>
<p>
The strongest support in the current primary literature is for <strong>better decoding</strong>, <strong>closed-loop improvement in local subsystems</strong>, <strong>structure-function mapping</strong>, and <strong>stimulus-conditioned digital-twin / connectome-constrained predictor families</strong>. The key question is therefore not route preference, but <strong>which evidence profile supports which rung of the claim ladder</strong>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Do not collapse connectome progress into one rung</strong>
<p>
This site now separates at least five kinds of connectome progress: <strong>wiring atlas</strong>, <strong>same-brain local scaffold</strong>, <strong>human macro pathway prior / tractography connectome</strong>, <strong>connectome-constrained conditional predictor</strong>, and <strong>identifiability audit</strong>. Dorkenwald et al. (2024), MICrONS Consortium et al. (2025), tractography-validation papers such as Thomas et al. (2014) and Grisot et al. (2021), and Lappalainen et al. (2024) strengthen different parts of that ladder, while Beiran &amp; Litwin-Kumar (2025) show why connectome alone still does not uniquely fix dynamics. On this site, the MICrONS-side `digital twin` language is therefore read as a <strong>stimulus-conditioned response model</strong> layered onto a <strong>sequential same-brain scaffold</strong>, not as direct current synaptic-state readout or one solved local twin. Therefore, “connectome progress” is not one claim level on this site.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>2026-03-21 addendum: flagship connectome papers still solve different problems</strong>
<p>
The current connectome frontier is stronger than older summaries allowed, but its strongest papers still do not support one single inference. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07558-y" target="_blank">Dorkenwald et al. (2024)</a> provide a whole-adult-fly wiring atlas, yet the authors still warn that false-negative and false-positive synapses remain and explicitly threshold connections for downstream analysis. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07686-5" target="_blank">Schlegel et al. (2024)</a> then showed across three fly hemispheres that edges stronger than ten synapses or at least 0.9% of the target cell type's total inputs persist more than 90% of the time, which means weaker edges and fine cell-type claims still need a robustness audit rather than binary trust. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08790-w" target="_blank">MICrONS Consortium et al. (2025)</a> provide a same-brain local structure-function scaffold in one mouse visual-cortex volume, but the EM route still followed in vivo imaging sequentially and remained a local visual-system pipeline. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08805-6" target="_blank">Gamlin et al. (2025)</a> further show that even inside a large EM volume, transcriptomic Sst types were reached by morphology-based cross-modal prediction rather than by direct transcriptomic readout from the connectome itself. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07939-3" target="_blank">Lappalainen et al. (2024)</a> showed that connectome structure plus task optimization can predict rich activity, but models with only cell-type connectivity predicted neural activity poorly. Finally, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-02080-4" target="_blank">Beiran &amp; Litwin-Kumar (2025)</a> showed that recurrent networks sharing the same synaptic weights can still diverge strongly in dynamics when biophysical parameters differ unless additional recordings collapse the solution space. On this site, these are therefore read as <strong>different evidence classes</strong>, not as one monotonic countdown to state-complete WBE.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Connectome-constrained predictors need a route card</strong>
<p>
Even a strong connectome-constrained predictor is still read here first as a <strong>conditional model / hypothesis engine</strong>. The safe reading depends on disclosing <strong>which structural prior was actually used</strong>, <strong>which parameters remained fitted</strong>, <strong>which task/state regime was tested</strong>, <strong>which mechanisms were omitted</strong>, and <strong>where the claim stops</strong>. Without that route card, this site does not promote a good activity-prediction result to unique internal-state recovery. The longer operational rule is in <a href="wiki/connectome-is-not-enough.html#conditional-predictor-route-card">Wiki: conditional-model route card</a> and <a href="verification.html#observability-budget">Verification: Observability Budget</a>.
</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="section" id="what-to-do-next">
<h2 class="section-title">What should be built next to count as progress?</h2>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>The next bottleneck is not modality count alone</strong>
<p>
Recent primary literature makes one correction necessary even at the entry page. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1155/2019/8497093" target="_blank">Villaverde (2019)</a> separates observability from identifiability, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1352" target="_blank">Prinz et al. (2004)</a> showed that similar circuit activity can arise from disparate parameters, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1162/netn_a_00354" target="_blank">Rasero et al. (2024)</a> showed that similar human responses can still hide different macroscopic network states, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-02080-4" target="_blank">Beiran &amp; Litwin-Kumar (2025)</a> showed that connectome-constrained networks remain degenerate until additional activity observations are added, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.csbj.2025.10.058" target="_blank">Liu et al. (2025)</a> showed that practical identifiability depends on the data-collection policy itself. Therefore, after fixing the victory conditions, the next build step is to decide <strong>which measurement or perturbation would actually rule out the main alternative internal-state explanations</strong>.
</p>
</div>

<div class="stage-item">
<div class="stage-number">1</div>
<div class="stage-body">
<h4>Fix the victory conditions first</h4>
<p>This field is unusually vulnerable to progress-by-wording. The most important first move is to write the metric and the counter-condition in advance.</p>
<a href="tech_roadmap.html#definition" class="btn-action">Roadmap: Definition of progress</a>

<div class="stage-item">
<div class="stage-number">2</div>
<div class="stage-body">
<h4>Collapse competing internal-state solutions</h4>
<p>Before adding modalities by count, first name the surviving ambiguity, then choose the smallest extra regime, perturbation, targeted recording, or adaptive measurement window that is actually expected to separate the near-equivalent models, states, or controllers, and say what minimum-sufficiency stop rule would show that the ambiguity truly narrowed rather than merely shifting model error elsewhere.</p>

<h4>Gather reproducible inputs</h4>
<p>Use BIDS or EEG-BIDS, metadata, and QC logs so a third party can run the same setup.</p>
<a href="datasets.html" class="btn-action">Datasets</a>

<div class="stage-number">4</div>
<div class="stage-body">
<h4>Publish comparable outputs</h4>
<p>Do not publish scores alone. Publish comparison rules, failure cases, and leakage checks together.</p>

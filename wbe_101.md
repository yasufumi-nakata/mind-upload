<div class="abstract-box">
<h2>TL;DR</h2>
<p>
If you want to move mind uploading forward seriously, the fastest route is to define in advance both the condition under which you can say “this worked” and the condition under which you must say “this failed.” Mind-Upload is building the verification infrastructure needed to make those checks comparable.
</p>

<section class="section" id="what-you-can-say">
<h2 class="section-title">First: what can and cannot be said now?</h2>
<div class="key-points">
<h4>Rule</h4>
<ul>
<li><strong>You can say:</strong> “Under these conditions, this task improved on this metric.”</li>
<li><strong>You cannot say:</strong> strong claims such as “consciousness was transferred” or “the person survived,” unless the definition and verification conditions are already fixed.</li>
<li><strong>Mind-Upload's role:</strong> decompose strong claims into smaller claims that can be falsified, and accumulate those instead.</li>
</ul>
</div>
</section>

<section class="section" id="definition">
<h2 class="section-title">What is WBE? An operational definition</h2>
<p>
WBE, or Whole Brain Emulation, is a broad label for research on running brain-relevant function on another substrate. But the required technology changes depending on what exactly counts as “reproduced.” To prevent the discussion from collapsing into slogans, Mind-Upload separates claim strength with a <strong>claim ladder</strong> (see P0/P1 in the Roadmap).
</p>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Key point</strong>
<p>
“The inputs and outputs look similar” is not enough for the kind of generation and internal causation WBE would require. That is why benchmark design is central.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>When you get stuck on “Isn't the connectome enough?”</strong>
<p>
Wiring diagrams matter, but they do not by themselves satisfy even the lower bound of WBE requirements. If you want a literature-based explanation of what is still missing when synaptic efficacy, activity-dependent transcription / chromatin state, post-transcriptional RNA-state, cargo-transport / cytoskeletal trafficking state, perisynaptic ECM / perineuronal nets, thermal-state, ionic milieu / chloride homeostasis, bioenergetic / mitochondrial state, delay and myelination, neuromodulation, glia, cell-type labels, and <strong>intrinsic excitability / homeostatic set points</strong> are not captured, see <a href="wiki/connectome-is-not-enough.html">Wiki: Why wiring diagrams alone are not enough</a> and <a href="wiki/homeostatic-plasticity-and-maintenance-state.html">Wiki: Homeostatic plasticity and maintenance state</a>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>When a thermodynamic paper sounds like the whole answer</strong>
<p>
At the WBE entry point, it is too early to treat one thermodynamic label as one solved layer. Mind-Upload separates <strong>Landauer lower bounds</strong>, <strong>biological energy budgets</strong>, <strong>irreversibility of coarse-grained neural time series</strong>, and <strong>model-based entropy-flow estimates</strong>. These can strengthen hypothesis formation, but on this site they remain <strong>auxiliary evidence</strong> unless the paper also discloses its <strong>signal route</strong>, <strong>estimator family</strong>, <strong>null / surrogate control</strong>, <strong>quantity type</strong>, and <strong>cost isolation</strong>. The shortest follow-up is <a href="faq.html#q1f">FAQ: how to read thermodynamic claims</a> plus <a href="wiki/thermodynamic-grounding-basics.html">Wiki: thermodynamic grounding basics</a>.
</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="section" id="state-completeness">
<h2 class="section-title">Hidden state that cannot be ignored at the entry point</h2>
<p>
It is dangerous to read WBE as if the wiring diagram and cell type were enough and everything else were just fine calibration. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-2907-3" target="_blank">Gouwens et al. (2021)</a> showed that morpho-electric variation still remains inside a single transcriptomic type. Furthermore, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adg9982" target="_blank">Santoni et al. (2024)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-025-02368-y" target="_blank">Coda et al. (2025)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09774-6" target="_blank">Terceros et al. (2026)</a> show that activity-dependent chromatin and transcriptional state can change which neurons are eligible for memory allocation and which late programs stabilize memory. But gene-level abundance is still not the whole RNA-state: <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4069" target="_blank">Wang et al. (2015)</a> showed that a neuron-specific LSD1 splice isoform regulates memory formation, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2019.03.032" target="_blank">Dai et al. (2019)</a> showed that presynaptic neurexin alternative splicing changes postsynaptic receptor balance and contextual memory, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0666-1" target="_blank">Shi et al. (2018)</a> and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.202514926" target="_blank">Li et al. (2025)</a> showed that m6A-reader routes can alter hippocampus-dependent learning and memory, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/scisignal.adr1442" target="_blank">Peterson et al. (2025)</a> showed that ADAR2-mediated GluA2 RNA editing contributes to homeostatic synaptic plasticity. In addition, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/385533a0" target="_blank">Frey &amp; Morris (1997)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms2250" target="_blank">Shires et al. (2012)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2010.12.008" target="_blank">Govindarajan et al. (2011)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2006.08.015" target="_blank">Fonseca et al. (2006)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1080/15548627.2020.1775393" target="_blank">Pandey et al. (2021)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-08459-0" target="_blank">Thomas et al. (2025)</a> show that late stabilization also depends on where synaptic tags are set, which branches capture plasticity-related proteins, how local translation/degradation/autophagy remain balanced, and which spine-state persists long enough to serve as the tag. Separately, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2006.09.040" target="_blank">Park et al. (2006)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0902304106" target="_blank">Maas et al. (2009)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2011.03.026" target="_blank">Yin et al. (2011)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.53456" target="_blank">Zhao et al. (2020)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2021.109369" target="_blank">Swarnkar et al. (2021)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2024.03.010" target="_blank">Aiken &amp; Holzbaur (2024)</a> show that recycling-endosome mobilization, microtubule-track regulation, kinesin-dependent receptor / RNA delivery, and presynaptic cargo retention can change spine growth, LTP, memory, and synaptogenesis even when the graph is held fixed. In addition, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2016.01.046" target="_blank">Hengen et al. (2016)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2021.04.004" target="_blank">Torrado Pacheco et al. (2021)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-47838-5" target="_blank">Xu et al. (2024)</a> show that sleep/wake cycles affect firing-rate homeostasis and recovery of the network's operating regime. More specifically, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2013.03.006" target="_blank">Ngo et al. (2013)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2017.06.025" target="_blank">Latchoumane et al. (2017)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-23520-2" target="_blank">Schreiner et al. (2021)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-023-01324-5" target="_blank">Geva-Sagiv et al. (2023)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-49572-8" target="_blank">Schreiner et al. (2024)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2025.03.020" target="_blank">Deng et al. (2025)</a> show that overnight consolidation depends not only on whether sleep occurred but on phase-locked slow-oscillation / spindle / ripple coordination and specific NREM windows. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1252304" target="_blank">Gibson et al. (2014)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1254960" target="_blank">McKenzie et al. (2014)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-023-01558-3" target="_blank">Looser et al. (2024)</a> show that myelin and oligodendrocyte support matter for timing and axonal health. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7793.1998.249bu.x" target="_blank">Hardingham &amp; Larkman (1998)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7793.2000.00059.x" target="_blank">Volgushev et al. (2000)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.8446900" target="_blank">Moser et al. (1993)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/nature07448" target="_blank">Long &amp; Fee (2008)</a> further show that local thermal-state can change synaptic reliability, spike generation, field-potential amplitude, and sequence timing without rewiring. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1072699" target="_blank">Pizzorusso et al. (2002)</a> showed that digesting chondroitin-sulfate proteoglycans can reopen adult ocular-dominance plasticity, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2338" target="_blank">Frischknecht et al. (2009)</a> showed that brain extracellular matrix constrains AMPA-receptor lateral mobility and short-term plasticity, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1174146" target="_blank">Gogolla et al. (2009)</a> showed that perineuronal nets protect fear memories from erasure, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.matbio.2024.11.001" target="_blank">Jabłońska et al. (2024)</a> showed that ECM integrity regulates hippocampal GABAergic plasticity. In addition, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1245423" target="_blank">Glykys et al. (2014)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-017-01749-0" target="_blank">Heubl et al. (2017)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aad4821" target="_blank">Ding et al. (2016)</a> show that local chloride set point and interstitial ion composition can change inhibitory polarity and brain-state regime even without rewiring. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/99496" target="_blank">Galarreta &amp; Hestrin (1999)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2727" target="_blank">Anastassiou et al. (2011)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2313042121" target="_blank">Yang et al. (2024)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-53074-y" target="_blank">Selfe et al. (2024)</a> further show that gap-junction coupling, endogenous electric fields, dynamic electrical-synapse assembly, and inhibitory driving force can alter fast synchrony and spike timing outside the chemical synapse graph. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2013.12.042" target="_blank">Rangaraju et al. (2014)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2018.12.013" target="_blank">Rangaraju et al. (2019)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2018.09.025" target="_blank">Divakaruni et al. (2018)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-44233-8" target="_blank">Bapat et al. (2024)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-08963-3" target="_blank">Hu et al. (2025)</a> further show that local ATP supply, mitochondrial positioning, fission/fusion, and synaptic ATP-synthase organization can still change repeated-burst reliability and dendritic plasticity even on the same graph. Finally, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2011.02.018" target="_blank">Suzuki et al. (2011)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07311-5" target="_blank">Cahill et al. (2024)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08170-w" target="_blank">Williamson et al. (2025)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09619-2" target="_blank">Dewa et al. (2025)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-10068-0" target="_blank">Bukalo et al. (2026)</a> show that astrocyte-state is not just metabolic background: astrocytes can support long-term memory formation, encode minute-scale network responses to local transmitter input, and causally influence recall, multiday stabilization, and amygdala fear-state representations in rodents.
</p>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Transcription / chromatin evidence now gets its own route card</strong>
<p>
At the entry point, the important correction is that <strong>allocation eligibility</strong>, <strong>acute time-course mapping</strong>, <strong>persistent stabilization programs</strong>, and <strong>locus-specific causal editability</strong> are not one evidence class. On this site, readers are now directed to the <a href="wiki/homeostatic-plasticity-and-maintenance-state.html#transcription-route-card">Wiki: transcription / chromatin route card</a> so species, region, time window, assay, animal-level independence, perturbation status, and abstention boundary are fixed before a claim is promoted.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Post-transcriptional RNA-state is not just transcript abundance</strong>
<p>
At the entry point, it is now too coarse to read <strong>post-transcriptional RNA evidence</strong> as one class. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4069" target="_blank">Wang et al. (2015)</a> is a splice-isoform route whose downstream object is chromatin / transcriptional control, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2019.03.032" target="_blank">Dai et al. (2019)</a> is a splice-dependent receptor-balance route, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0666-1" target="_blank">Shi et al. (2018)</a> and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.202514926" target="_blank">Li et al. (2025)</a> are distinct m6A translation-versus-degradation routes, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/scisignal.adr1442" target="_blank">Peterson et al. <h3 class="section-title" id="human-observability-ladder">What human evidence currently looks like</h3>
<p>
One weakness of the earlier front door was that it listed hidden states without separating the layers of human evidence that now exist. The primary literature no longer supports either extreme reading: neither <strong>"humans show almost nothing"</strong> nor <strong>"humans are close to state-complete measurement."</strong> Instead, different measurement classes push different layers upward, and they reduce different error terms. Another weakness was that <strong>proxy class</strong> and <strong>operational maturity</strong> could still be silently collapsed into one impression. This table now keeps those two readings separate.
The first row is included because it is a human-side advance, but it is <strong>not</strong> a living-human in vivo route and must be read through the destructive-structure audit rather than through proxy-ladder language alone.

<div class="note-box">
<strong>The myelin row also needs a route card</strong>
<p>
At the entry point, it is too coarse to read <strong>myelin evidence</strong> as one class. On this site, <strong>learning-dependent oligodendrogenesis</strong>, <strong>node / internode / periaxonal timing control</strong>, <strong>plasticity-brake evidence</strong>, <strong>remyelination-to-function recovery</strong>, and <strong>human macro-myelin proxy rows</strong> are treated as different inferential objects. Therefore, a human myelin map or tract-speed estimate is not promoted here to solved biological timing-state, and functional recovery after remyelination is not read as proof that healthy myelin-state was completely restored. The longer operating rule is in <a href="wiki/homeostatic-plasticity-and-maintenance-state.html#myelin-route-card">Wiki: myelin / oligodendrocyte route card</a>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Ionic / chloride evidence now gets its own route card</strong>
<p>
At the entry point, it is also too coarse to read <strong>ionic evidence</strong> as one class. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1245423" target="_blank">Glykys et al. (2014)</a> is about <strong>local chloride set point</strong>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-017-01749-0" target="_blank">Heubl et al. (2017)</a> is about <strong>activity-dependent KCC2 regulation</strong>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aad4821" target="_blank">Ding et al. (2016)</a> and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1111/jsr.13522" target="_blank">Forsberg et al. (2022)</a> are about <strong>interstitial / CSF ion composition linked to sleep-wake state</strong>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2023.112934" target="_blank">Byvaltsev et al. (2023)</a> is about <strong>perisynaptic K<sup>+</sup> clearance by reverse-mode KCC2</strong>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2025.01.050" target="_blank">Alfonsa et al. (2025)</a> is about <strong>sleep-wake-history-related E<sub>GABAA</sub> shifts that alter LTP induction</strong>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.23225" target="_blank">Qian et al. (2012)</a> plus <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-07800-1" target="_blank">Qian et al. (2025)</a> remain <strong>macro human ionic proxies</strong>. On this site, ionic claims now require a route card that fixes <strong>claim family</strong>, <strong>direct ionic observable</strong>, <strong>spatial regime</strong>, <strong>perturbation / controller route</strong>, <strong>human proxy class</strong>, and <strong>abstention boundary</strong>. The longer rule is in <a href="wiki/homeostatic-plasticity-and-maintenance-state.html#ionic-route-card">Wiki: ionic / chloride route card</a>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Do not fold local human ultrastructure into the in vivo proxy ladder</strong>
<p>
The safe reading here is stricter than simply saying that human evidence is getting richer. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crmeth.2023.100520" target="_blank">Lu et al. (2023)</a> showed that preservation route changes extracellular-space retention and native geometry, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adk4858" target="_blank">Shapson-Coe et al. (2024)</a> remained a rapidly preserved local surgical fragment, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08790-w" target="_blank">MICrONS Consortium et al. (2025)</a> remained a sequential same-brain pipeline rather than same-time capture, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07558-y" target="_blank">Dorkenwald et al. (2024)</a> still required large proofreading effort at petascale. Therefore, this site treats local human ultrastructure as a <strong>destructive-route class</strong> and the living-human rows below as a separate <strong>in vivo proxy ladder</strong>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Clearance / immune support is not passive cleanup</strong>
<p>
At the entry point, it is also too coarse to read <strong>clearance evidence</strong> as one class. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14432" target="_blank">Louveau et al. (2015)</a> and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1419-5" target="_blank">Ahn et al. (2019)</a> established meningeal-lymphatic drainage routes, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2025.02.022" target="_blank">Kim et al. (2025)</a> showed that the meningeal-lymphatics-microglia axis can regulate synaptic physiology, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awaa443" target="_blank">Eide &amp; Ringstad (2021)</a> showed that sleep deprivation impairs molecular clearance in humans, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-02073-3" target="_blank">Hirschler et al. (2025)</a> measured region-specific CSF-mobility drivers, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-68374-8" target="_blank">Dagum et al. (2026)</a> linked sleep-active physiology to overnight Aβ / tau clearance to plasma in healthy older adults. On this site, <strong>drainage anatomy</strong>, <strong>microglia-related synaptic control</strong>, <strong>human CSF-mobility MRI</strong>, and <strong>model-based human biomarker efflux</strong> are treated as different inferential objects. Therefore, a human CSF-mobility map is not read as direct flux ground truth, and a plasma Aβ / tau clearance model is not read as local immune-controller identification. The longer operating rule is in <a href="wiki/homeostatic-plasticity-and-maintenance-state.html#clearance-route-card">Wiki: clearance / immune route card</a>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>The MRSI metabolic-connectome row also needs a route card</strong>
<p>
The remaining weakness in this ladder was that <strong>metabolic connectome</strong> could still sound like another brain-wide wiring graph or a direct metabolic-flux map. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-66124-w" target="_blank">Lucchetti et al. (2025)</a> defined the object more narrowly: a within-subject similarity matrix built from <strong>pairwise correlations among five metabolites across gray-matter parcels</strong> in <strong>51 healthy participants</strong>, with replication in an independent sample of 13. The same paper also showed that overall metabolic similarity aligns only <strong>weakly</strong> with tractography-based structural connectivity and more closely with cytoarchitectonic similarity and gene co-expression. On this site, that row is therefore read first as a <strong>parcel-level biochemical similarity scaffold</strong>, not as another structural connectome.
</p>
<p>
The measurement model also has to stay visible. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/brb3.1852" target="_blank">Bhogal et al. (2020)</a> showed that in vivo MRSI remains sensitive to <strong>low SNR</strong>, <strong>partial-volume effects</strong>, and <strong>extracranial lipid artifacts</strong>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2022.119574" target="_blank">Wright et al. (2022)</a> showed that using averaged instead of voxel-specific metabolite T<sub>1</sub> corrections can bias maps, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1148/radiol.232401" target="_blank">Baboli et al. (2024)</a> showed that absolute quantification changes when tissue-water / relaxation correction is individualized. By contrast, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf072" target="_blank">Li et al. (2025)</a> used <strong>dynamic DMRSI plus a kinetic model</strong> to map glucose-transport and metabolic rates, which is a different inferential object. Therefore, on this site, an MRSI-derived metabolic graph is not read without the <strong>metabolite set</strong>, <strong>parceling unit</strong>, <strong>resolution plus PSF / partial-volume correction</strong>, <strong>water / lipid handling</strong>, <strong>spectral QC thresholds</strong>, and whether the claim is <strong>static similarity</strong> or <strong>kinetic rate imaging</strong>. The longer operational rule is in <a href="wiki/homeostatic-plasticity-and-maintenance-state.html#metabolic-connectome-route-card">Wiki: metabolic-connectome route card</a>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>The tractography row now needs a route card</strong>
<p>
The weak point on this page was not that it called tractography a <strong>macro pathway prior</strong>, but that the phrase could still sound too stable, as if every tractography-derived connectome were the same inferential object once a modern pipeline had been applied. The newer route-audit literature argues against that shortcut. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2023.120376" target="_blank">Sarwar et al. (2023)</a> showed that filtering helps much less in complex human-like architectures than in simple bundles, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2024.120904" target="_blank">He et al. (2024)</a> showed that filtering can materially change structural laterality estimates, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mri.2025.110424" target="_blank">McMaster et al. (2025)</a> showed that voxel size changes the resulting connectome, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2025.103580" target="_blank">Manzano-Patrón et al. (2025)</a> showed that uncertainty should be propagated rather than hidden, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2025.103498" target="_blank">Zhu et al. (2025)</a> improved reconstruction by fusing MRI with microscopy. Therefore, this site now asks readers to treat the tractography row as a <strong>route card</strong>: read acquisition, priors, filtering, uncertainty, and abstention before reading the graph. The longer operational rule is in <a href="wiki/connectome-is-not-enough.html#tractography-route-card">Wiki: tractography route card</a>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Human proxy class is not the same thing as operational maturity</strong>
<p>
The strongest correction needed here was not to delete these rows, but to stop readers from treating them as equally mature. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1750-23.2024" target="_blank">Johansen et al. (2024)</a> built an SV2A atlas from <strong>33 healthy participants</strong> calibrated against postmortem autoradiography, which is a real atlas resource but still a cohort-level synaptic-density proxy. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-66124-w" target="_blank">Lucchetti et al. (2025)</a> used <strong>51 healthy adolescents</strong> plus an <strong>independent replication sample of 13</strong> to derive a metabolic similarity matrix from five <sup>1</sup>H-MRSI metabolites, which is a biochemical organization scaffold rather than direct flux imaging. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf072" target="_blank">Li et al. (2025)</a> quantified glucose-transport and metabolic-rate maps in only <strong>five healthy participants</strong> using <strong>7 T</strong>, custom dual-frequency coils, and blood input functions, while <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.29998" target="_blank">Baadsvik et al. (2024)</a> demonstrated myelin-bilayer mapping in <strong>two healthy volunteers</strong> on high-performance hardware. Likewise, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-02073-3" target="_blank">Hirschler et al. (2025)</a> introduced a specialized 7 T CSF-mobility route whose whole-brain rest maps were shown in <strong>20 healthy younger individuals</strong>, with driver analyses reported in <strong>11 of 24 total healthy participants</strong>. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-68374-8" target="_blank">Dagum et al. (2026)</a> combined a <strong>randomized crossover trial with 39 participants</strong>, an <strong>investigational wearable</strong>, and a <strong>compartmental model</strong> to infer sleep-linked clearance. Therefore, on this site, the ladder now has to be read along two axes at once: <strong>what variable class the route constrains</strong> and <strong>how specialized or deployment-limited that route still is</strong>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>PET rows need a measurement model</strong>
<p>
Within this ladder, PET-based rows are not readable from the modality label alone. <a href="https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.120.249144" target="_blank">Naganawa et al. (2021)</a> showed that human SV2A PET quantification depends on the tracer, arterial-versus-reference quantification route, compartment model, and named scan window. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1177/0271678X20946198" target="_blank">Smart et al. (2021)</a> showed that [<sup>11</sup>C]UCB-J binding measures remain unchanged during brief visual activation even when tracer influx rises with blood flow, so momentary neural activity is not the same object as synaptic-density PET. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-022-01186-3" target="_blank">Hansen et al. (2022)</a> collated group-average receptor maps from more than 1,200 healthy participants, so receptor atlas is a normative chemoarchitectural prior rather than an individual's current state. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-013-3103-z" target="_blank">Wong et al. (2013)</a> showed selected D<sub>2</sub>-receptor target engagement by an administered drug, whereas <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/30498" target="_blank">Koepp et al. (1998)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-08143-4" target="_blank">Lippert et al. (2019)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41386-019-0567-5" target="_blank">Erritzoe et al. (2020)</a> used challenge-linked binding changes as release-sensitive proxies for selected dopamine or serotonin systems. Therefore, on this site, PET evidence is not read without its tracer, occupancy-versus-displacement design, quantification model, spatial scope, and time window.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Occupancy and displacement PET answer different questions</strong>
<p>
<a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-013-3103-z" target="_blank">Wong et al. (2013)</a> estimated whether an administered drug occupied a selected receptor target. By contrast, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/30498" target="_blank">Koepp et al. (1998)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-08143-4" target="_blank">Lippert et al. (2019)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41386-019-0567-5" target="_blank">Erritzoe et al. (2020)</a> used task- or drug-challenge-linked binding changes as endogenous release proxies within bounded windows. Those are not the same inferential object. On this site, occupancy PET is read as <strong>target engagement</strong>, while displacement PET is read as a <strong>challenge-limited release proxy</strong>. Neither is promoted to direct whole-brain neuromodulatory state.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Do not compress neuromodulation into one line</strong>
<p>
Mixed arousal proxies, local transmitter sensors, receptor / transporter atlas priors, occupancy PET, and displacement / release-sensitive PET are not one measurement class. On this site, they reduce different error terms and support different claim ceilings. The short rule is simple: do not promote a receptor map, an occupancy result, or a challenge-limited PET effect to the claim that the current whole-brain neuromodulatory state was identified. For the detailed ladder, see <a href="wiki/measurement-stack-and-claim-ceiling.html#neuromodulatory-proxy-ladder">Wiki: neuromodulatory proxy ladder</a>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Do not read multiple human proxy rows as automatic state completeness</strong>
<p>
A second correction follows from the same literature: even when several human proxy classes appear in the same argument, they still do not compose automatically into a state-complete readout. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adk4858" target="_blank">Shapson-Coe et al. (2024)</a> is a local structural scaffold, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1750-23.2024" target="_blank">Johansen et al. (2024)</a> is a cohort-level synaptic-density proxy, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-66124-w" target="_blank">Lucchetti et al. (2025)</a> is a parcel-level biochemical scaffold, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf072" target="_blank">Li et al. (2025)</a> and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.29998" target="_blank">Baadsvik et al. (2024)</a> are specialized macro energetic / myelin routes, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-02073-3" target="_blank">Hirschler et al. (2025)</a> plus <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-68374-8" target="_blank">Dagum et al. (2026)</a> are support-state routes with explicit model burden. These rows differ in quantity type, spatial unit, time window, quantification model, and operating burden, and the cited papers do not show that they can already be acquired in the same person, same session, same perturbation regime, with externally validated fusion. Therefore, on this site, <strong>proxy-rich human evidence</strong> is read as stronger than a single proxy row but still weaker than <strong>same-subject, cross-stack, externally calibrated state identification</strong>. The longer technical rule is summarized in <a href="verification.html#human-proxy-composition-card">Verification: Human Proxy Composition Card</a>, <a href="wiki/measurement-stack-and-claim-ceiling.html#human-proxy-composition">Wiki: human-proxy composition rule</a>, and <a href="wiki/human-proxy-composition.html">Wiki: Human Proxy Composition and Route Maturity</a>. That ranking is an inference from the measurement properties summarized above.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box" id="human-proxy-composition-rule">
<strong>2026-03-21 rule: proxy bundles need an explicit composition audit</strong>
<p>
The correction that still remained after the March 2026 ladder update was operational, not conceptual. Readers could now see that <a href="https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1750-23.2024" target="_blank">Johansen et al. (2024)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-66124-w" target="_blank">Lucchetti et al. (2025)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf072" target="_blank">Li et al. (2025)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.29998" target="_blank">Baadsvik et al. (2024)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-02073-3" target="_blank">Hirschler et al. (2025)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-68374-8" target="_blank">Dagum et al. (2026)</a> constrain different state families, but the page still did not say <strong>when several rows may legitimately be promoted together</strong>. The primary literature supports a stricter rule: one row is a cohort-level synaptic-density atlas, one is a five-metabolite similarity graph in 51 adolescents plus 13-site replication, one is a 7 T dynamic kinetic map in five participants, one is a two-volunteer myelin proof-of-principle, one is a 20-person CSF-mobility rest-mapping route whose driver analysis was reported in 11 of 24 total healthy participants, and one is a model-heavy glymphatic clearance study in a randomized crossover trial of 39 participants. Those are not interchangeable pieces of one already-validated whole-brain state meter. The same multimodal literature also shows why agreement across rows is not enough by itself: <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-44363-z" target="_blank">Vafaii et al. (2024)</a> found both common and divergent structure across simultaneous Ca<sup>2+</sup> and BOLD, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-64414-x" target="_blank">Chen et al. (2025)</a> found tightly coupled global progression plus <strong>two distinct network patterns</strong> in simultaneous EEG-PET-MRI, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-01945-y" target="_blank">Bolt et al. (2025)</a> showed that a major global fMRI mode is substantially coupled to autonomic physiology, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-02132-9" target="_blank">Epp et al. (2025)</a> showed that significant task BOLD changes can coexist with opposite oxygen-metabolism changes across many cortical voxels. On this site, a bundle of human proxy rows is promoted only after a <a href="verification.html#human-proxy-composition-card">Human Proxy Composition Card</a> names <strong>(1) the claimed latent variable</strong>, <strong>(2) which stack directly constrains it</strong>, <strong>(3) whether the evidence is same-subject / same-session / same-perturbation or only cross-cohort</strong>, <strong>(4) the quantity type, model burden, and acquisition burden of each row</strong>, <strong>(5) whether the apparent agreement survives a cross-row nuisance / independence audit</strong>, <strong>(6) the external calibration route</strong>, <strong>(7) what the bundle adds beyond the strongest single row under matched conditions</strong>, and <strong>(8) the hidden-state families that still remain latent</strong>. Without that card, the bundle stays at <strong>proxy-rich but ceiling-limited human evidence</strong>. For the longer cross-stack critique, see <a href="wiki/human-proxy-composition.html">Wiki: Human Proxy Composition and Route Maturity</a>.
</p>

<div class="note-box">
<strong>Same-subject is still not same-state when the bridge is sequential</strong>
<p>
One more shortcut still has to be blocked at the entry point. A result can be <strong>same-subject</strong> or <strong>same-brain</strong> and still fail to be one state sample if the bridge runs from live measurement to later fixation / ex vivo follow-up or from one day / regime to another. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crmeth.2023.100520" target="_blank">Lu et al. (2023)</a> showed that preservation route changes extracellular-space retention and geometry, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adk4858" target="_blank">Shapson-Coe et al. (2024)</a> remained a rapidly preserved local human fragment, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08790-w" target="_blank">MICrONS Consortium et al. (2025)</a> remained a sequential local pipeline from in vivo function to ex vivo EM. Therefore, on this site, specimen identity is not allowed to stand in for <strong>state continuity</strong>. If a claim depends on treating a multi-stage bridge as one latent-state sample, it now also needs a <a href="verification.html#state-continuity-bridge-card">Verification: State-Continuity Bridge Card</a> in addition to the <a href="verification.html#human-proxy-composition-card">Human Proxy Composition Card</a> or <a href="verification.html#destructive-structure-card">Destructive-Structure Route Card</a>. The longer bridge-specific background is in <a href="wiki/state-continuity-bridge.html">Wiki: State-Continuity Bridge</a>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Proxy-rich evidence is still not the same as unique internal-state recovery</strong>
<p>
The remaining weak point in this ladder was that several strong rows placed side by side could still sound like a near-complete solution. The primary literature does not support that shortcut. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1155/2019/8497093" target="_blank">Villaverde (2019)</a> distinguished observability from structural identifiability, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1352" target="_blank">Prinz et al. (2004)</a> showed that similar circuit activity can arise from disparate parameters, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1162/netn_a_00354" target="_blank">Rasero et al. (2024)</a> showed that similar human activation patterns can still hide different macroscopic network states, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-02080-4" target="_blank">Beiran &amp; Litwin-Kumar (2025)</a> showed that even connectome-constrained networks remain degenerate until additional activity observations are added. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.csbj.2025.10.058" target="_blank">Liu et al. (2025)</a> then showed that practical identifiability depends on how data are collected, not only on how models are fit. Therefore, on this site, the human observability ladder answers <strong>which variable class becomes more constrained</strong>; it does <strong>not</strong> answer by itself whether the compatible internal-state family has collapsed to one explanation. That second question is handled separately in <a href="verification.html#identifiability-card">Verification: Identifiability Card</a>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box" id="causal-observability-asymmetry">
<strong>The strongest causal papers and the best human routes are not the same ladder</strong>
<p>
A further correction is needed at the entry point. The papers that most strongly show that a maintenance-state family <strong>matters causally</strong> and the papers that most strongly improve <strong>living-human observability</strong> are often not the same papers, not the same species, and not the same spatial unit. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-66975-3" target="_blank">Hadzibegovic et al. (2025)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09774-6" target="_blank">Terceros et al. (2026)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09619-2" target="_blank">Dewa et al. (2025)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-10068-0" target="_blank">Bukalo et al. (2026)</a> strengthen causal relevance in local rodent circuits, whereas the strongest current human routes remain perturbation-conditioned excitability proxies, macro energetic / support proxies, and bounded clearance proxies such as <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brs.2017.11.016" target="_blank">Zrenner et al. (2018)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-02073-3" target="_blank">Hirschler et al. (2025)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-68374-8" target="_blank">Dagum et al. (2026)</a>. Therefore, the primary literature does not license the shortcut from <strong>"causal importance was shown somewhere"</strong> plus <strong>"a human proxy improved somewhere"</strong> to <strong>"the responsible controller is now measured in humans"</strong>.
</p>
</div>

<div class="note-box">
<strong>Even simultaneous multimodal human acquisition still needs a Fusion Card</strong>
<p>
Same-session acquisition narrows one kind of mismatch, but it still does not prove that the modalities share one externally validated biological state variable. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1162/IMAG.a.136" target="_blank">Kothe et al. (2025)</a> showed that synchronization middleware solves stream alignment rather than device-side delay truth, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2020.116595" target="_blank">Wei et al. (2020)</a> showed that EEG-fMRI fusion remains model-conditioned, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-44363-z" target="_blank">Vafaii et al. (2024)</a> plus <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-64414-x" target="_blank">Chen et al. (2025)</a> showed that simultaneous multimodal recordings can reveal both common and divergent structure across modalities. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-01945-y" target="_blank">Bolt et al. (2025)</a> and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-019-0659-0" target="_blank">Özbay et al. (2019)</a> further showed that low-frequency/global fMRI-linked fluctuations can also carry autonomic physiology. Therefore, on this site, even strong same-session multimodal human evidence is still read through a <a href="verification.html#fusion-card">Fusion Card</a> rather than as automatic state completeness.
</p>
</div>

<div class="note-box">
<strong>A shared multimodal factor is not yet one solved state variable</strong>
<p>
This site now makes one more separation explicit at the entry page. A <strong>shared cross-modal component</strong> is a statistical object, not yet a biologically unique one. If a paper reports a coupled low-frequency mode or one common latent factor, the public claim still has to say whether that factor is being read as a <strong>shared neural candidate</strong>, a <strong>modality-specific residual left in the background</strong>, or a <strong>physiology-linked global factor</strong>. That disclosure now lives inside the <a href="verification.html#fusion-card">Fusion Card</a> rather than being left implicit under the word <strong>multimodal</strong>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Nanoscale structure still needs a destructive-route audit</strong>
<p>
At the entry point, it is also too strong to read <strong>nanoscale</strong>, <strong>petascale</strong>, or <strong>same-brain EM</strong> as if the route had already preserved the native live state by default. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crmeth.2023.100520" target="_blank">Lu et al. (2023)</a> showed that fixation route materially changes extracellular-space preservation and that even high-pressure freezing remains thickness-limited, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adk4858" target="_blank">Shapson-Coe et al. (2024)</a> showed that a human nanoscale reconstruction is still a rapidly preserved <strong>local surgical fragment</strong>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08790-w" target="_blank">MICrONS Consortium et al. (2025)</a> showed that same-brain function plus EM is a <strong>sequential local pipeline</strong> rather than simultaneous whole-state capture. Therefore, on this site, destructive ultrastructure is read through a <strong>preservation / registration / proofreading audit</strong>, not from resolution language alone. The longer rule is in <a href="wiki/measurement-stack-and-claim-ceiling.html#destructive-ultrastructure-wall">Wiki: preservation / registration / throughput wall for destructive ultrastructure</a> and <a href="verification.html#destructive-structure-card">Verification: Destructive-Structure Route Card</a>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Entry rule from this ladder</strong>
<p>
Do not collapse <strong>"human evidence exists"</strong> into one sentence. Structural scaffold, synaptic-density PET, receptor / transporter atlas priors, occupancy PET, displacement / release-sensitive PET, perturbation-conditioned sleep-homeostasis / plasticity proxies, state-gated perturbation routes, biochemical organization, energetic imaging, ionic proxies, thermometry, myelin maps, and clearance proxies are not interchangeable. On this site, each row reduces a different latent-state error term, and several of the higher-rung human routes still remain <strong>specialized, model-heavy, or small-cohort</strong>. None of them by itself upgrades WBE to state-complete or field-ready measurement. That last sentence is an inference from the measurement classes summarized above.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Do not promote perturbation-conditioned human routes to direct controller readout</strong>
<p>
Within this ladder, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhs014" target="_blank">Huber et al. (2013)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms12455" target="_blank">Kuhn et al. (2016)</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2026.121723" target="_blank">Fehér et al. (2026)</a>, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brs.2017.11.016" target="_blank">Zrenner et al. (2018)</a> show that wake / sleep / nap history and ongoing EEG-defined state can change plasticity efficacy in humans. That is a real advance, but it still does not directly reveal AIS geometry, channel distribution, or the cell-specific recovery controller that produced the change. On this site, these rows are therefore read as <strong>perturbation-conditioned proxies</strong>, not as direct excitability ground truth.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Do not confuse hidden state with the measurement stack</strong>
<p>
The hidden states listed here are a list of important variables, not a list of variables currently observed. For what EEG, fMRI, spatial transcriptomics, Patch-seq, volume EM, and same-brain connectomics directly observe, and where each one reaches its claim ceiling, see <a href="wiki/measurement-stack-and-claim-ceiling.html">Wiki: Measurement-stack observability and claim ceilings</a>. The purpose is to stop the word “multimodal” from being read as if it already meant state-complete. In the same way, healthy-human <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awab466" target="_blank">MRS thermometry (Rzechorzek et al., 2022)</a> is an important macro thermal route, but it still does not directly observe cell-specific temperature microgradients. Human <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/nbm.3384" target="_blank">31P-MRS (Ren et al., 2015)</a> and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf072" target="_blank">dynamic DMRSI (Li et al., 2025)</a> are important macro energetic routes, but they still do not directly observe branch-local ATP reserve or mitochondrial positioning. Current human in vivo routes likewise do not directly observe isoform choice, m6A-reader engagement, or RNA-editing ratios across the whole living brain; specialized long-read atlas work such as <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-024-01616-4" target="_blank">Joglekar et al. (2024)</a> is informative but still not a whole-brain in vivo human route. Current human in vivo routes likewise do not directly observe phosphosite occupancy, kinase/phosphatase balance, or compartment-specific second-messenger nanodomains across the whole living brain; ex vivo phosphoproteome atlas work such as <a href="https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jproteome.2c00244" target="_blank">Biswas et al. (2023)</a> is informative but still not a comparable in vivo whole-brain human route. Human sodium MRI, from mm-scale 7 T mapping to newer multi-echo single-quantum separation, is an important ionic route, but it still does not directly observe cell-specific chloride concentration, KCC2 / NKCC1 balance, or local inhibitory reversal potential (<a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.23225" target="_blank">Qian et al., 2012</a>; <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-07800-1" target="_blank">Qian et al., 2025</a>). Current human in vivo routes likewise do not directly observe branch- or bouton-specific cargo pausing, motor engagement, or microtubule traffic state. Likewise, receptor / transporter atlas priors, occupancy PET, and displacement / release-sensitive PET constrain selected neuromodulatory systems, but they still do not directly observe the instantaneous whole-brain transmitter field.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>How this hidden-state list becomes an operational rule</strong>
<p>
The March 2026 update added the <a href="verification.html#observability-budget">Observability Budget</a> to Verification in order to convert hidden-state criticism into an operational rule. For several living-human proxy rows used together, the site now also asks for a <a href="verification.html#human-proxy-composition-card">Human Proxy Composition Card</a> so direct observables, same-subject relation, model burden, and residual latent states are not hidden behind the word <strong>multimodal</strong> or the phrase <strong>proxy-rich</strong>. For multimodal or atlas-prior results, the site additionally stacks a <a href="verification.html#fusion-card">Fusion Card</a> so that acquisition relation, synchronization, co-registration, fusion model, shared-vs-specific component disclosure, and abstention boundary are made explicit rather than implied by the word <strong>multimodal</strong>. For destructive ultrastructure claims, the site additionally asks for a <a href="verification.html#destructive-structure-card">Destructive-Structure Route Card</a> so preservation route, registration scope, throughput burden, and omitted live-state families are not hidden behind the words <strong>petascale</strong> or <strong>nanoscale</strong>. From L1 upward, results are expected to report these boundaries together.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>What this means in the introduction</strong>
<p>
The direct takeaway is that <strong>connectome-complete means progress on the structural scaffold</strong>, not that one may already declare <strong>emulation-complete</strong>. That is an inference from the literature above, which leaves maintenance-state variables as a separate problem. The detailed justification is concentrated in <a href="wiki/connectome-is-not-enough.html">Wiki: Why wiring diagrams alone are not enough</a> and <a href="wiki/homeostatic-plasticity-and-maintenance-state.html">Wiki: Homeostatic plasticity and maintenance state</a>.
</p>

(2013)</a> and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1232655" target="_blank">Ravassard et al. (2013)</a> showed that locomotion, optic flow, vestibular, and other sensory cues reshape cortical and hippocampal codes, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2586-16.2016" target="_blank">Zelano et al. (2016)</a> showed that nasal respiration modulates human limbic activity and memory, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abd0380" target="_blank">Flesher et al. (2021)</a> showed that tactile feedback improves a local bidirectional BCI loop. Therefore, Mind-Upload reads a closed-loop result safely only after the paper says which body / environment channels were preserved, substituted, or omitted.
</p>

<section class="section" id="decode-vs-emulate">
<h2 class="section-title">Decode (translation) and emulate (generation) are different</h2>
<p>
Decoding stays at the level of translating observed neural activity into a sentence, label, or other output. WBE would require a system whose internal state evolves autonomously over time, responds to intervention, and can generate future output from that internal dynamics. That gap is why <strong>counterfactuals</strong> and <strong>intervention prediction</strong> matter.
</p>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>The common substitution error</strong>
<p>
If a result is summarized only as “brain to text worked,” what may actually exist is a translator optimized to produce plausible text. To support a claim closer to WBE, the system must be checked under branching conditions: if the condition changes, does the internal behavior still match in the relevant way?
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>When you get stuck at the entrance to causal verification</strong>
<p>
Held-out accuracy, intervention, counterfactual, and perturbation-based verification have different strengths. For a plain-language route into that distinction, see <a href="wiki/counterfactual-and-perturbation-verification.html">Wiki: Counterfactual, intervention, and perturbation verification</a>.
</p>
</div>

<td><strong>Invasive / hybrid neuroprosthesis</strong></td>
<td>Willett et al. (2023) showed a 62 words-per-minute speech BCI with a 125,000-word vocabulary; Flesher et al. (2021) improved robotic grasp time with bidirectional BCI; Berger et al. (2011) reported closed-loop memory-task recovery.</td>
<td>There is evidence approaching <strong>L2-L3</strong> for local circuits: not just decoding, but also causal intervention and task-specific closed-loop improvement.</td>
<td>Multi-region long-term stability, whole-brain coverage, drift management, neuron-by-neuron replacement benchmarks, and a disclosed body / environment boundary are still missing.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Structural connectomics / local calibration / connectome-constrained modeling</strong></td>
<td>Dorkenwald et al. (2024) provided an adult drosophila whole-brain wiring atlas, MICrONS Consortium et al. (2025) linked responses of 75,909 excitatory neurons to ex vivo EM within one mouse visual-cortex volume, and Lappalainen et al. (2024) showed rich fly-visual-system activity prediction only when single-neuron-resolution connectivity and task optimization were used.</td>
<td>Extremely important as a structural atlas, same-brain local scaffold, and task-bounded conditional hypothesis engine for local circuit work.</td>
<td>Edge robustness for weaker connections, cross-modal cell-label robustness, unique internal-state recovery, shared extracellular / electrical state such as electrical synapses or ephaptic field effects, nonlinear chemical synapses, neuromodulation, whole-brain generalization, and maintenance-state completeness are still missing.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Large-scale biophysical simulation</strong></td>
<td>Billeh et al. (2020) built a data-driven multiscale model of awake mouse V1, while Prinz et al. (2004) showed that similar activity can arise from very different parameter sets.</td>
<td>Useful for mechanistic hypothesis testing, local digital twins, and sensitivity analysis of missing measurements.</td>
<td>Parameter identifiability, whole-brain fidelity, OOD generalization, and perturbation matching remain open.</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>What this comparison really shows</strong>
<p>
The strongest support in the current primary literature is for <strong>better decoding</strong>, <strong>closed-loop improvement in local subsystems</strong>, <strong>structure-function mapping</strong>, and <strong>local digital twins</strong>. The key question is therefore not route preference, but <strong>which evidence profile supports which rung of the claim ladder</strong>.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Do not collapse connectome progress into one rung</strong>
<p>
This site now separates at least five kinds of connectome progress: <strong>wiring atlas</strong>, <strong>same-brain local scaffold</strong>, <strong>human macro pathway prior / tractography connectome</strong>, <strong>connectome-constrained conditional predictor</strong>, and <strong>identifiability audit</strong>. Dorkenwald et al. (2024), MICrONS Consortium et al. (2025), tractography-validation papers such as Thomas et al. (2014) and Grisot et al. (2021), and Lappalainen et al. (2024) strengthen different parts of that ladder, while Beiran &amp; Litwin-Kumar (2025) show why connectome alone still does not uniquely fix dynamics. Therefore, “connectome progress” is not one claim level on this site.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>2026-03-21 addendum: flagship connectome papers still solve different problems</strong>
<p>
The current connectome frontier is stronger than older summaries allowed, but its strongest papers still do not support one single inference. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07558-y" target="_blank">Dorkenwald et al. (2024)</a> provide a whole-adult-fly wiring atlas, yet the authors still warn that false-negative and false-positive synapses remain and explicitly threshold connections for downstream analysis. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07686-5" target="_blank">Schlegel et al. (2024)</a> then showed across three fly hemispheres that edges stronger than ten synapses or at least 0.9% of the target cell type's total inputs persist more than 90% of the time, which means weaker edges and fine cell-type claims still need a robustness audit rather than binary trust. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08790-w" target="_blank">MICrONS Consortium et al. (2025)</a> provide a same-brain local structure-function scaffold in one mouse visual-cortex volume, but the EM route still followed in vivo imaging sequentially and remained a local visual-system pipeline. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08805-6" target="_blank">Gamlin et al. (2025)</a> further show that even inside a large EM volume, transcriptomic Sst types were reached by morphology-based cross-modal prediction rather than by direct transcriptomic readout from the connectome itself. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07939-3" target="_blank">Lappalainen et al. (2024)</a> showed that connectome structure plus task optimization can predict rich activity, but models with only cell-type connectivity predicted neural activity poorly. Finally, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-02080-4" target="_blank">Beiran &amp; Litwin-Kumar (2025)</a> showed that recurrent networks sharing the same synaptic weights can still diverge strongly in dynamics when biophysical parameters differ unless additional recordings collapse the solution space. On this site, these are therefore read as <strong>different evidence classes</strong>, not as one monotonic countdown to state-complete WBE.
</p>
</div>
<div class="note-box">
<strong>Connectome-constrained predictors need a route card</strong>
<p>
Even a strong connectome-constrained predictor is still read here first as a <strong>conditional model / hypothesis engine</strong>. The safe reading depends on disclosing <strong>which structural prior was actually used</strong>, <strong>which parameters remained fitted</strong>, <strong>which task/state regime was tested</strong>, <strong>which mechanisms were omitted</strong>, and <strong>where the claim stops</strong>. Without that route card, this site does not promote a good activity-prediction result to unique internal-state recovery. The longer operational rule is in <a href="wiki/connectome-is-not-enough.html#conditional-predictor-route-card">Wiki: conditional-model route card</a> and <a href="verification.html#observability-budget">Verification: Observability Budget</a>.
</p>
</div>
</section>

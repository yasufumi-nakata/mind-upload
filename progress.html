Connectome coverage and reconstruction accuracy

StudyScope and resultBoundary of the interpretation
Cook et al., 2019Whole-animal nervous-system connectomes of hermaphrodite and male C. elegans.[1]Composite reconstructions combine observations from several animals and some extrapolation, rather than exhaustively measuring one individual.
Winding et al., 2023The brain of one female first-instar fly larva: 3,016 neurons.[2]The count includes inputs to the brain. It does not cover the complete ventral nerve cord or the adult nervous system.
FlyWire, 2024One adult female fly brain: 139,255 neurons and about 54.5 million chemical synapses between reconstructed neurons.[3]At a threshold of at least five synapses per directed neuron pair, the graph has 2,700,513 connections among 134,181 neurons. Synapse counts and edge counts differ. The ventral nerve cord, electrical synapses and learning history are outside this reconstruction.
H01, 2024About 1 mm³ of surgically resected human temporal cortex: 57,180 cells, including 16,087 neurons and 32,315 glia.[4]Local tissue from one patient, with largely automated reconstruction and neurites truncated at volume boundaries.
MICrONS, 2025Activity and electron microscopy (EM) in one mouse's visual cortex. The estimated 75,909 unique recorded neurons are distinct from the 15,439 neurons manually matched to EM.[5]The activity count combines several scans; it is neither a simultaneous recording count nor a count of completed structural matches. Proofreading also has restricted coverage.
BANC / MaleCNS, 2026Independent female and male adult central nervous-system resources linking brain and ventral nerve cord.[6][7]Imaging coverage, residual fragments, synapse detection and proofreading differ. Neither measures all individual states or memories.

Distinguish cells from neurons, synapse detection from identifying connected partners, and imaging from manual proofreading. Check dataset version, missing regions and reconstruction errors before using a connectome as model input.

Predicting function from connectivity

Shiu et al. (2024) used fly-brain connectivity in a leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) model. Predictions of taste-triggered feeding initiation and antennal grooming were compared with specific activation and inhibition experiments. Brain-scale wiring and validation of every function remain distinct.[8]

Lappalainen et al. (2024) used a consensus connectome assembled from local visual-system reconstructions. Synapse counts and transmission signs constrain a model optimized for optic-flow estimation. They then compared its neural responses with 26 existing studies. Neural activity was not used as a training target, but task information was. This representation differs from Shiu’s FlyWire neuron-specific model.[9]

Currier and Clandinin (2025) compared measured fly optic-lobe responses with connectivity-based predictions. Relatively strong inputs carried a large share of predictive information, while connectivity similarity did not adequately explain detailed response properties. Removing inputs was a computational analysis, not evidence that weak biological connections can safely be discarded.[10]

A mouse visual-cortex foundation model improved predictions for new stimulus types after pretraining on several animals and adapting to recordings from a new individual. This is distinct from reconstruction using a connectome. ZAPBench compares predictions of brain-wide calcium activity from one larval zebrafish using held-out times and stimulus conditions. The models evaluated in the paper do not use a connectome, and the test does not establish cross-individual generalization. The 2025 paper reports completed EM imaging of the same animal used for activity recording, with connectome reconstruction and manual proofreading in progress. Completed correspondence would support testing with paired structure and activity from one individual; the paper does not establish a completed connectome or individual-specific functional reproduction. These different evaluation targets help define complementary experiments.[11][12]

Results and limits of models interacting with bodies and environments

BAAIWorm couples 136 C. elegans neurons with a body and environment to study zigzag movement toward an attractant. It does not cover all 302 neurons or all behaviours. Connections are optimized against activity correlations, with simplified sensory input and a learned neural-to-muscle readout. It is a step toward evaluating the sensation–action feedback loop.[14]

Eon Systems' 2026 fly demonstration integrates existing brain, vision, and body models. Its technical description identifies manually selected descending-neuron mappings, imitation-learned controllers, and missing validation of internal dynamics. Carboncopies also questions whether the evidence warrants the term “upload.” This guide treats the result as embodied model integration, distinct from recovery of an individual's memories and complete internal state.[15][16]

Neural-interface findings and scope

Neuroprosthetics derive useful outputs from local neural signals and individual training. Speech systems illustrate why decoding text and generating streaming audio need different evaluation measures.

StudyDemonstrated capabilityConditions
Willett et al., 2023Real-time, large-vocabulary text decoding from attempted speech.[17]One participant with ALS; held-out prompted sentences, with daily recordings and calibration.
Card et al., 2024Brief initial calibration followed by continued training and conversational use.[18]One participant with ALS. The first day's 30-minute calibration used a 50-word vocabulary; large-vocabulary use required further training.
Littlejohn et al., 2025Streaming speech and text from attempted speech.[19]Online demonstration in one participant after brainstem stroke. The 80 ms value is an update interval; median audio-onset latency for general sentences was 1.12 seconds.

The whole-brain emulation (WBE)-relevant lessons concern long-term signal stability, individual adaptation and closed-loop output evaluation. These systems decode local activity associated with attempted speech, rather than reading an entire brain or arbitrary memories.

A hippocampal prosthesis modelled each person's CA3-to-CA1 activity and delivered stimulation in eight participants; seven contributed to the short-term memory task and six to delayed recognition. Comparisons with no stimulation and random stimulation provide an example of testing model-based interventions. Memories were not transferred to another person or machine.[20]

Publication and data status in 2026

The peer-reviewed BANC paper, the MaleCNS paper and dataset, and Mohinta et al.'s October 2026 draft larval fly CNS resource have different publication and reconstruction status. The last is a preprint providing 8 nm isotropic imaging and automated reconstruction of a first-instar CNS. Most reconstruction remains unproofread; 24 ventral nerve cord neurons were completely proofread. Treat it as a resource for testing reconstruction and proofreading, not a completed connectome.[6][7][21][22]

Publication and dataset status in this section were checked on 4 November 2026. Record preprint version, formal publication date and dataset updates separately.

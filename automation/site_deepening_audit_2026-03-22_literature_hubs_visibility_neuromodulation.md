## Why this topic was selected

- The core public pages and wiki already tightened two important scientific rules on 2026-03-22:
  - `field-formation visibility wall` for EEG / MEG source-reading
  - `neuromodulatory route-card` for transmitter-related claims
- However, the two literature hubs were still lagging behind that update.
- This left a real reading gap. A technically trained reader could enter through the literature map or the paper archive and still overread:
  - `direct source validation` as if it bypassed upstream visibility limits
  - `neuromodulatory evidence` as if pupil-linked covariation, local transmitter sensing, receptor atlases, occupancy PET, and challenge-linked displacement PET all constrained one common state object

## Evidence-backed critique

### 1. Source-imaging reading is still unsafe if field formation is left implicit

- Problem:
  - The literature hubs already separated inverse methods, direct validation, and human observability at a high level, but they still did not foreground the more upstream question:
    - `what source classes can reach the sensors at all?`
- Evidence:
  - Ahlfors et al. (2010, *Brain Topography*) quantified source-orientation sensitivity and showed that detectability differs sharply across EEG and MEG depending on geometry.
  - Ahlfors et al. (2010, *Human Brain Mapping*) showed that extended / distributed sources can cancel at the surface.
  - Goldenholz et al. (2009) showed that source extent and anatomy materially change cortical source SNR.
  - Piastra et al. (2021) showed that head-model detail, including the CSF compartment, changes EEG / MEG sensitivity estimates.
- Critique:
  - Therefore, `validated ESI` is still downstream of `field formation`.
  - If a literature hub lists source-imaging anchors without forcing a visibility check, readers can still slide from `benchmarkable localization under some source regimes` to `general hidden-state recoverability`.

### 2. Neuromodulatory papers do not share one inferential object

- Problem:
  - The literature hubs already treated hidden-state and human-observability papers more carefully than before, but they still did not expose neuromodulatory evidence as a distinct route-family split.
- Evidence:
  - Reimer et al. (2016) showed that pupil fluctuations track both adrenergic and cholinergic cortical activity, so the route is a mixed arousal proxy rather than a transmitter-specific meter.
  - Lohani et al. (2022) and Neyhart et al. (2024) showed that local cortical acetylcholine signals are spatially heterogeneous and shaped by both axonal activity and local clearance.
  - Hansen et al. (2022) and Goulas et al. (2021) provided human receptor / transporter atlas structure, which is a regional prior rather than momentary occupancy or release.
  - Wong et al. (2013) measured exogenous dopamine D2 occupancy, while Koepp et al. (1998) and Erritzoe et al. (2020) addressed challenge-linked endogenous release with different ligands and model burdens.
- Critique:
  - These papers do not constrain one common neuromodulatory object.
  - If a literature hub does not separate them explicitly, `human neuromodulatory measurement` can still be misread as a single rising ladder toward present whole-brain transmitter-state readout.

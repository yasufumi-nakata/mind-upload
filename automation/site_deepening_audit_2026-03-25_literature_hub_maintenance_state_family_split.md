# Site Deepening Audit: Literature Hub Maintenance-State Family Split

- Date: 2026-03-25
- Main targets: `mind_uploading_papers.md`, `research_harvest_50.md`
- Scope: technical / natural science only

## Why this was the next page family to deepen

The public entry pages and verification rules had already become much stricter about hidden-state families. They now distinguish:

- intrinsic-excitability / allocation control
- ionic / chloride regulation
- transcriptional stabilization
- phospho-signaling
- local proteostasis
- cargo-routing
- bioenergetic / mitochondrial support
- clearance / immune support
- astrocyte-state

However, the literature hubs still lagged behind that taxonomy.

- `mind_uploading_papers.md` still concentrated its maintenance-state fast lane around transcriptional and astrocyte papers.
- `research_harvest_50.md` still described the U3 literature route mainly through the older transcriptional / phospho / proteostasis / cargo / astrocyte framing.

That mismatch created a real scientific weakness for technical readers:

- the front door said `maintenance-state is a family split`
- the literature hubs still made it easy to read that split as `mostly gene / glia papers plus generic support background`

## Primary-literature basis for the criticism

### 1. Maintenance-state is not one support variable

- [Hadzibegovic et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-66975-3) shows that early intrinsic-excitability plasticity in neocortical engram neurons changes memory formation and precision.
- [Alfonsa et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2025.01.050) shows that sleep-wake-related intracellular chloride shifts regulate glutamatergic cortical plasticity.
- [Terceros et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09774-6) shows time-dependent thalamocortical transcriptional gates for memory stabilization.
- [Vishwanath et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42255-026-01451-w) shows that mitochondrial Ca2+ efflux controls neuronal metabolism and long-term memory across species.
- [Kim et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2025.02.022) shows that the meningeal-lymphatics-microglia axis regulates synaptic physiology.
- [Dewa et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09619-2) and [Bukalo et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-10068-0) show distinct astrocyte-state routes for multiday stabilization and fear-related neural representations.

Criticism:

- These papers do not refine one common latent variable.
- They constrain different controller families.
- A literature hub that compresses them back into one maintenance bucket weakens the site's own reading rule.

### 2. The literature hubs were narrower than the current public stop lines

The current site already has route cards and verification rules for:

- intrinsic excitability
- ionic / chloride
- bioenergetic / mitochondrial state
- clearance / immune support

But the literature-front-door text did not surface those families with equal clarity.

Criticism:

- This creates a front-door inconsistency.
- A technical reader can still leave the literature hubs with the impression that the important new maintenance-side papers are mainly about transcription or astrocytes.
- That is weaker than the actual evidence organization already implemented elsewhere on the site.

### 3. This is a technical reading failure, not a philosophical one

The issue here is not personhood, law, or policy.
It is a purely scientific reading problem:

- what physiological family is being constrained,
- what variable is directly observed or perturbed,
- and what remains outside connectome-only reading.

If the literature hubs do not preserve that split, the reader can silently re-collapse several controller families into a single vague phrase such as `support variables`.

## What I changed

### 1. `mind_uploading_papers.md`

- Expanded the maintenance-state framing in front matter from a transcription / astrocyte-heavy subset to a broader family split.
- Rewrote the maintenance-side correction in the fast-lane introduction so the route now names intrinsic-excitability, ionic / chloride, bioenergetic / mitochondrial, clearance / immune, transcriptional, and astrocyte families explicitly.
- Replaced the old `maintenance-state / mechanistic boundary` row with a broader family-split anchor set.
- Rewrote the maintenance-state note-box to block the shortcut from `many support-related papers` to `one support variable`.
- Renamed the shortlist row from `Maintenance-state boundary papers` to `Maintenance-state family split`.

### 2. `research_harvest_50.md`

- Expanded the front-matter highlights and known-points to include intrinsic-excitability, ionic / chloride, bioenergetic / mitochondrial, and clearance / immune families.
- Broadened the main priority-route paragraph so the evidence list now reflects the larger family split.
- Renamed route 6 from `maintenance-state` to `maintenance-state family split`.
- Expanded the `what to verify first` cell so readers must now audit a wider family set inside U3.
- Rewrote the maintenance-state note-box so the literature map no longer collapses recent maintenance papers back into transcription / astrocyte plus generic background.

## Resulting site-level rule

The literature hubs now match the stricter public rule:

1. Do not read maintenance-state as one support variable.
2. Ask which hidden-state family the paper actually constrains.
3. Keep family-split evidence separate from direct human observability.
4. Demote any argument that recombines these papers into one generic progress bar.

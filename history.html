Keep historical proposals in their context

Joseph Strout's Mind Uploading Home Page is an early web resource organizing scanning, computation, and replacement proposals. It displays a 2006 update date. It is useful historical context, but its resolution, cost, computing, and feasibility assumptions are not current evidence.[1]

Sandberg and Bostrom's 2008 technical roadmap organized preservation, scanning, interpretation, and simulation as dependencies. Its framework remains a reference point; quantitative estimates need reassessment against current measurements and methods.[2]

A sequence of technical advances

  1. 1952

    Quantifying electrical dynamics

    The Hodgkin–Huxley model provides a foundation for membrane-current modelling. It also illustrates the distance between modelling a cellular process and reproducing an individual organism.[3]

  2. 2008–2019

    Roadmaps and small nervous systems

    WBE requirements were organized, while whole-animal connectomes and integrated-model research expanded.[2][4][5]

  3. 2023–2024

    Reaching fly-brain scale

    Larval and adult brain connectomes advanced alongside connectome-constrained functional prediction.[6][7][8]

  4. 2025

    Measuring and benchmarking correspondence

    MICrONS, LICONN, ZAPBench, and fidelity research sharpened measurement and validation questions.[9][10][11][12]

  5. 2026

    Linking brains with bodies

    Brain-and-cord datasets, embodied models, and functional-validation debates progressed. They do not establish human uploading.[13][14][15][16]

Use reviews as entry points

The State of Brain Emulation Report 2025 surveys enabling technologies. The archived arXiv v3 is the 178-page version dated 5 November 2025, distinct from the 182-page final report released in 2026. Follow consequential claims back to the underlying studies and check their experimental conditions.[17][18]

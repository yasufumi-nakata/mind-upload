What must we measure to reproduce a brain?

Connectivity, cellular responses, plasticity, molecular state, and interaction with a body constrain different aspects of a model.

Separate structure, state, and update rules

A conceptual model is x(t+Δt)=F(x(t),u(t);θ). Here x includes voltages and adaptation states, u denotes sensory input, and θ includes connectivity and transmission parameters. Learning can change θ itself. Separating wiring from dynamics makes missing measurements explicit.[1][2]

A chemical-synapse map does not automatically specify electrical coupling, receptors, neuromodulation, or all intracellular and glial effects. Whether a detail can be omitted depends on the target function and validation evidence.[3][4]

Combine complementary measurements

MeasurementMain informationWhat remains missing
Electron microscopy (EM)Ultrastructure and synaptic connectivityFixed images do not directly record the living brain's activity history.
Expansion microscopy and molecular labelsMorphology and locations of labelled moleculesMissing labels, deformation, registration, and imaging volume need evaluation.
Electrophysiology and calcium imagingTemporal responses and population activityThese do not necessarily observe every state of every cell simultaneously.
Perturbation and behaviourIntervention responses and input–output relationshipsDifferent internal mechanisms can produce similar behaviour.

MICrONS demonstrates structure–function registration; LICONN combines dense optical reconstruction with molecular information. A modality comparison needs volume, throughput, missing data, cost, and reconstruction error as well as resolution.[5][6][7]

The inverse problem from data to model

Observed data may not identify a unique parameter set. Different cellular and synaptic configurations can generate similar activity. Predictive accuracy and recovery of the original mechanism therefore need separate evaluation.[8][9]

Connectivity, task constraints, recordings, and interventions can narrow the possibilities. Evaluating only on fitted conditions can still miss incorrect responses to new stimuli or lesions. Reserve these conditions before fitting, as described in validation design.[10][9]

What would demonstrate retained memory?

Engram research investigates ensembles and synapses altered by learning. Finding a memory-associated structure does not establish a procedure for reading arbitrary personal experiences. A survey about candidate molecular and structural substrates measures researchers' views, not the success of a memory-extraction technology.[11][12]

An experimental design should pair pre/post-learning measurements, recall in the same individual, retention intervals, untrained tasks, and intervention effects. Training a model on the desired answers must be distinguished from recovering the original memory.

Preservation is one stage in a longer chain

Aldehyde-stabilized cryopreservation (ASC) research evaluates preserved neural ultrastructure. Observable structure, retention of functionally necessary information, and successful readout into a model require separate tests.[13][14]

The WBE research question is what changes during preservation and how those errors affect model predictions. The availability of a preservation service or a well-preserved EM image is not evidence of successful reconstruction of function.

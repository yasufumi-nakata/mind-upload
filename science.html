What must we measure to reproduce a brain?

Connectivity, cellular responses, plasticity, molecular state, and interaction with a body constrain different aspects of a model.

Terms that should remain distinct

Connectome
Connectivity data within a specified boundary. Report cell coverage, regions, and chemical or electrical synapse coverage.
Simulation and emulation
Simulation computes an assumed model. Emulation aims at functional correspondence to a specified target system.
System identification and inverse problem
Inferring model form or parameters from observed inputs and outputs. It is an inverse problem because the causes of observations must be inferred.
Identifiability
The ability to distinguish different models using available observations.
Fidelity
Correspondence to the original system for explicitly defined functions and conditions.
Closed loop
Sensation changes action, which changes subsequent sensation.
Calcium activity
An indirect observation of activity through intracellular calcium changes, distinct from directly measuring voltage or every spike.
Engram
A concept for studying physical changes and cell ensembles involved in memory retention and recall.
Preprint
A manuscript released before formal peer-reviewed publication; identify its version and date.

Separate structure, state, and update rules

The following is this site's conceptual notation, not an equation reproduced from a particular paper or a completed brain model.

x(t+Δt) = F(x(t), u(t); θ(t))
θ(t+Δt) = G(θ(t), x(t), u(t))

x denotes fast states such as voltage and adaptation; u, inputs; θ, connection strengths and transmission properties; F, responses; and G, learning and plasticity. The distinction between state and parameter depends on timescale. Model form itself, such as point neurons versus multicompartment cells, also requires selection and validation. Quantitative membrane-current models and input–output system identification provide foundations.[1][2]

Receptors and neuromodulation can change responses beyond what a wiring diagram specifies. To choose necessary detail, test whether restoring an omitted mechanism improves predictions on independent evaluation conditions.[3][4]

Combine complementary measurements

MeasurementMain informationWhat it does not supply alone
Electron microscopyFine morphology and synaptic connectivityFixed images do not directly reveal the previous activity history.
Expansion microscopy and molecular labelsMorphology and locations of labelled moleculesLabel loss, distortion, registration and imaging volume require evaluation.
Electrophysiology and calcium imagingTemporal changes, stimulus responses and population activityNot every state of every cell is necessarily observed simultaneously.
Perturbation and behaviourIntervention responses and input–output relationsDifferent mechanisms may produce similar behaviour.

MICrONS provides paired structure and activity in the same animal. LICONN combines roughly sixteenfold tissue expansion, dense optical reconstruction and molecular labels. Its largest imaged native tissue volume was approximately one million µm³ (0.001 mm³), rather than a whole brain. Some antibodies and conditions do not survive the workflow well, so molecular targets require individual validation.[5][6]

Compare resolution, volume, speed, missing data, cost and reconstruction errors together. Imaging-time estimates are not end-to-end estimates for preservation, reconstruction and functional validation.[7]

The inverse problem from data to model

Circuits with different parameter settings can produce similar activity. This degeneracy means that fitting observations need not yield a unique answer.[8]

Han and colleagues tested artificial vision models with known ground truth and found conditions in which activity-prediction scores did not select the correct model architecture. Identifiability also changed with unit coverage and stimulus choice. The lesson is to design observations that discriminate candidates, rather than to conclude that identification is impossible.[9]

As a research proposal, a set of models functionally equivalent over specified interventions can be a provisional target. State the tested conditions and do not assume equivalence outside them. Combine wiring, activity, task constraints and interventions to narrow the candidates.[10][9]

What theory can establish: NETT analyses existence, stability and convergence for inverse problems with learned regularization under assumptions including continuity and coercivity. Its numerical examples reconstruct photoacoustic images. Such guarantees help design constrained inference, but do not automatically guarantee unique recovery of a brain's true state.[11]

What would demonstrate retained memory?

Uytiepo et al. (2025) labelled cells active during fear learning in mice and examined local hippocampal synaptic structure one week later. The main EM comparison used three conditioned and two control animals, finding differences including terminals contacting multiple spines. The result was not simply preferential wiring among co-labelled cells. This detects learning-associated structural differences; it does not decode arbitrary memory content.[12]

A researcher survey also found varied views on the structural scale and information needed for memory recovery. Its broad definition of static structure includes individual biomolecules and assumes successful, ideal preservation. Response percentages are neither measured recovery probabilities nor proportions of memories preserved.[13]

The next experiments should link learning, measurement and held-out recall in the same individual, examining retention, forgetting, relearning and intervention responses. Keep test answers out of model fitting, so the experiment can assess whether retained information, rather than additional answer training, supports recall.

Preservation is one stage in a longer chain

Aldehyde-stabilized cryopreservation (ASC) preserves the ultrastructure of chemically fixed tissue. The 2015 study examined, among other preparations, eight rabbits under the final protocol and one successfully processed pig. Images showed traceable neurites and synapses; restoration of physiological function or learned memories was not evaluated.[14]

The next experimental task is to measure what preservation changes and how those losses affect functional prediction. Retaining ultrastructure, retaining necessary states, reading out information and validating function are separate milestones.[15]

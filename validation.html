Testing whether an emulation is correct

Define success first, then evaluate on held-out conditions and interventions. Similar-looking behaviour alone does not identify the internal mechanism.

Use more than one score

These are proposed research-design checks, not an established certification standard. Define fidelity for the target function and distinguish predictive agreement from identification of mechanisms.[1][2]

TargetQuestionExample evaluation
StructureDo cells, connections and types correspond?Missing or false edges, split/merge errors and downstream effects.
ActivityDo responses match for unseen stimuli?Time series, state transitions, timescales and a repeat-measurement noise ceiling.
Causal responseDoes the same intervention produce the same change?Stimulation, suppression and lesion conditions excluded from fitting.
Individual specificityIs the original individual predicted better than other-individual or average models?Matched registration and computational budgets; separate individual and trial variation.
Learning and memoryIs information acquired by that individual retained?Untrained and differently trained individuals, retention, relearning and forgetting.
EmbodimentDoes correspondence survive a sensorimotor loop?Fixed body controllers; varied environments and delays.

Test correspondence to that individual

Evaluate a model derived from individual A on A's held-out trials. Apply the same evaluation to models derived from other individuals, cell-type-average wiring, and activity-only models. Match regional and cell-type correspondence, training data and computational budgets.

An advantage for A's model is not sufficient evidence of memory reconstruction. Tests of learned content must keep the answers out of additional model training. Estimate a noise ceiling from repeated trials and establish that the relevant individual differences are measurable. Generalising to unseen individuals and specifically reproducing a known individual are different experiments.

This comparison is proposed by this site; the cited literature does not demonstrate the entire procedure.

Practical validation starting points

ZAPBench benchmarks brain-wide activity prediction using recordings of over 70,000 neurons in a larval zebrafish. It measures activity prediction under recorded conditions, not complete structural recovery or memory transfer.[3]

The Brain Emulation Challenge uses synthetic ground truth to examine measurement and reconstruction choices. MICrONS supports biological structure–function comparisons. These resources address different questions and are complementary rather than interchangeable.[4][5]

A protocol for interpretable comparison

  1. Fix the target: individual, region, function, timescale and available observations.
  2. Fix the split: prevent leakage between nearby time points and reserve unseen stimuli, sessions and interventions. Use a separate split for across-individual generalisation.
  3. Fix controls: simple, shuffled-connectivity, average and activity-only models.
  4. Fix the decision rule: preregister the primary metric, minimum meaningful difference δ, equivalence tolerance ε and sample size. Derive δ and ε from repeat biological measurements or the research objective, not from the final result.
  5. Report uncertainty: distinguish trial, individual and random-seed variation; report intervals and computational costs.
  6. Retain failures: disclose unreproduced conditions, additional fitting and data/code versions.

No universal numerical δ or ε has been established for WBE. Without biological repeat measurements, report an exploratory model comparison rather than a pass for individual emulation.

Failures also move the research forward

Failure conditions help choose the next measurement. Compare adding information about transmission strength, receptors, adaptation or neuromodulation with removing corresponding components from an existing model. Test selected combinations so that conclusions do not merely reflect the order of additions.

Prioritise an added measurement when its benefit persists on independent conditions and justifies its cost. Otherwise revisit measurement quality, model form and the sensitivity of the evaluation. This is the iterative procedure proposed by this site.

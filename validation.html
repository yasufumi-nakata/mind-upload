Testing whether an emulation is correct

Define success first, then evaluate on held-out conditions and interventions. Similar-looking behaviour alone does not identify the internal mechanism.

Use more than one score

These are proposed research-design checks, not an established certification standard. Define fidelity for the target function and distinguish predictive agreement from identification of mechanisms.[1][2]

TargetQuestionExample evaluation
StructureDo cells, connections and types correspond?Missing or false edges, split/merge errors and downstream effects.
ActivityDo responses match for unseen stimuli?Time series, state transitions, timescales and a repeat-measurement noise ceiling.
Causal responseDoes the same intervention produce the same change?Stimulation, suppression and lesion conditions excluded from fitting.
Individual specificityIs the original individual predicted better than other-individual or average models?Matched registration and computational budgets; separate individual and trial variation.
Learning and memoryIs information acquired by that individual retained?Untrained and differently trained individuals, retention, relearning and forgetting.
EmbodimentDoes correspondence survive a sensorimotor loop?Fixed body controllers; varied environments and delays.

Human comparison data and the limits of inference

When whole-brain acquisition after death is envisaged, physiological recordings cannot subsequently be repeated in the same person. The following is this site's proposed design. Prior recordings are not whole-brain ground truth: compare model outputs at the level of the observables actually measured. No universal required duration, repeat count or coverage is established. Use pilot repeated recordings to estimate reliability and individual variation, then plan individuals and sessions that can resolve the prespecified δ and ε.

TargetExamples of reference data to secureLimit and claim withheld
StructureSame-person brain images, independent checks on sampled acquired tissue, and registration errors.Agreement in coarse images does not certify every synapse or molecular state.
ActivityRepeated sensory/cognitive tasks with EEG/MEG or fMRI; the available scope of recordings made for clinical purposes.Map simulations to the same measured observables. Matching population signals does not establish matching activity in every cell.
Causal responseResponses to controlled inputs and stimulation/clinical-intervention records available under appropriate review and consent.These do not reproduce the animal suppression/lesion intervention set. Observational associations do not identify internal causal mechanisms.
Individual specificityMultiple repeat sessions and comparable data from multiple individuals.Compare at regional, cell-type or population levels. Do not infer unmeasured differences or general identification performance from one case.
Learning and memoryDocumented learning content/timing plus independent delayed recall, forgetting and relearning tests.Withhold test answers from fitting. Success on selected tasks does not establish recovery of all lifetime memories.
Body and environmentInput/output records from tasks varying feedback, delay and environment.Match input/output conditions. Limited simulated-body agreement does not reproduce the whole original body.

Keep acquisition conditions distinct. H01 used local surgically resected tissue; central ASC demonstrations used conditions including animal perfusion fixation. Neither directly establishes post-mortem human whole-brain applicability or retention of memory-relevant information. A human plan must record acquisition delay, terminal condition and fixation, and specify tested structural/molecular losses.[3][4]

Research review, consent and sample-provision arrangements are included here as prerequisites for executable data collection, without extending the guide into institutional ethics. Include their lead times in the 2026–2035 recording design. An unavailable comparison remains untested at the 2041–2050 gates; it is not silently waived.

Test correspondence to that individual

Evaluate a model derived from individual A on A's held-out trials. Apply the same evaluation to models derived from other individuals, cell-type-average wiring, and activity-only models. Match regional and cell-type correspondence, training data and computational budgets.

An advantage for A's model is not sufficient evidence of memory reconstruction. Tests of learned content must keep the answers out of additional model training. Estimate a noise ceiling from repeated trials and establish that the relevant individual differences are measurable. Generalising to unseen individuals and specifically reproducing a known individual are different experiments.

Evidence for individual variation: Schlegel and colleagues compared FlyWire and hemibrain across two individuals and three hemispheres. Many strong connections were conserved, while cell counts and connection weights varied; coverage, cell-type matching and reconstruction errors also contributed. Three hemispheres are not three individuals, and structural differences do not directly define a functional tolerance.[5]

An identification matrix across individuals: our proposed design requires multiple individuals with paired structural and functional evaluation data. Rows identify the individual from which a model was derived; columns identify the individual being predicted. Measure each own-model advantage over other-individual models on held-out data. Fix cell correspondence and model selection independently of the evaluation outcomes. Match sex and developmental stage, and permute individual labels only within strata where exchangeability is defensible. Too few independent individuals for informative permutations, or inadequate precision in measuring individual differences, makes the result inconclusive. The statistical procedure specifies the test and sample-size design.

This comparison is proposed by this site; the cited literature does not demonstrate the entire procedure.

Practical validation starting points

ZAPBench benchmarks brain-wide activity prediction using recordings of over 70,000 neurons in a larval zebrafish. It measures activity prediction under recorded conditions, not complete structural recovery or memory transfer.[6]

The Brain Emulation Challenge uses synthetic ground truth to examine measurement and reconstruction choices. MICrONS supports biological structure–function comparisons. These resources address different questions and are complementary rather than interchangeable.[7][8]

A protocol for interpretable comparison

The following is this site's proposed validation plan, combining methods from the literature. The cited papers do not report completion of this entire procedure.

  1. Fix the target, controls and registration fields. Specify individual, region, function, timescale, available observations, primary metric and independent experimental unit. Compare simple, shuffled-connectivity, average and activity-only models under matched training and compute budgets. An OSF or similar preregistration should record hypotheses, correspondence, splits, preprocessing, exclusions and missing-data handling, δ and ε with their rationale, tests, multiplicity family, sample-size and stopping rules, seed counts, and data/code versions. For existing data, disclose results and analyses already examined.
  2. Specify decisions for δ and ε. δ is the minimum meaningful improvement; ε is the tolerated difference for equivalence. Set them before inspecting outcomes, using task relevance and independent repeat measurements. For a higher-is-better metric, assess superiority by whether the one-sided 95% confidence lower bound for the difference exceeds δ. Use TOST, two one-sided tests, for equivalence: for one primary endpoint at α=.05 per one-sided test, the corresponding 90% confidence interval must lie within (−ε,+ε). A nonsignificant conventional difference test does not establish equivalence. Apply step 7 when making multiple decisions.[9]
  3. Check the noise ceiling and measurement precision. Repeated trials under matched conditions can separate signal variance from trial variability and estimate CCmax and normalized correlation CCnorm.[10] For repeated responses to independent stimulus conditions, another option is approximately unbiased, noise-corrected r²_ER with the authors' ECCI interval. Check its fixed-stimulus, independence and variance-stabilization assumptions and report SNR. Negative estimated signal variance, or an interval covering all of [0,1], can make performance inconclusive. Do not apply the estimator unchanged to correlated adjacent time bins; separately validate a method that handles temporal dependence. Also report absolute error in the original units and mean bias.[11]
  4. Block the split for the prediction target. Reserve contiguous time blocks for future prediction in the same individual, whole sessions for new-session prediction, and whole individuals for generalization to unseen individuals. Also reserve novel stimuli and interventions in advance. Fit preprocessing and select models within training data; set gaps against nearby-time leakage from training-data autocorrelation or comparable evidence. When blocking changes the condition distribution, distinguish interpolation from extrapolation. A mixed model alone does not make the split independent.[12]
  5. Separate hierarchical and random-seed variation. One proposed analysis uses model condition as a fixed effect and individual and sessions nested within individual as random effects, while explicitly modelling trial residual correlation and variation across model seeds. Choose the distribution, link and interval method for the metric, checking coverage with pilot data or simulations. Do not count neurons, trials or seeds as independent individuals. This particular hierarchical analysis is our proposal.
  6. Test an individual-identification matrix. For a higher-is-better score S, form S_ij for model i predicting target individual j. One statistic averages, across targets, the difference between the own-model score S_jj and the mean other-model score. Prespecify score direction, weighting and missing-data handling. Permute model-to-target identity labels only among individuals exchangeable under the null, preserving the dependence among each individual's repeats, conditions and seeds. Confounding of identity with sex or stage, or too few possible permutations, makes the test inconclusive. Significance of identification and an effect exceeding δ require separate checks.
  7. Control multiple comparisons. Prespecify the family of superiority, equivalence and identification claims across primary functions and tasks. This proposal uses Holm correction to control family-wise error at .05. For equivalence, use the larger of the two one-sided p-values as the p-value for one TOST before correction. An unadjusted 90% interval alone is not evidence of passing a multiplicity-adjusted criterion. Report additional exploratory tasks separately and do not substitute them for the prespecified decisions.
  8. Design individual and repeat counts. Estimate signal, trial, session and individual variance, autocorrelation and missingness from independent pilot data or comparable evidence. For the proposed δ and ε, simulate several true-effect and noise scenarios through the complete planned analysis, including splitting, TOST, superiority tests, permutation and multiplicity correction. Choose independent-individual, session and repeat counts to meet, for example, 80–90% power and the required interval width; report sensitivity to assumptions. More repeats do not replace missing independent individuals. SNR-based stimulus/repeat calculations can inform experiments with independent stimulus responses, but power to detect stimulus tuning is distinct from power for individual identification or equivalence.[9][11]

No universal numerical δ or ε has been established for WBE. Without biological repeat measurements, report exploratory model comparison rather than a pass for individual emulation. Retain intervals, compute costs, unreproduced conditions, additional fitting, deviations from the plan and data/code versions alongside negative results.

Failures also move the research forward

Failure conditions help choose the next measurement. Compare adding information about transmission strength, receptors, adaptation or neuromodulation with removing corresponding components from an existing model. Test selected combinations so that conclusions do not merely reflect the order of additions.

Prioritise an added measurement when its benefit persists on independent conditions and justifies its cost. Otherwise revisit measurement quality, model form and the sensitivity of the evaluation. This is the iterative procedure proposed by this site.

Concrete additions and omissions: Compare a wiring-only model with versions adding receptor/peptide information and measurable glial states or modulation. Match training data, tuning attempts and compute budgets so that extra flexibility alone does not confer an advantage. Include shuffled molecular labels and removal of each added mechanism. Evaluate held-out stimuli, recall, interventions and timescales, reporting uncertainty and added costs. This is a proposed design motivated by the biological findings below, not a completed whole-brain experiment.[13][14]

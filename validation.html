Testing whether an emulation is correct

Define success first, then evaluate on held-out conditions and interventions. Similar-looking behaviour alone does not identify the internal mechanism.

Whole-brain emulation evaluation criteria

These are proposed research-design checks, not an established certification standard. Define fidelity for the target function and distinguish predictive agreement from identification of mechanisms.[1][2]

TargetQuestionExample evaluation
StructureDo cells, connections and types correspond?Missing or false edges, split/merge errors and downstream effects.
ActivityDo responses match for unseen stimuli?Time series, state transitions, timescales and a repeat-measurement noise ceiling.
Causal responseDoes the same intervention produce the same change?Stimulation, suppression and lesion conditions excluded from fitting.
Individual specificityIs the original individual predicted better than other-individual or average models?Matched registration and computational budgets; separate individual and trial variation.
Learning and memoryIs information acquired by that individual retained?Untrained and differently trained individuals, retention, relearning and forgetting.
EmbodimentDoes correspondence survive a sensorimotor loop?Fixed body controllers; varied environments and delays.

Human comparison data and the limits of inference

When whole-brain acquisition after death is envisaged, physiological recordings cannot subsequently be repeated in the same person. The following is this site's proposed design. Prior recordings are not whole-brain ground truth: compare model outputs at the level of the observables actually measured. No universal required duration, repeat count or coverage is established. Use pilot repeated recordings to estimate reliability and individual variation, then plan individuals and sessions that can resolve the prespecified δ and ε.

TargetExamples of reference data to secureLimit and claim withheld
StructureSame-person brain images, independent checks on sampled acquired tissue, and registration errors.Agreement in coarse images does not certify every synapse or molecular state.
ActivityRepeated sensory/cognitive tasks with EEG/MEG or fMRI; the available scope of recordings made for clinical purposes.Map simulations to the same measured observables. Matching population signals does not establish matching activity in every cell.
Causal responseResponses to controlled inputs and stimulation/clinical-intervention records available under appropriate review and consent.These do not reproduce the animal suppression/lesion intervention set. Observational associations do not identify internal causal mechanisms.
Individual specificityMultiple repeat sessions and comparable data from multiple individuals.Compare at regional, cell-type or population levels. Do not infer unmeasured differences or general identification performance from one case.
Learning and memoryDocumented learning content/timing plus independent delayed recall, forgetting and relearning tests.Withhold test answers from fitting. Success on selected tasks does not establish recovery of all lifetime memories.
Body and environmentInput/output records from tasks varying feedback, delay and environment.Match input/output conditions. Limited simulated-body agreement does not reproduce the whole original body.

Keep acquisition conditions distinct. H01 used local surgically resected tissue; central aldehyde-stabilized cryopreservation (ASC) demonstrations used conditions including animal perfusion fixation. Neither directly establishes post-mortem human whole-brain applicability or retention of memory-relevant information. A human plan must record acquisition delay, terminal condition and fixation, and specify tested structural/molecular losses.[3][4]

Research review, consent and sample-provision arrangements are included here as prerequisites for executable data collection, without extending the guide into institutional ethics. Include their lead times in the 2026–2035 recording design. An unavailable comparison remains untested at the 2041–2050 gates; it is not silently waived.

Test correspondence to that individual

Evaluate a model derived from individual A on A's held-out trials. Compare its predictions with control models under matched conditions.

ModelComparison question
Model derived from AHow well does it predict A's responses?
Other-individual modelWhat does information from A add?
Cell-type-average wiring modelWhat do departures from average wiring add?
Activity-only modelWhat does structural information add?

Match regional and cell-type correspondence, training data and compute budgets. This separates individual information from differences in comparison conditions.

An own-model advantage alone does not establish memory reconstruction.

  • Keep answers to tests of previously learned content out of additional model training.
  • Estimate a noise ceiling from repeated trials.
  • First establish sufficient precision to measure the relevant individual differences.
  • Evaluate generalization to unseen individuals separately from specific reproduction of a known individual.
Designing a comparison across individuals

Pair structural and functional evaluation data within each individual. Assemble a score matrix:

  • Rows: the model's source individual.
  • Columns: the target individual being evaluated.
  • Entries: prediction scores for that target's held-out data.

Compare own-model scores with other-model scores. Fix cell correspondence and model selection independently of evaluation data.

Match conditions such as sex and developmental stage. Permute correspondence labels only within strata that are exchangeable under the null. Insufficient individuals or measurement precision makes the result inconclusive.

What the current sources establish

Schlegel et al. compared FlyWire and hemibrain across two individuals and three hemispheres. Many strong connections were conserved. Cell counts and connection weights also varied. Coverage, cell-type matching and reconstruction errors contributed to the differences. Three hemispheres are not three individuals. Structural differences do not directly define functional tolerances.[5]

The cited MICrONS and ZAPBench resources each concern one individual. They do not establish this multi-individual comparison design.[6][7]

Set individual counts from test resolution and statistical power. Report the current permutation count and additional individuals needed per stratum. See steps 6–8 of the validation procedure for calculations.[8]

This comparison is our proposal. The cited papers do not demonstrate the entire procedure.

Validation datasets and benchmarks

ZAPBench predicts preprocessed relative change in fluorescence (ΔF/F) traces from 71,721 putative neurons in a larval zebrafish. Activity volumes are recorded every 914 ms, about 1 Hz. Forecasts cover 32 steps, approximately 30 seconds, and are compared by mean absolute error (MAE). Evaluation follows alignment, segmentation and fluorescence normalization.[7]

Slow calcium-indicator responses smooth fast electrical activity. Unimaged ventral cells, segmentation splits/merges and fluorescence mixing remain limitations. Better ΔF/F prediction alone therefore does not establish recovery of voltage, individual spike times or unobserved cells. Direct ΔF/F predictors can be compared in that signal space; mechanistic models with internal states require an observation model H for comparison.[7]

The Brain Emulation Challenge uses artificial-circuit ground truth to examine observation and reconstruction choices. MICrONS pairs local biological structure and function. These resources are complementary foundations; none by itself certifies transfer of individual memories.[9][6]

A protocol for interpretable comparison

The following is our proposed validation plan, combining methods from the literature. The cited papers do not report completion of the entire procedure. Open each detail panel for conditions and calculations.

  1. Fix the target, controls and observation conditions.

    Specify what to predict and compare before inspecting outcomes. Calibrate the observation model independently of final evaluation data.

    Fields to preregister

    Record the following in an Open Science Framework (OSF) or similar preregistration:

    • Target: individual, region, function and timescale.
    • Evaluation: available observations, primary metrics and independent experimental units.
    • Hypotheses: claims and cell/individual correspondence.
    • Analysis: splits, preprocessing, exclusions and missing-data handling.
    • Decisions: δ and ε with their rationale, tests and the multiplicity family.
    • Scale: sample counts, stopping rules, model-seed counts and permutation draws.
    • Reproduction: data and code versions.

    For existing data, disclose results and analyses already examined.

    Control models

    • Simple models.
    • Models with shuffled connectivity.
    • Average models.
    • Activity-only models.

    Match training data and compute budgets across controls.

    Conditions for comparing observed signals
    • Measurement modality and model output.
    • Comparison signal and units.
    • Sampling interval and preprocessing.
    • Observation model H and its calibration data.

    Do not tune φ or preprocessing on final evaluation data. Pass mechanistic model states through H to compare observed signals. Test recovery of internal states separately.[7]

  2. Distinguish superiority from equivalence.

    δ is the minimum meaningful improvement. ε is the tolerated difference for equivalence. Set both in advance from task relevance and independent repeat measurements.

    Margins and confidence intervals

    Superiority: use the difference in a higher-is-better metric. Require its one-sided 95% confidence lower bound to exceed δ.

    Equivalence: use two one-sided tests (TOST). For a single primary endpoint, set α=.05 for each one-sided test. The corresponding 90% confidence interval for the difference must lie within (−ε,+ε).

    A nonsignificant conventional difference test does not establish equivalence. Apply step 7 when making multiple decisions.[10]

  3. Check the noise ceiling and measurement precision.

    Separate signal from trial variability using repeats under matched conditions. Report absolute error in the original units and mean bias alongside corrected scores.

    Metrics and inconclusive results
    MethodPurpose and conditions
    maximum attainable correlation coefficient (CCmax) and normalized correlation coefficient (CCnorm)Estimate a noise ceiling and normalized correlation from repeated trials.[11]
    squared correlation with the expected response (r²_ER) and estimate-centered credible interval (ECCI)Use repeated responses to independent stimulus conditions. These provide approximately unbiased noise-corrected estimation and an associated interval.[12]

    To estimate r²_ER, check the fixed-stimulus set and response-independence assumptions. Also check variance stabilization and signal-to-noise ratio (SNR).

    • Negative estimated signal variance makes performance inconclusive.
    • An interval covering all of [0,1] also makes performance inconclusive.
    • Do not apply the estimator unchanged to correlated adjacent time bins.
    • Separately validate methods that account for temporal dependence.

  4. Reserve evaluation data for the prediction target.

    Set aside unseen stimuli and interventions in advance. Fit preprocessing and select models within training data.

    Split units and learning-rule G
    Prediction targetUnit held out
    The same individual's futureContiguous time blocks
    A different sessionWhole sessions
    An unseen individualWhole individuals
    Unseen learning conditions for GReward changes, extinction, reversal and retention intervals

    Use gaps between splits to prevent nearby-time leakage. Set them from training-data autocorrelation or comparable evidence. Distinguish interpolation from extrapolation when splitting changes the condition distribution. A mixed model alone does not make the split independent.[13]

    Freeze G parameters before evaluation. Allow G to update weights during evaluation. Specify the supplied stimuli and rewards, and the behavioural or neural signals to be predicted.

  5. Separate individual, session and seed variation.

    Do not count neurons, trials or seeds as independent individuals. Choose an analysis suited to the metric and check interval coverage.

    A proposed hierarchical analysis

    One option in this proposal is a mixed model with:

    • Fixed effect: model condition.
    • Random effects: individual and sessions nested within individual.
    • Additional variation: trial residual correlation and variation across model seeds.

    Choose the distribution, link and interval method for the metric. Check coverage using pilot data or simulations. This particular hierarchical analysis is our proposal.

  6. Test an individual-identification matrix.

    Summarize own-model advantages over other-model scores across individuals. Test identification significance separately from whether the advantage exceeds δ.

    The statistic and exchangeable correspondence labels

    Form S_ij for model i predicting target j with a higher-is-better score. One statistic T averages the following difference across targets:

    S_jj − mean_{i≠j}(S_ij)

    Prespecify score direction, weights and missing-data handling.

    • Permute correspondence labels only among individuals exchangeable under the null.
    • Preserve dependencies among each individual's repeats, conditions and seeds.
    • Do not apply the test when sex, stage or measurement confounding invalidates exchangeability.
    Exhaustive p-values and individual-count limits

    Include the original correspondence among K admissible assignments.

    p = #{π : T_π ≥ T_obs} / K

    Count ties in the tail, so p≥1/K. Equality requires the observed assignment to be uniquely most extreme.

    Unstratified exhaustive testing: K=N!
    Independent individuals NAssignments KBest-case p-value
    361/6 ≈ .1667
    4241/24 ≈ .0417
    51201/120 ≈ .00833
    67201/720 ≈ .00139

    Four individuals can reach α=.05 for a single one-sided test.

    With n_s individuals in stratum s, within-stratum permutations give K=∏n_s!.

    Assignments when exchangeability holds within strata
    Individuals per stratumAssignments KBest-case p-value
    2 + 22! × 2! = 4.25
    3 + 33! × 3! = 36≈ .02778
    3 + 43! × 4! = 144≈ .00694

    Step 7 relates these limits to multiplicity correction.[8]

    Randomly sampling assignments

    Under a valid null-permutation design, randomly draw B assignments. Let b count statistics at least as extreme as the observed value.

    p = (b+1)/(B+1)

    Do not report b/B or zero. This formula can be conservative, depending on the sampling scheme. Prefer exhaustive testing when K is small. Increasing B does not add independent individuals.[8]

  7. Fix the multiplicity family in advance.

    Specify which superiority, equivalence and identification claims to test. This proposal uses Holm correction to control family-wise error at .05.

    Correction and an example with up to six claims
    • Prespecify primary functions, tasks and their family of tests.
    • For each TOST, correct the larger of its two one-sided p-values as one p-value.
    • An unadjusted 90% interval alone does not establish a multiplicity-adjusted pass.
    • Report exploratory tasks separately from prespecified decisions.

    This acquisition example prespecifies at most six confirmatory claims. Design individual counts to reach even the first, most stringent threshold.

    ClaimsFirst Holm thresholdRequired assignments KUnstratified individual-count floor
    1.05At least 204
    2.05/2 = .025At least 405
    6.05/6 ≈ .00833At least 1205

    This is a resolution bound for a nonrandomized exhaustive test. Later thresholds are less stringent, so the table concerns the first decision.

    Strata of 3+3 give K=36 and cannot reach the first threshold for six claims. Strata of 3+4 give K=144 and can arithmetically reach it. Ties or statistic symmetries can raise the actual minimum p-value.

    N≥5 does not guarantee power. Recalculate assignment requirements if the family grows.[8]

  8. Design individual and repeat counts.

    First check the lower bound on K. Then plan acquisition to meet power and interval-width requirements. More repeats alone do not resolve a shortage of independent individuals.

    Steps for planning acquisition
    • Calculate K for the proposed strata.
    • If insufficient, state the additional individuals needed in each stratum.
    • Estimate variability and missingness from independent pilot data or comparable evidence.
    • Assume several true-effect and noise scenarios for δ and ε.
    • Simulate the entire planned analysis.
    • Select individual, session and repeat counts; report sensitivity to assumptions.

    Variability to estimate

    • Signal, trial, session and individual variance.
    • Autocorrelation and missingness.

    Analyses to include in the simulation

    • Evaluation-data splitting.
    • TOST and superiority tests.
    • Permutation tests of individual identification.
    • Multiplicity correction.

    Example targets are at least 80% or at least 90% power. Prespecify the required interval width as well. Evaluate whether the design meets these targets under each assumed scenario.[10]

    SNR-based stimulus/repeat calculations can inform experiments with independent stimulus responses. Power to detect stimulus tuning is a different quantity. It cannot substitute for power for individual identification or equivalence.[12]

No universal numerical δ or ε has been established for whole-brain emulation (WBE). Without biological repeat measurements, report exploratory model comparisons. Do not declare individual emulation validated at that stage.

Information to retain with the results
  • Intervals and compute costs.
  • Unreproduced conditions and negative results.
  • Additional fitting and deviations from the plan.
  • Data and code versions.

Revising measurements based on prediction errors

Failure conditions help choose the next measurement. Compare adding information about transmission strength, receptors, adaptation or neuromodulation with removing corresponding components from an existing model. Test selected combinations so that conclusions do not merely reflect the order of additions.

Prioritise an added measurement when its benefit persists on independent conditions and justifies its cost. Otherwise revisit measurement quality, model form and the sensitivity of the evaluation. This is the iterative procedure proposed by this site.

Concrete additions and omissions: Compare a wiring-only model with versions adding receptor/peptide information and measurable glial states or modulation. Match training data, tuning attempts and compute budgets so that extra flexibility alone does not confer an advantage. Include shuffled molecular labels and removal of each added mechanism. Evaluate held-out stimuli, recall, interventions and timescales, reporting uncertainty and added costs. This is a proposed design motivated by the biological findings below, not a completed whole-brain experiment.[14][15]

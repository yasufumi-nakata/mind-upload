Current findings and four stages toward realization

There are already foundations to build on: fly-brain wiring maps, predictions of limited functions, and paired structure and activity in local mouse cortex. The next major step is to connect these within one individual and test previously acquired memory and learning.[1][2][3]

Where to begin: three research routes

Route 1

Reconstruct from a connectome

  1. Evidence and research foundations

    Sensorimotor models use neuron-specific wiring; visual models can share connections by cell type and position.[1][2][4]

  2. Next experiment

    Reproduce one result, specify fixed quantities and vary wiring at units the model represents.

  3. Tests enabled by success

    Narrow required precision for that task and estimation regime, then test generality and individual responses.

See the experiment and branches

Route 2

Identify necessary information from activity

  1. Evidence and research foundations

    MICrONS pairs structure and activity; ZAPBench benchmarks activity prediction; inference methods represent parameter uncertainty.[3][5][6]

  2. Next experiment

    Compare models that fit the same observations and select measurements that distinguish their unseen responses.

  3. Tests enabled by success

    Narrow candidate states needed for function and connect them to structural reconstruction.

See the experiment and branches

Route 3

Run models with a body and environment

  1. Evidence and research foundations

    Circuit simulators and limited models linking neurons, bodies and environments are available.[7][8][9]

  2. Next experiment

    Run with sensory feedback and measure how delay, state and learning affect behaviour.

  3. Tests enabled by success

    Establish conditions for stable operation and integrated tests of individual responses and memory.

Shared foundation for all three routes

Preserve and acquire the information models need

A shared acquisition foundation for all three routes.

  1. Evidence and research foundations

    electron microscopy (EM) connectomes, preserved ultrastructure and LICONN’s local optical reconstruction provide starting points.[1][3][10][11]

  2. Next experiment

    Measure errors, time and data volume from preservation through imaging, reconstruction and proofreading on small volumes.

  3. Tests enabled by success

    Accepted accuracy and cost can guide which information to preserve, which modality to use and which stages to automate.

Definition and goals of mind uploading

Mind uploading is the proposed implementation of brain-based information processing in another medium. One technical route is whole-brain emulation (WBE): measuring a particular brain and reproducing its individual responses and learned functions computationally. The central requirement is an experiment that tests correspondence to the original individual.[19][16]

The next question: what must be measured, and at what precision, to predict the original individual better than an average-brain model? This guide separates that question into measurement, modelling and validation.

Still unachieved: none of the research whose full text we reviewed demonstrates extraction of an individual's previously acquired memories followed by their reproduction and validation in a whole-brain model. This is a conclusion about the reviewed evidence, not proof of absence across the entire field.[18]

Key findings in measurement, modelling and validation

Measure · structure

From the fly brain to the central nervous system

FlyWire reconstructed 139,255 neurons in an adult female brain. BANC and MaleCNS, reported in 2026, are independent resources for circuits connecting the brain and ventral nerve cord.[1][12][13]

Measure · structure and activity

Pair connections with responses

MICrONS links live activity recordings and subsequent electron microscopy in a partial volume of mouse visual cortex. The number of recorded cells and the number matched to the structural reconstruction must be distinguished.[3]

Build · functional prediction

Turn wiring into testable predictions

Connectome-based models of fly sensorimotor circuits and the visual system have been compared with experimental measurements.[2][4]

Validate · comparison

Test methods under matched conditions

ZAPBench evaluates neural activity prediction; the Brain Emulation Challenge provides a framework for reconstructing artificial circuits with known ground truth.[5][17]

Carboncopies: from measurements to models

Carboncopies Foundation’s research programme has three main components.

  • The Brain Emulation Challenge for comparing reconstruction methods in synthetic circuits
  • The BrainGenix development platform
  • An updated whole-brain emulation roadmap

The Challenge uses synthetic circuits with known ground truth to test reconstruction from limited observations. This provides a controlled comparison of methods; biological validation is a separate step.[20][17]

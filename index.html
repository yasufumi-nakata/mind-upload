What are we trying to build?

Mind uploading is the proposed implementation of brain-based information processing in another medium. One technical route is whole-brain emulation (WBE): measuring a particular brain and reproducing its individual responses and learned functions computationally. The central requirement is an experiment that tests correspondence to the original individual.[1][2]

The next question: what must be measured, and at what precision, to predict the original individual better than an average-brain model? This guide separates that question into measurement, modelling and validation.

Still unachieved: none of the research whose full text we reviewed demonstrates extraction of an individual's previously acquired memories followed by their reproduction and validation in a whole-brain model. This is a conclusion about the reviewed evidence, not proof of absence across the entire field.[3]

Measure, build, validate: current advances

Measure · structure

From the fly brain to the central nervous system

FlyWire reconstructed 139,255 neurons in an adult female brain. BANC and MaleCNS, reported in 2026, are independent resources for circuits connecting the brain and ventral nerve cord.[4][5][6]

Measure · structure and activity

Pair connections with responses

MICrONS links live activity recordings and subsequent electron microscopy in a partial volume of mouse visual cortex. The number of recorded cells and the number matched to the structural reconstruction must be distinguished.[7]

Build · functional prediction

Turn wiring into testable predictions

Connectome-based models of fly sensorimotor circuits and the visual system have been compared with experimental measurements.[8][9]

Validate · comparison

Test methods under matched conditions

ZAPBench evaluates neural activity prediction; the Brain Emulation Challenge provides a framework for reconstructing artificial circuits with known ground truth.[10][11]

Carboncopies: from measurements to models

Current Carboncopies Foundation work centres on the Brain Emulation Challenge, the BrainGenix platform, and an updated WBE roadmap. The Challenge uses synthetic circuits with known ground truth to test reconstruction from limited observations. This provides a controlled comparison of methods; biological validation is a separate step.[12][11]

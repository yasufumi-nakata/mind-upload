What are we trying to build?

Whole-brain emulation (WBE) aims to reproduce a particular brain's information processing from measurements of its structure and state. In addition to constructing a large brain simulation, the research needs tests of correspondence to the original individual. The sufficient state variables and measurement precision remain open questions.[1][2]

Current position: large insect connectomes, paired structure–activity measurements in mammalian tissue, and connectome-constrained activity prediction are established achievements. The research reviewed here does not demonstrate human WBE retaining an individual's memories.[3][4][5][6]

Milestones from 2024–2026

2024 · Structure

Wiring an adult fly brain

FlyWire reported a connectome of 139,255 neurons in an adult female brain. The map does not itself reproduce the animal's learned function.[3]

2025 · Structure + function

Measuring correspondence

MICrONS paired calcium recordings from around 75,000 mouse visual-cortex neurons with an EM reconstruction containing over 200,000 cells and about half a billion synapses. It covers part of a brain.[4]

2025 · Measurement

Dense circuits with light microscopy

LICONN combined expansion and molecular labelling to reconstruct dense connectivity in mammalian brain tissue.[7]

2026 · Embodiment

Connecting brain and nerve cord

BANC and MaleCNS extend analysis to adult fly central nervous systems that include circuits linking the brain to bodily control.[8][9]

Carboncopies: from measurements to models

Current Carboncopies Foundation work centres on the Brain Emulation Challenge, the BrainGenix platform, and an updated WBE roadmap. The Challenge uses synthetic circuits with known ground truth to test reconstruction from limited observations. This provides a controlled comparison of methods; biological validation is a separate step.[10][11]

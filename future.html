Research routes: each next step opens a path forward

Build routes from results already available

Work can begin with structure, activity or execution. Each route below moves from a published foundation to this site’s proposed experiment and the next test that success would make possible.

Route 1

Reconstruct from wiring

Turn a brain map into a model that predicts.

  1. Available now

    Starting from fly connectomes, Shiu et al. tested sensorimotor predictions and Lappalainen et al. tested visual responses. Public data and models provide a foundation for the next experiment.[1][2][3]

  2. The next experiment

    First reproduce a result with the same data version and evaluation conditions. Then vary missing edges, false connections and weights, comparing cell-type averages and shuffled controls. For comparisons across individuals, match sex, coverage and cell correspondence, reserving unseen stimuli or interventions for evaluation.

  3. What success would open

    If an error tolerance reproduces in another model, it can guide what to acquire and at what precision. Adding activity from the same individual then enables tests of whether individual measurements outperform an average model.

Evidence boundaries and branches if the test fails

Conditions for using this foundation

Wiring alone does not uniquely determine transmission strength or current state. Prediction differences between connectomes alone do not establish reproduction of individual memories.[4][5]

Choose the next branch from the result

Use the failure pattern to choose between correcting wiring, cell correspondence or weight estimates. If structure does not explain the differences, use the activity route to test added functional, molecular or state measurements.

Start the comparison experiment

Route 2

Identify necessary information from activity

Test which measurements make a difference.

  1. Available now

    MICrONS pairs structure and activity in part of mouse visual cortex; ZAPBench compares activity prediction in larval zebrafish. Gonçalves et al. demonstrate inference of posterior distributions over neural-model parameters, rather than forcing a single parameter solution.[6][7][8]

  2. The next experiment

    In synthetic circuits with known truth, vary noise and recorded variables and check whether inferred ranges cover the truth. In biological data, compare predictions for unseen stimuli and interventions across models that fit the same recordings. Select measurements where predictions diverge, then test whether added data distinguish them.

  3. What success would open

    If improvement persists on held-out data, it supports adding that measurement to acquisition. This helps choose between finer structure and additional molecular, activity or plasticity information, informing a plan to test individual memory.

Evidence boundaries and branches if the test fails

Conditions for using this foundation

MICrONS covers a local volume, and connectomes are not inputs to the ZAPBench evaluation. Posteriors depend on model and prior assumptions. Predictive agreement and identification of biological mechanisms require separate judgments.[9][5]

Choose the next branch from the result

If candidates remain indistinguishable, vary recording times or inputs to seek discriminating observations. If added measurements do not improve prediction, revisit the model class or evaluation task. The result still helps reassess measurement priorities and remaining uncertainty.

Route 3

Run models with a body and environment

Make circuit predictions work in an environment.

  1. Available now

    NEURON and BMTK provide circuit-simulation infrastructure; BAAIWorm links neurons, body and environment for a limited neural and behavioural scope. Local neural-prosthesis studies offer a separate foothold for testing specific functions through recordings and interventions.[10][11][12][13][14]

  2. The next experiment

    First replay identical sensory inputs to compare circuit models. Then swap circuit models in a closed loop, matching the body model, controller, initial conditions, environment and task while allowing behaviour to change sensory feedback. Vary feedback delay, environment, model state and learning conditions one at a time; record predictive accuracy, execution cost and long-run stability, including after saving and resuming state.

  3. What success would open

    If circuit-derived improvements persist in the body–environment loop, they support integrating models obtained through measurement and identification. Compare retention, forgetting and new learning with data from the same individual, then extend the workflow to independent replication.

Evidence boundaries and branches if the test fails

Conditions for using this foundation

Performance supplied by a body controller or additional training must not be credited to circuit reproduction. Assisting a limited function with a neuroprosthesis does not establish gradual brain replacement or transfer of individual memories to another medium.[13][14][15]

Choose the next branch from the result

When behaviour breaks down, separate circuit prediction errors, body control, sensory feedback and computational delay. Return to state measurements or wiring reconstruction as indicated, then verify improvement on the same task.

Design the individual comparison

Converge on the same individual: connect information and test

These are this site’s proposed complementary research routes. Combine their requirements for structure, state and execution into a workflow that tests the original individual’s responses, memory and learning on held-out data.

Each experiment can inform the next measurement. A practical first step is to run one public model and compare one changed condition.

Use distance and timing to guide research

The routes converge on demonstrated components → individual information → sustained whole-brain function → a human workflow. Review the present position through six domains and 12 tasks, and the 2050 challenge through five review windows. Milestones M1–M4 below define experiments for judging early success and advancing.

Four milestones and decision rules

Break the long-term goal into testable milestones. The following is this site's proposed strategy, informed by the 2008 roadmap, a recent technical review and Carboncopies' update programme; decisions belong to individual experiments.[16][17][18]

Change direction when evidence changes

Working hypothesis: test whether structure and molecular information surviving fixation retain enough information about the long-term memories and individual differences of interest. Preserved anatomy and expert opinion do not establish sufficiency.[21][22]

  • If structure alone fails on intervention predictions, add functional or molecular observations and measure the benefit.
  • If individual wiring does not outperform an average model, inspect task sensitivity and model form. Report the possibility that average information is sufficient for the tested generic function.
  • If a method succeeds on artificial circuits but fails on biology, revise the synthetic-data assumptions.

Iterate between artificial circuits, small animals and local circuits, then test whether correspondence survives greater anatomical coverage. A sequence of species is not a guarantee or a timetable for human WBE.

Explore imaging and computation scale

Separate imaging volume from simulation cost. The calculator accepts anisotropic voxels. For example, 1 mm³ at 10×10×10 nm and one byte per voxel gives 1 PB of raw images. This is a geometric example, not an estimate of required resolution or actual stored data.

Resolution and maturity differ; these are not measured comparisons at identical performance.[23]

What faster computers do not solve

Compute is one requirement. Faster hardware cannot recover unmeasured information simply by running longer. Segmentation, registration, parameter identifiability, learning rules, and independent evaluation data remain linked challenges.[23][15][9]

Tracking demonstrated capabilities is more useful for research planning than assigning one arrival date. Extrapolation to humans must state differences in species, region, learning history, and observable timescales.

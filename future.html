The history page brings together six domains and 12 open tasks and five stages for testing the conditions required by 2050. This page explains the M1–M4 tests underlying the early stages and how to estimate computational scale.

Four milestones and decision rules

Break the long-term goal into testable milestones. The following is this site's proposed strategy, informed by the 2008 roadmap, a recent technical review and Carboncopies' update programme; decisions belong to individual experiments.[1][2][3]

MilestoneRequired comparison and outputDecision to advancePresent evidence
M1: audit reconstruction methodsCompare point estimates with approximate posteriors in artificial circuits with known truth. Vary observation type/count, missingness and noise; report reconstruction error, posterior-interval coverage, prior/seed sensitivity and posterior predictions under held-out stimuli and interventions.Meet prespecified criteria on unseen circuits and interventions, detect false certainty or model mismatch, and replicate in another implementation. Posterior concentration alone is insufficient.Artificial-model identification studies, approximate posterior inference for neural dynamics and the Challenge provide starting points. This combined evaluation remains our proposal.[4][5][6]
M2: bound required wiring accuracyPublic models, such as fly models; vary missing/false connections and measure prediction changes.Reproduce the original result and confirm an error range that preserves predictions within a prespecified tolerance in a second model.Connectome-based predictions exist; a shared error tolerance is not established.[7][8]
M3: establish individual correspondencePair structure, activity and interventions in multiple individuals; compare other-individual and average models in an identification matrix. For mammals, prespecify cell-type, regional or population correspondence independently of test data, without assuming one-to-one neuronal matches.Confirm an own-individual advantage exceeding δ on unseen interventions with intervals/tests accounting for measurement error and hierarchical variation. Too few individuals or unresolved confounding makes identification inconclusive.MICrONS supports structure–activity pairing; fly multi-connectome comparisons document structural variation. ZAPBench reports reconstruction in progress for the same animal used for activity recording. None establishes all of M3.[9][10][11]
M4: test previously learned informationDifferently trained and control individuals; independent recall after reconstruction.Prespecified correspondence in retention, forgetting and relearning without training the model on the test answers.Unachieved in this review's scope. Engram research helps identify candidate measurements.[12]

See the validation protocol for setting δ and tolerances. M4 alone would not certify completed WBE; it addresses claims about an individual's memory.

Change direction when evidence changes

Working hypothesis: test whether structure and molecular information surviving fixation retain enough information about the long-term memories and individual differences of interest. Preserved anatomy and expert opinion do not establish sufficiency.[13][14]

  • If structure alone fails on intervention predictions, add functional or molecular observations and measure the benefit.
  • If individual wiring does not outperform an average model, inspect task sensitivity and model form. Report the possibility that average information is sufficient for the tested generic function.
  • If a method succeeds on artificial circuits but fails on biology, revise the synthetic-data assumptions.

Iterate between artificial circuits, small animals and local circuits, then test whether correspondence survives greater anatomical coverage. A sequence of species is not a guarantee or a timetable for human WBE.

Explore imaging and computation scale

Separate imaging volume from simulation cost. The calculator accepts anisotropic voxels. For example, 1 mm³ at 10×10×10 nm and one byte per voxel gives 1 PB of raw images. This is a geometric example, not an estimate of required resolution or actual stored data.

Compression, indexing, communication, redundancy, preprocessing, proofreading, training and intracellular compartment calculations are excluded. Cost estimates require method- and sample-specific measurements.[15]

V × 10¹⁸ / (dₓ × dᵧ × dᶻ) × b bytes (raw image)
S × m bytes (synapse states only)
S × f updates/s (dense update count, not FLOPS)

Estimate imaging time from instruments and throughput

Rate q is effective throughput expressed as original, pre-expansion tissue volume. Do not deduct downtime again if it is already included. From Collins et al. Tables 1–2, presets use 0.3 mm³/month and 200 instruments for EM, and 802/4.5³ ≈ 8.8 mm³/month and 333 instruments for ExLSFM. Resolution and maturity differ; these are not measured comparisons at identical performance.[15]

Imaging years = V / (12 × q × n); Required aggregate rate = V / (12 × T).

Assumes independent parallel instruments and constant effective throughput. Recalculation from rates differs slightly from the rounded durations in Table 3. Excludes preparation, repeat imaging, transfer, reconstruction, correction, modelling and functional validation. This calculation cannot establish feasibility by 2050.

What faster computers do not solve

Compute is one requirement. Faster hardware cannot recover unmeasured information simply by running longer. Segmentation, registration, parameter identifiability, learning rules, and independent evaluation data remain linked challenges.[15][16][4]

Tracking demonstrated capabilities is more useful for research planning than assigning one arrival date. Extrapolation to humans must state differences in species, region, learning history, and observable timescales.

<div class="abstract-box">
<h2>First divide into 4 parts</h2>
<p>
The theory pages are all about "theory," but the difference lies in what the page decides. If you first divide it into <strong>Introduction</strong>, <Strong text organization</strong>, <strong>Design principles</strong>, and <strong>Dependency map</strong>, the reading order will be much more stable.
</p>
</div>

<div class="note-box">
<strong>When you want to decide the next page after getting started with WBE</strong>
<p>
If you would like to see not only the differences in the roles of the theory pages as a whole, but also where to proceed after WBE 101 in terms of clearing up misconceptions, design principles, long notes, and dependency maps, please take a look at <a href="wbe-to-theory-route.html">Four routes that dig deeper into theory from an introduction to WBE</a>.
</p>
</div>

<section class="section" id="common-mistakes">
<h2 class="section-title">Common ways to get lost</h2>
<div class="key-points">
<h4>Mistake</h4>
<ul>
<li><strong>Starting suddenly from Perspective:</strong> Since there is a large amount of information, it is safer to prepare your argument level in WBE 101 first. </li>
<li><strong>Read the Theoretical Frame section as a proven conclusion:</strong> This is a summary of the design principles, not a declaration that the proof is complete. </li>
<li><strong>Read Roadmap as theory text:</strong> This is a dependency map, with condensed theory background. </li>
<li><strong>Complete the theory judgment with just a short answer to the FAQ:</strong> The stronger the argument, the more you need to go back to Perspective and Verification. </li>
</ul>
</div>
</section>
